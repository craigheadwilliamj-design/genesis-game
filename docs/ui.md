# UI, map rendering, menus

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- Fonts (index.html): two pixel fonts from Google Fonts, set as `--font-head` (Silkscreen: titles, HUD, tabs, map labels) and `--font-body` (Pixelify Sans: everything else) on `:root`. Use the vars, never a font name; Silkscreen is wide, so long or packed labels (build tray items) use `--font-body`. `tools/fonttest` compares candidates.
- `js/menu.js`: the left tool column (`buildMenu`): small Bulldoze and Move buttons (`data-tool`; tapping the active one again goes back to Select, as does Esc), then four big category buttons (`BUILD_CATS`: Buildings, Landscaping, Exhibits, Paths). Each opens its tray along the bottom of the screen (`.tray` in `#sideBody`, `sideOpen(cat)` in map.js, open one in `sideCat`). The tray's bottom bar is menu A (`.trayA`): Buildings lists its groups as `GROUP_ICONS` buttons (each a `.sitem.sgroup`, its tools flattened by `flatRows` so Dining, Retail and Hotels spread out, and Logistics also holds the Supply lines toggle). The other trays list their group's `menuRow`s. A plain tool in menu A picks it at once. A row that holds a list (`.srow.head`) opens that list (`.ssub`) as menu B, a bar above (one at a time). Plant lists keep their biome filter and biome labels, and Water and Paint Ground list their types the same way (`it.water`/`it.paint` in `menuRow`: Own biome plus each `waterTypes()` water, or each biome plus Erase, as `data-water`/`data-paint` rows that set `waterPick`/`paintPick` and turn the tool on; `pressPicks` in map.js shows the picked one pressed, and tapping it again goes back to Select). Rock and Boulder show their shape picker as a bar above while that tool is on (`.mapwrap.tool-land-rock`). Item notes become button tooltips. Any tool with a picture in the sprite pack shows it as its button picture (`menuSprite`): vivariums their thumbnail (`sprites/menu/viv-<S|M|L>.png`, from `tools/pixelvivicon.py`: one height, wider for the bigger tanks), a `BLD_PICS` building or `PROP_PICS` path prop its front view, habitat props their shelter or tray picture, Rock and Boulder their first temperate shape, fences their pristine section (the Fence Types head the wooden one, Gates the wooden gate), paths a patch of their tile at its own pixel size, Water its temperate tile, plants their picture (a plant-list head takes the first medium plant of the period with one). The rest keep their drawn icons from `ICON_LINES`. The trays stay open while you build. Esc drops the tool first, then closes the tray. One delegated click handler on `#sideMenu` (map.js) runs it all, so `buildMenu()` can redraw it any time, and a mouse wheel scrolls a bar sideways. Also `BUILD_MENU`, `refreshMenu`; Statues dropdown, statue locks via `data-lock`). The Quaternary plants dropdown also holds the 27 named park plants (`PARK_PLANTS_OF`) next to the Quaternary biome plants; there is no separate Park Plants dropdown. See `plantRow`. Both group by biome (`BIOMES` order, a `snote` heading each) and order each biome small, medium, large (`bySize`), with the size on the row. Groups and dropdowns are generated from the data, so prices follow `BUILDINGS`, `BARRIERS` and `fenceRate`. Loads before map.js. Each tray (menu A) and each list above it (menu B) gets an × `.xclose` button from `buildMenu` (`data-close="a"` closes the tray, `"b"` the list).
- `js/parkmap.js`: the Park Map dialog (`#dlgParkMap`, right-column `#mapBtn`, key M; Esc, Close or a click outside closes it). `drawParkMap(canvas)` paints the whole park top-down in pixel blocks straight from `state` (owned land in the park biome color, unowned dimmed, water, exhibits in biome color with barrier-colored fence outlines, `fenceGrid` pieces, paths, buildings as `BUILDINGS[type].color` blocks with science ones pale, the south wall and entrance gap, guest and staff dots, and a white box for the main view's viewport). It never touches the main renderer or `tilt`. Drawn on open and every `PMAP.refreshMs`; a click on the map recenters the main camera and closes it. Loads just before main.js.
- `js/vivview.js`: the guest view of a vivarium, aquarium or paludarium (`#dlgVivView`, the exhibit panel's View button, `openVivView(e)`; Esc, Close or a click outside closes it). A fullscreen SVG in true meters looking straight through the front glass: the back pane is `VIV_VIEW.back` the size of the front (the eye `VIV_VIEW.eye` of the way up), with the scene's `-wall.png` on it, the floor, lid and side panes in perspective, and the water (`VIV_VIEW.tint` of the map's thickness, from `wqOf`) over the animals. `vvFrame` (requestAnimationFrame while open) draws each animal in the case back to front as a `<use>` of its map picture (it gives the live `.spr` the id `vvs-<animal id>`), so rigs, strips, turns and pitch come along; x and depth from `h.x`/`h.y`, height from `h.lift + h.bob` times `TILT` (the map's heights are stretched), kept under the waterline. The fittings (`vvProps`, each frame) are their `VIV_SPRITES` pictures upright on the floor at their depth with square pixels (flat ones, a dish or pool, squashed to `VIV_VIEW.flat`), sorted in with the animals by depth.
- `js/map.js`: SVG rendering, camera (the 3/4 view is the only main view: `tilt` is a constant `true`, there is no toggle or stored setting, and the old flat top-down `!tilt` branches are dead code left in place to remove piece by piece), build tools (including the Move tool: `moveTarget`, `moveBy`, `moveProblem`, `dropMove`; land items (rocks, plants, shelters, statues) move too, via `landMoveSpot` and `dropLand`, see landscape.md; and `fenceSel`, the fence the fence tool lays. The fence tool is `tool === "exhibit"` but not a draw: a left drag lays grid fence (`fdrag`, `fenceBar`, `fenceDragStat`, `endFenceDrag`, preview in `renderOverlay`), and in Bulldoze a drag or tap on a fence lifts pieces (`pieceNear`, `fhover`). The middle or right mouse button always pans), and drawing guests (`drawParties`). Everything placed or moved snaps to the 1 m grid (`GRID_STEP`, `snapAt`, `landPoint`); there's no free placement and no toggle. Placing a building (`placeGhost`): everything that isn't an `onPath` prop goes on the 1 m grid at the pointer, square to the map, and Rotate (`rotateTool`, `rotateMoved`, R) turns it a quarter turn; everything, path props included, faces north, south, east or west (`rot` stays in 45° units and moves 2 at a time). The turn also swings the door: a new building faces south (`face` 2), each quarter turn adds one (`ghost.face = (2 + rot / 2) % 4`), `placeBuilding` saves it as `b.face` and `rotateMoved` bumps it; the ghost and the Move selection show a door arrow out of that wall (`doorArrowSvg`). Its door wall then slides onto the nearest grid line (`doorOnGrid`, up to half a meter; also after `rotateMoved` turns it; a picture card facing north snaps its base line instead), so a building of any depth, like the 2.6 m food cart, stands flush against a path, whose edges are on whole meters. It never snaps to a path or takes a path's angle. It is refused over path chunks (`bldOnPath` in pathcells.js: `shapeOnChunks` plus old path lines on `pathSolid`, the footprint less the back `backOver` meters of a picture card like the food cart; `pathChunkProblem` uses the same shape), exhibits, buildings, water, plants, rocks and fences (`shapeHitsLandscape`); flush against a chunk edge is fine. Not being near a path is only a warning ("No path nearby, so it won't work until one reaches it"), using the same test as `recompute`: `nearCells(shape, REACH - PATH_HALF_WIDTH, guests)` (staff reach for `dept` buildings; `serviceOnly` ones also need a staff `"s"` chunk, via `nearCells`'s `kind` argument). The Move tool's `moveProblem` uses the same overlap rules; `rotateMoved` turns in place about the middle or refuses, with no sliding to fit. `onPath` props (bench, bin, lamp, camera, donation box, signs) also sit on the 1 m grid at the pointer, but only where every footprint corner is on a guest or bridge chunk (`shapeOnGuestPath` in pathcells.js, refused with "Keep it on a path."), so they can stand anywhere on a wide path. Their front (`propFront`) defaults to the nearest exhibit fence (`EDU.signReach` for signs, 8 m otherwise) or path edge, whichever is nearer (`propFacing`, due north, south, east or west, south if neither), and Rotate adds quarter turns to that (`rot`). A prop whose footprint overlaps a fence slides away from what it faces until clear. Overlap with other buildings, props and exhibits is refused as usual, and `moveProblem` applies the same on-path rule (the Move tool and `rotateMoved` share it). `shapesOverlap` also counts a shape sitting exactly on another. Placement checks `exhibitProblem`/`pathProblem`, `removeItem` (bulldoze), `findItem`, `insidePlot`. Also defines `$`, `esc` and `nodeKey`, which sim files use too. Its `moveProblem(kind, it)` is for the Move tool; moving animals between exhibits uses keepers.js's `keeperMoveProblem(e)`.
- Power (map.js, ui.js): the Buildings tray has a Power group (`BUILD_MENU` id `power`: the five stations, Transformer, and the Power Cable tool `cable`). The cable tool (`cableTap`, `cableHoverAt`; `cableFrom` is the node it starts at, and after each cable it carries on from the far end) joins two grid nodes, and a tap on a cable away from any node lifts it (`cableAt`). `gridSvg` (in `renderOverlay`, when `gridShown()`: the cable tool, placing a grid building, or one selected) draws every cable and each node's reach ring, tinted by network, red-edged where a network is short or dead; a power-building ghost shows its own reach. Solar arrays, wind turbines and transformers are drawn by `powerArt34` (`STAND34` fronts `solar`, `wind`, `xfmr`); the gas and geothermal plants reuse the lab block. Panels: `gridNodeHtml` for stations and transformers (output, condition, the network's supply, demand and load, what it leaves without power), and `powerRow` (Powered/No power and kW) on every other building panel, just above the theme picker.
- Bulldozing (map.js): a click removes the thing at once, on the map (`bulldozeTap`) and from a panel's `data-action="demolish"` button (`demolishRow`, path and fence panels) alike; both go through `bulldozeItem`. Only costly removals ask with `askConfirm` (ui.js): exhibits (`confirmExhibitRemoval`), gates (`confirmGateRemoval`), buildings `asksBulldoze` picks (`BUILDINGS[t].dept`, or `confirm:true` in data.js: the hotels, restaurant and Education Center), and a fence lift that opens a gap in a closed grid exhibit (`endFenceDrag` names the exhibits; a loose fence lifts at once). Hovering in Bulldoze outlines the target in red (`isDoomed`).

## Time Travel Lab timeline test page (tools/timeline)
The standalone prototype the game's Time Travel Lab panel was built from, NOT part of the game (its site block is still written by placesites.py, but its drones, reports and clock are its own): `tools/timeline/index.html` (opens from disk; the published copy is the same file with the `<!doctype>`/`<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper stripped, plus `maps/*.png`). Twelve equal period bars, each split into equal epoch slices; inside a slice the years run on a log scale (`yearsAt`/`uAt`), with the Holocene's floor at 10 years. Labels round to two significant figures under a million years, then to millions (`fmtYearsAgo`). Maps are 260 x 130 px pictures in `tools/timeline/maps`, one a period with epoch overrides where the world changes a lot (Early/Late Jurassic, Early/Late Cretaceous, Pleistocene/Holocene; `MAPS`, `mapOf`), in one palette: navy rim, deep ocean, shallow sea, land, mountains, desert, ice. `tools/timeline/genmaps.py` drafts them from the PALEOMAP PaleoDEMs (Scotese & Wright 2018, CC BY 4.0, credited on the page; it needs numpy, h5py and Pillow and the unzipped 1-degree netCDF set, see its docstring): shelf above -200 m is shallow sea, the top 8% of land is mountains, deserts grow inland in the 20 to 30 degree dry belts, and ice is set by hand-written rules per map (the Pleistocene also drops the sea 120 m). The Late Cretaceous is drawn at 75 Ma so the Western Interior Seaway is open, and land needs a majority of a pixel's samples so narrow seas stay open. The drafts want a tidy in the pixel editor. The sites live in `tools/timeline/sites.py` (83 real localities, or historical ranges for the recently extinct, with modern latitude and longitude, a window in Ma and species ids; every one of the 142 species is at one or more). `tools/timeline/placesites.py` rotates each back with the PALEOMAP plate model (pygplates, from the PaleoAtlas for GPlates on Zenodo, CC BY 4.0) to the age of each map its window touches, takes species names, sizes and stars and each period's trip cost, days and risk from js/data.js, and rewrites the `SITES` block of index.html (between `SITES:BEGIN` and `SITES:END`; never edit it by hand). It stops if a species is at no site or a site holds a species of another period. It also nudges sites sharing a map apart (`spread`) until no two 6 px marker frames touch (centres `SEP` 7 px apart on an axis), costing up to about 5 px of accuracy in the crowded Late Cretaceous and Paleogene, and prints how far each map's markers moved. A tap goes to the nearest marker within `HIT_PX`; the selected marker's frame turns white; sites are drawn over them in code from `SITES` (pixel spot per map name, `from`/`to` years, fauna, trip cost, days, risk) and show for the whole of any epoch their window touches. A site starts dim and unnamed; the first scouting trip lights it and reveals its name. Scouting can fail: each animal still unseen there turns up with its own chance (`scoutChance`, `SCOUT.find` by size, so big animals are easier to spot), at most `SCOUT.max` (2) a trip, and the rest show as Undiscovered species rows until later trips find them. Once every animal has turned up the site reads Site Complete (`complete`: a badge on the card and its chip, a line in the report) and the scouting button goes away. An expedition targets one of those animals, and each row shows its genome % and its own Survey Success (`success`, the game's `tripOdds` maths: size from `TRIP.fail` plus the site's risk). Each trip ends in a post-mission overview (`report`): a scouting report lists "<animal> Discovered" for every animal no scouted site had before ("also found here" if one had), and an expedition report says "<animal> Sample Found" (plus "<animal> Genome Complete" on the find that fills it) or "Came back empty handed", while the Genome column updates. The Time Travel Lab's drones (`DRONES`): every park starts with the Time Travel Lab Mk. I, and the Research Lab researches Mk. II to V in order, which cut the empty-handed chance of scouting and expeditions by 15% to 50% of itself. The Time Travel Lab Drones menu (`renderFleet`) lists the fleet, 1 to `MAX_DRONES` (5) drones of the park's current Mk., bought with money at the Time Travel Lab (they replace Temporal Researchers and aren't staff, so no wage or staff screen: the first comes with the Time Travel Lab, then `dronePrice` is `DRONE_PRICE.base` (15,000) times `growth` (1.5) for each one owned past the first; the test page doesn't charge): where each is (site and epoch), what it is doing (scouting, or looking for an animal), a progress bar and when it gets back (`eta`, park-clock hours under a day). Each drone flies one mission at a time (`missions`, timed in park days on a test clock with a speed picker; a game day is 240 s at 1x), so a send needs a free drone, and a site takes one scouting trip and one expedition per target at a time. A mission resolves when it lands (`land`) and its report waits in the menu, and on its site's card, until dismissed. An animal can't be targeted until the park rating (a test picker) reaches its `stars`; each find adds `findGain` (2 to 8 trips a genome by size, as in the game). Scouted sites are per viewer in localStorage, a test convenience only.

## Paleopedia
The right column's Paleopedia button (`#animalsBtn`) opens `#dlgAnimals` (forest-green header bar), which fills the screen bar a 12 px margin, with Close at the top right and a bigger picture box (`.pp-pic` grows with the window height). A row of forest-green tabs, one per period with launch-roster species and showing only the name (`ppPeriod`; clicking the open one closes it). An open tab shows just that period's species as text tiles: known ones (`ppKnown`: starter, discovered, or DNA collected) are buttons, the rest blank dashed slots, with "N of M discovered". Beside them is the card for the picked species (`ppCard`): sprite on a patch of its first biome's ground (`ppBg`/`ppGroundUrl`: the same 1 m tiles as the map, `sprites/ground/<biome>-<n>.png`; "No picture yet" when `SPRITES` has none), stats from `SPECIES`/`traitsOf`, `SPECIES_INFO` (data.js: `sci`, `ft`, `desc`), and an Acquire line from `speciesStatus`. Appeal stars are one per 5 points of `appeal` (1 to 25 in the data), 1 to 5 stars.
- Crates UI: crates are drawn in `render` (`crateSvg`, in the `standing` depth list with plants and rocks, `data-kind="crate"`), so they tap and select like anything else (`select("crate", id)`, `listFor("crate")`; bulldoze ignores them). A crate's panel (`crateHtml`) shows the animal and Move, Open in <exhibit> (only while it stands in one it fits) and Sell. Move starts the `uncrate` tool (`startUncrate`, `crateSel`, `crateHover`/`crateTap`/`crateGhostSvg` in map.js): the crate rides the pointer at its size (`crateArt`, never under 16 px), over an exhibit it fits the exhibit tints green and a tap opens it there (a fight warning from `conflict` shows in the bar); anywhere else the ghost is red unless the spot is free (`crateSpotProblem`), and a tap sets it down.

# Animal rigs and the sprite rigger

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- `js/rigs.js`: animals drawn from parts. `RIGS[id]` (one line a species, written by `tools/rigtogame.py` from a rigger project) is a list of parts in draw order (a cropped picture `img` with its box `ix,iy,iw,ih` around the joint, a parent index `p`, the joint's place `x,y` in the parent's frame, `f` flip) and the poses of a `walk` loop, an `idle` clip (`fps`; standing still the animal holds its first frame and plays the clip once: with chance `RIG_IDLE.chance` as it stops, and again after `RIG_IDLE.gap` seconds of standing, never while eating, drinking or hiding; a rig can have more idles, `idle2`, `idle3`..., and each time one is picked at random, `R.idles`/`g.idleC`, eased in over `RIG_BLEND` so a clip that starts off the standing pose doesn't jump) and a `rest` clip (`fps`; played once when the animal settles to rest, then its last frame is held until the act changes) A pose is r (degrees), dx, dy a part; frames in the data are sparse (`{partIndex: r or [r,dx,dy]}`, absent means rest) and `rigPrep` makes them flat arrays. `rigSample` tweens a loop at a point in its cycle (0 to 1), `rigMatrices` gives each part's world matrix (parent · translate · rotate), `rigSvg(sp, h)` is the picture (a `.rig` group scaled so the cell is as wide as a strip's, one `<image>` a part with its own `transform`, so the browser turns each part at screen size and details stay whole) and `rigStep(h, "walk"|"idle"|"rest")` is the motion (a one-shot, the idle clip or lying down, sets `g.lock` and `animateAnimals` leaves the animal alone until it finishes, whatever its AI now wants), called from `animateAnimals` (walk by `h.stride` over `r * step * walk frames`, idle by the clock, both from `h.rg`; changing mode eases over `RIG_BLEND` seconds). Opt in with `rig:1` in `SPRITES` (keep its strips, `walk` and `step` as the fallback); `setRigs(false)` (saved in localStorage as `genesis-rigs`) draws the strips; a vivarium draws the rig like the park (`rigSvg` is 4 dots wide there, not 2.5, and a `foot` in `SPRITES` sets its ground row, in the park too; `animateAnimals` runs `rigStep` stand/rest in place of the idle strip). `spriteSvg` and `animateAnimals` in map.js are the only hooks. Only the Plateosaurus, the Prionosuchus and the Dimetrodon (`tools/rigger/dimetrodon`: `dimetrodon-rig.json` is the hand-made project from the pixel editor, parts shaded and outlined there, laid out like the Lystrosaurus's (every limb a thigh or upper limb, leg and foot off the body; head, jaws, one tail piece). `gen-clips.py` (`python3 -I tools/rigger/dimetrodon/gen-clips.py dimetrodon-rig.json dimetrodon.json`, `SHEETS=<dir>` for contact sheets and GIFs; it borrows `lystkit.py`'s renderer and the same stiff-limb posing) writes `walk` (8-frame lateral-sequence sprawl), `stand` (breathing), `idle` (rears its head and gapes, then snaps shut), `dynamic idle` (sniffs the ground twice, looks up, snaps) and `rest` (belly down, legs stretched flat along it, a yawn, chin sinks). Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/dimetrodon/dimetrodon.json dime --walk walk --stand stand --idle idle "dynamic idle" --rest rest`, `rstep:.0682` (printed by the script), `rsize:2.4` (the drawing fills less of its 110 px cell than the old rig did); `sprites/dime.png` is stand frame 0 with `rigdraw.py`, `dime-walk.png` the older strip fallback) have a rig so far. The Coelophysis has one too (`tools/rigger/coelophysis`): `coelophysis-clips.json` is the game's project, animated by hand in the rigger (walk, a 6-frame `idle` breathing loop, `dynamic idle`, `dynamic idle 2`, `rest`) with parts shaded by `tools/pixelcoel_rig_shade.py` (shading and texture only, every pixel a step lighter or darker in its own color). Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/coelophysis/coelophysis-clips.json coel --walk walk --stand idle --idle "dynamic idle" "dynamic idle 2" --rest rest`; `sprites/coel.png` and `coel-walk.png` are its rest pose and walk strip from the rigger. A `stand` clip (`--stand`) loops while the animal stands; each time it stops or a loop ends, one of its idle clips plays once in its place with chance `RIG_IDLE.dyn` (`rdyn` in `SPRITES` overrides that chance for a species; rigs without `stand` keep the hold-and-play-now-and-then idles). The Compsognathus has one (`tools/rigger/compsognathus/compsognathus-rig.json`, hand-animated in the rigger: an `idle` breathing loop (`gen-idle.py` lays a bolder breath, neck/head sway and tail wave over it and writes `compsognathus-clips.json`, which is what gets exported) and three dynamic idles; no walk or rest clip yet, so it holds its pose while moving). Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/compsognathus/compsognathus-clips.json comp --stand idle --idle "Dynamic idle 1" "dynamic idle 2" "dynamic idle 3"`; `sprites/comp.png` is idle frame 0 composed from the parts. `gen-strips.py` renders `sprites/comp-idle.png` (the standing loop, then the three dynamic idles; the numbers it prints are the `idle`, `base` and `dyn` of its `SPRITES` entry), the strip fallback for when rigs are off. A rig that also has an idle strip needs map.js's `idleOnly` to skip rigs, or the strip would hijack it in an open exhibit. `coelophysis-rig.json` and `gen-clips.js` (an IK walk and lie-down on the older flat parts) are the earlier version; running `gen-clips.js` would overwrite the hand-made clips. The Carcharodontosaurus has one (`tools/rigger/carcharodontosaurus`: parts in `carcharodontosaurus-hand.json`; `gen-walk.py`, `gen-idle.py`, `gen-rest.py` write `carcharodontosaurus-anim.json`, which `rigtogame.py ... carc --walk walk --idle idle --rest rest` takes, `rstep:.047`, and `sprites/carc.png` is its idle frame 0. `rigkit.py` holds their 2-bone leg IK (knee bent forward, `knee=-1`), angle unwrapping so tweens never spin, and `Rig.lowest`, a ground check from the parts' own pixels: walk feet planted with a heel-off at push-off, idle sniffs then rears and roars on a staggered stance, rest crouches and folds the legs forward under the belly with the feet shuffling only as far as the ground needs). The Cotylorhynchus has one too (`tools/rigger/cotylorhynchus/cotylorhynchus-clips.json`, the hand-made project: 16 parts with a thigh, leg and foot a leg and an upper arm, lower arm and foot an arm, one head and a jaw; its hidden scratch layers were stripped before export). Its limbs are tiny bones under big pictures, so any knee bend pulls the pieces apart: `cotykit.py` poses a limb stiff, turned as one piece about its hip with the hip joint giving a little so the foot lands on the ground row (`stiff`). `gen-walk.py` (lateral-sequence walk, each limb lifted at the hip in the swing, a hind heel peeling up with the hip rising so the toes stay on the ground, `stiff(..., floor=)`), `gen-idle.py` (breathes, tips forward to sniff, chomps twice) and `gen-rest.py` (rocks back, sinks onto its belly (the drop measured from the body's own pixels) with the limbs sprawled flat, lays the tail down, lowers its head) each rewrite only their own clip; `idle2` (a jaw chomp) is hand-made. `SHEETS=<dir>` writes a contact sheet and GIF. Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/cotylorhynchus/cotylorhynchus-clips.json coty --walk walk --idle idle idle2 --rest rest`, `rstep:.0221`, `pace:.55` (a slow plodder). The Lystrosaurus has one (`tools/rigger/lystrosaurus`: `lystrosaurus-rig.json` is the hand-made project, 16 parts drawn and shaded in the pixel editor (outlines and texture in them) with every limb hanging off the body, and a tail. `gen-clips.py` (`python3 -I tools/rigger/lystrosaurus/gen-clips.py lystrosaurus-rig.json lystrosaurus.json`, `SHEETS=<dir>` for contact sheets and GIFs) writes every clip into `lystrosaurus.json`, which is what gets exported, leaving the parts alone: an 8-frame lateral-sequence `walk`, a 12-frame `stand` breathing loop, `idle` (lifts its head and chomps twice), `dynamic idle` (tips forward on bent front legs, grazes with the beak at the ground, looks up) and `rest` (folds its legs under, front feet back and hind feet forward, lays the tail and chin down, yawns and nods off). Its joints sit low in tall pictures (the elbow's pivot is at the wrist, the arm's and thigh's mid-picture), so IK bending flips the forearm or opens gaps at the ankle: `stiff()` swings each limb as one piece about the shoulder or hip, the joint giving up to about 3 px so the limb's lowest pixel lands on the ground row, and a lifted foot turns with its limb. The script prints any part under the ground row. `lystkit.py` holds the part ids and the preview renderer. Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/lystrosaurus/lystrosaurus.json lyst --walk walk --stand stand --idle idle "dynamic idle" --rest rest`, `rstep:.056` (the script prints it from its 6 px hind stride and `rsize`), `pace:.5`, `rsize:1.8` (the rig replaces `size:.7`, which is the old strips'); `sprites/lyst.png` is stand frame 0 drawn with `rigdraw.py`. `lyst-walk.png` and `lyst-idle.png` are older art, the fallback when rigs are off. The rigger can swap parts: parts marked `off` in the rigger ("hidden unless a frame shows it") get `o:1` in `RIGS`, and a frame that shows or hides one carries `v:{index:1|0}`; `rigPrep` turns these into `c.vis`, and `rigStep` shows or hides parts at the key frame (`g.vis`, never tweened). A part hidden this way is still posed, so a part hanging off it (the jaw) follows. A species whose strips' `step` doesn't suit the rig gives it its own `rstep` in `SPRITES` (the Prionosuchus: short legs, so `rstep:.04`), and a rig with a `swim` clip (`rigSwims(sp)`; `{fps, wl, frames}`, `wl` the cell row of the waterline, written by `rigtogame.py --swim <clip> --waterline <row>`) is drawn in the water: `rigSvg` clips the parts at `wl` (`.rig`), draws a faint ghost of what is under it (`.rig-ghost`) and a ripple line on it in the exhibit's water colors (`.rig-wake`, from `WATER_TEX`; dashes drift in `rigStep`), and in `rigStep` any walking in water becomes the swim clip (strokes by distance, `rswim` in `SPRITES` is its stride like `rstep`) and any standing a slow float; the waterline rises over `RIG_BLEND` as it sinks in (`g.wlk`, `rigWl`). `swimCheck` in map.js (every frame, not only while moving) flips `h.swim` and redraws it. A species with a `swim` strip but no swim clip keeps the strip in water (`spriteSvg` skips the rig when `h.swim`), going back to the rig on the bank. Its part pictures (`sprites/plat/*.png`) and strips are shaded by `tools/pixelplat_shade.py` (lifts the rig's near-black navy to a mid violet and keeps its tan and teal; a solid lit top-left edge, solid shadow under and right, staggered scale-row dashes, belly scutes, no scatter; each part in its own frame, run twice gives the same result). `tools/rigtogame.py` writes flat parts, so run the shade script after it.

The Platyhystrix's (`tools/rigger/platyhystrix`): `platyhystrix-rig.json` is the hand-made project from the pixel editor, parts shaded there, laid out like the Dimetrodon's but with one hind limb (the far front upper limb is the near one's picture flipped). `gen-clips.py` (`python3 -I tools/rigger/platyhystrix/gen-clips.py platyhystrix-rig.json platyhystrix-clips.json`, `SHEETS=<dir>` for contact sheets and GIFs; the Dimetrodon's stiff-limb posing on `lystkit.py`, drawn with `rigdraw.py` so the flip shows) writes `stand` (breathing), `idle` (lifts its head and gapes, snaps shut), `dynamic idle` (noses the ground twice on bent front legs, looks up, gulps) and `rest` (belly down, legs flat along it, a yawn, chin sinks); no walk, since vivarium animals don't walk. It also draws `sprites/plhy.png` (stand frame 0) and the `sprites/plhy-idle.png` strip (stand, idle, dynamic idle) and prints the strip's `SPRITES` numbers. Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/platyhystrix/platyhystrix-clips.json plhy --stand stand --idle idle "dynamic idle" --rest rest`. `tools/rigger/tiktaalik/gen-rig.py` cuts `tiktaalik.json`'s 20-frame idle into `stand`, `look around` and `push-up` and moves the parts to where the strips put it (`tiktaalik-clips.json`), printing the `rigtogame.py` command in its docstring; `gen-idles.js` still bakes its strips. `tools/rigger/rigdraw.py` draws a pose from a project in Python (for strips and checks), a flipped part mirrored about its joint like the rigger and the game.

## Sprite rigger (tools/rigger)
A standalone page for animating sprites from parts, with no build step: `tools/rigger/index.html`. It is NOT part of the game (not in `index.html`, not in the smoke test). Also published as an Artifact, https://claude.ai/artifact/Es5nLZcG73gpnDT71tbLgi (capability `downloads`); the artifact copy is the repo file with the `<!doctype>`/`<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper stripped (the repo copy keeps it, so it opens from disk), and it can't use `alert`/`confirm`/`prompt` or `<a download>` (it has `ask()` and the `downloads` capability instead). Republishing it is separate from publishing the game.
- **Model.** Each part is one PNG, a full cell in size with the part drawn where it belongs. A part ("node") has a joint `px,py` (pixel in the image it turns about), a `parent`, and `lx,ly`, its joint's place in the parent's frame (the world for a root). Pose per frame is `{nodeId:{r,dx,dy}}`: `r` degrees (positive turns clockwise on screen), `dx,dy` shift in the parent's frame. World matrix = parent world · translate(lx+dx, ly+dy) · rotate(r); the image is drawn at (-px,-py) (`flip` mirrors it about the joint). The empty pose is the rest pose, the standing picture. List order is draw order (last is in front) and is separate from parenting. `mirror` pairs two parts (near and far leg). A part's `off` (Rig mode: "Hidden unless a frame shows it") hides it in every frame unless the frame's pose has `v:1`; `v:0` hides any part for one frame (Pose mode: the Show box or V; `shownIn`, `showPart`); tweens keep the first frame's `v`. Its `hidden` (the eye in the Parts list) only hides it while editing, and `rigtogame.py` still exports it. Replace picture… (`swapPicture`) swaps a part's PNG and keeps its joint, parent, place and poses; other parts sharing the old picture (a duplicated leg) keep it. Update parts… (`updateParts`) does that for a whole parts file from the game's pixel editor (its Send to rigger, docs/art.md): a part with the same name (case and spaces ignored) gets the new picture, and a full-cell picture is re-anchored so the joint stays put (`px,py` = its rest-pose place, `flip` off, since the picture comes already as it shows); other names are added as new parts, parts the file doesn't name keep their pictures, and Undo puts it all back. The pixel editor imports a rigger project the other way, a part a layer, so parts can be redrawn there and sent back.
- **Project JSON** (Save/Load project): `{name, cell:{w,h}, ground, seq, assets:{id:dataURL}, nodes:[{id,name,asset,px,py,parent,lx,ly,flip,hidden,mirror}], ref, clips:[{name,fps,frames:[pose]}]}`. One rig, several clips (walk, idle...). Export strip gives `<name>-<clip>.png`, the frames side by side at `cell` size, the game's walk format. Export standing gives the rest pose. Export GIF (`makeGif`, own LZW encoder in the page: no library or worker, which an artifact can't load) writes the clip as an animated GIF at its fps (honoring ping-pong), 1× to 8× with no smoothing, the palette taken from the frames (index 0 is clear when Transparent is on, more colors than a GIF holds are merged to the closest), and delays spread so the average is exactly the fps. "Whole pixels" rounds each part's position to the pixel grid, rotation is nearest-neighbor (no smoothing).
- **Tools.** New project (top bar) clears every part and animation after a confirm; Undo brings the old project back (its pictures stay in memory, and the autosave and Save project leave out what nothing uses). Rig mode: Move, Joint (click the pivot; the picture and the children stay put), parent, Mirror partner, Duplicate (pairs the copy). Pose mode: drag the orange knob (or the part) to rotate about its joint, Shift snaps 5°, Move shifts, arrows nudge. The Parts list is the draw order (top row drawn last, in front): drag a row (a mouse from anywhere on it, a finger or pen from its handle) to reorder, with an accent line showing where it will land, or use the arrows; Undo puts the order back. Part picking: a click within a few screen pixels of a thin part counts, pressing on the selected part keeps working on it (a drag turns or moves that part, never a neighbor in front), while a plain click picks the topmost part under the pointer, and Alt+click cycles to the part beneath; the rotation knob wins only within about 7 screen pixels of itself. Timeline: copy frame, rest frame, reorder, Fill to next / first (tween), Mirror pose, Mirror clip, Half-cycle partner (copies the selected part's motion to its partner half a cycle later), onion skin, play with ping-pong.
- **Driving it headless** (how the Plateosaurus walk was made): load it in Playwright and call page globals: `openProject(json)`, `P`, `frames()`, `worlds(pose)`, `setParent(node, id)`, `setMirror`, `halfCycle`, `drawPose(ctx, pose, 1, snap)`, `stripCanvas(clip())`, `pruned()` (the project JSON), `save(true)`. `tools/rigger/prionosuchus/gen-clips.js` is the model for a short-legged animal (the hand-made rig is `prionosuchus-rig.json`; it first shades the flat parts with `tools/pixelprio_rig_shade.py` (the strips' rules from `tools/pixelprio_shade.py`, run per part; edges on joints skipped), then builds a lateral-sequence walk with no IK, an idle jaw gape and a lie-down rest with the chin lifted and the forelegs reaching forward, and a swim clip (body low, legs tucked, tail wave; run `rigtogame.py ... --swim swim --waterline 40`), and writes the project, contact sheets and GIFs). `tools/rigger/plateosaurus/gen-walk.js` is the model for a long-legged one: it sets the frames of a clip from code, with 2-bone IK on each hind leg (stance foot planted and sliding back at body speed, swing foot lifted, far leg half a cycle behind), body bob, and tail, neck and arm counter-motion, then writes the strip, a contact sheet and the project. Its node ids, ground height and gait numbers (`N`, `ds`, `S`, `af`, `lift`, `B`) are for the Plateosaurus, and its paths are for the session it was written in, so adapt them. Check a walk by looking at zoomed frames, not the numbers alone.
- **Workflow for an animal.** Cut it into flat base-color parts (no shading or texture), rig and animate, export the strip and the standing picture, then run the shading and detail pass on the strip (like the `tools/pixel*_shade.py` scripts). Shading baked into parts would rotate with them, turning the top-left light and breaking rows of dashes. Then add the `SPRITES` entry in data.js (`ratio:100/75, walk:N, step:`about `1/N`, and a standing `sprites/<id>.png` whose feet are on the same row as the walk frames) and copy the PNGs to `sprites/`. 4 to 8 frames suits a walk; the game only moves the viewport per frame, so the count costs nothing in speed.
- **Into the game.** `python3 tools/rigtogame.py PROJECT.json <id> --walk <clip> --idle <clip> [<clip> ...] --rest <clip> --rest-end <n>` writes `sprites/<id>/<part>.png` (each part cropped to its pixels) and the `RIGS.<id>` line in js/rigs.js; then add `rig:1` to the species' `SPRITES` entry (data.js) and publish `js/rigs.js`, the PNGs and the changed JS through `files`. The rig is drawn by turning each part's picture at screen size, so it is not baked to the 1x grid: details stay whole, but a turned part's pixels are tilted, and at the game's far zoom (under one screen pixel a sprite pixel) it reads the same as a baked frame. The walk clip must be one loop (the last frame leads back into the first); it is tweened, not stepped, so fewer keyframes are fine. The idle clip plays at its fps.
- **Known limits.** Rotated pixel art gets uneven edges, and big angles break texture and leave wedge gaps at joints (hide them with a small joint-cap part). Not built yet: IK in the tool (only in scripts), RotSprite-style pixel-clean rotation, replacing a part's image while keeping the rig, a darker tint for far-side limbs.

The Allosaurus, Dryosaurus and Compsognathus parts are textured by `tools/pixelretex4_rig.py` (`python3 -I tools/pixelretex4_rig.py [allo|dryo|comp]`; its `trex` entry is for the older flat T. rex parts): it reads each pixel back to a role from the colors a part holds, then repaints it (lit top-left edge, shade under and right, scale-dash rows, belly scutes, markings); far limbs and feet stay flat (the Dryosaurus and Allosaurus also run `deep` (the Allosaurus `deephead` and `deepthigh` too: lit columns, a shaded right side and a crease on the thigh, its top and knee ends left plain), shading by distance from the back and belly edges; the Allosaurus and Dryosaurus run `soft`: no edge shading on thin limbs, the jaw or the tail tip, since a lit edge on a 2 px part reads as a seam; the Dryosaurus's thighs are flat too; changing a species' palette means reshading from the pre-texture JSONs in git, as the script only reads back the palette it holds now), every pixel keeps its place, and a second run gives the same result. It rewrites the species' rig and clips JSONs in place; then export with the `rigtogame.py` line above, redraw `sprites/<id>.png` (idle/stand frame 0 with `rigdraw.py`) and the strips (`gen-strips.py`, `gen-strips.js`), and run `tools/packsprites.py`.

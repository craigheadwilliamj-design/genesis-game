# Animal rigs and the sprite rigger

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- `js/rigs.js`: animals drawn from parts. `RIGS[id]` (one line a species, written by `tools/rigtogame.py` from a rigger project) is a list of parts in draw order (a cropped picture `img` with its box `ix,iy,iw,ih` around the joint, a parent index `p`, the joint's place `x,y` in the parent's frame, `f` flip) and the poses of a `walk` loop, an `idle` clip (`fps`; standing still the animal holds its first frame and plays the clip once: with chance `RIG_IDLE.chance` as it stops, and again after `RIG_IDLE.gap` seconds of standing, never while eating, drinking or hiding; a rig can have more idles, `idle2`, `idle3`..., and each time one is picked at random, `R.idles`/`g.idleC`, eased in over `RIG_BLEND` so a clip that starts off the standing pose doesn't jump) and a `rest` clip (`fps`; played once when the animal settles to rest, then its last frame is held until the act changes) A pose is r (degrees), dx, dy a part; frames in the data are sparse (`{partIndex: r or [r,dx,dy]}`, absent means rest) and `rigPrep` makes them flat arrays. `rigSample` tweens a loop at a point in its cycle (0 to 1), `rigMatrices` gives each part's world matrix (parent · translate · rotate), `rigSvg(sp, h)` is the picture (a `.rig` group scaled so the cell is as wide as a strip's, one `<image>` a part with its own `transform`, so the browser turns each part at screen size and details stay whole) and `rigStep(h, "walk"|"idle"|"rest")` is the motion (a one-shot, the idle clip or lying down, sets `g.lock` and `animateAnimals` leaves the animal alone until it finishes, whatever its AI now wants), called from `animateAnimals` (walk by `h.stride` over `r * step * walk frames`, idle by the clock, both from `h.rg`; changing mode eases over `RIG_BLEND` seconds). Opt in with `rig:1` in `SPRITES` (keep its strips, `walk` and `step` as the fallback); a vivarium, or `setRigs(false)` (saved in localStorage as `genesis-rigs`), draws the strips. `spriteSvg` and `animateAnimals` in map.js are the only hooks. Only the Plateosaurus, the Prionosuchus and the Dimetrodon (`rstep:.031`, rig in `tools/rigger/dimetrodon`, walk rebuilt by its `gen-walk.js`, which also runs `tools/pixeldime_rig_shade.py` to texture the parts: that script marks the project `shaded` and refuses to run on it twice, so regenerate from the flat `dimetrodon-rig.json` each time) have a rig so far. The Coelophysis has one too (`tools/rigger/coelophysis`): `coelophysis-clips.json` is the game's project, animated by hand in the rigger (walk, a 6-frame `idle` breathing loop, `dynamic idle`, `dynamic idle 2`, `rest`) with parts shaded by `tools/pixelcoel_rig_shade.py` (shading and texture only, every pixel a step lighter or darker in its own color). Exported with `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/coelophysis/coelophysis-clips.json coel --walk walk --stand idle --idle "dynamic idle" "dynamic idle 2" --rest rest`; `sprites/coel.png` and `coel-walk.png` are its rest pose and walk strip from the rigger. A `stand` clip (`--stand`) loops while the animal stands; each time it stops or a loop ends, one of its idle clips plays once in its place with chance `RIG_IDLE.dyn` (rigs without `stand` keep the hold-and-play-now-and-then idles). `coelophysis-rig.json` and `gen-clips.js` (an IK walk and lie-down on the older flat parts) are the earlier version; running `gen-clips.js` would overwrite the hand-made clips. The Carcharodontosaurus has one (`tools/rigger/carcharodontosaurus`: parts in `carcharodontosaurus-hand.json`; `gen-walk.py`, `gen-idle.py`, `gen-rest.py` write `carcharodontosaurus-anim.json`, which `rigtogame.py ... carc --walk walk --idle idle --rest rest` takes, `rstep:.047`, and `sprites/carc.png` is its idle frame 0. `rigkit.py` holds their 2-bone leg IK (knee bent forward, `knee=-1`), angle unwrapping so tweens never spin, and `Rig.lowest`, a ground check from the parts' own pixels: walk feet planted with a heel-off at push-off, idle sniffs then rears and roars on a staggered stance, rest crouches and folds the legs forward under the belly with the feet shuffling only as far as the ground needs). The Cotylorhynchus has one too (`tools/rigger/cotylorhynchus`: parts by hand in `cotylorhynchus-rig.json`, clips from `gen-walk.py` (a lateral-sequence walk with big lizard steps, feet held planted through the body's bob and rock), `gen-idle.py` (leans down, chomps, chews, blinks) and `gen-rest.py` (lies down, nods off, sleeps); each script rewrites only its own clip; `rigtogame.py ... coty --walk walk --idle idle --rest rest`, `rstep:.032`). The Lystrosaurus has one (`tools/rigger/lystrosaurus`: `lystrosaurus-rig.json` is the hand-made flat project, parts, `idle` jaw chomp and `dynamic idle` grazing; `gen-walk.py` (lateral-sequence walk, 2-bone IK per leg through `lystkit.py`, which also draws contact sheets and GIFs with `SHEETS=<dir>`) and `gen-rest.py` (lies down, looks round, yawns, nods off) write `lystrosaurus-clips.json`, `tools/pixellyst_rig_shade.py` textures its parts into `lystrosaurus.json`, and `rigtogame.py tools/rigger/lystrosaurus/lystrosaurus.json lyst --walk walk --idle idle "dynamic idle" --rest rest`, `rstep:.09` (a hair over the stride's own 25 px a cycle, so a planted foot skates a little; the walk rides low with the feet planted wide so the legs sprawl like a lizard's). Its thighs are root parts, so the scripts shift each hip with the body's bob and rock (`View.hip`). The rig's art is smaller in its cell than the old strips, so `rsize:1.1` in `SPRITES` replaces `size:.7` for the rig only; the strips are the vivarium's). It swaps its head for the eye: parts marked `off` in the rigger ("hidden unless a frame shows it") get `o:1` in `RIGS`, and a frame that shows or hides one carries `v:{index:1|0}`; `rigPrep` turns these into `c.vis`, and `rigStep` shows or hides parts at the key frame (`g.vis`, never tweened). A part hidden this way is still posed, so a part hanging off it (the jaw) follows. A species whose strips' `step` doesn't suit the rig gives it its own `rstep` in `SPRITES` (the Prionosuchus: short legs, so `rstep:.04`), and a rig with a `swim` clip (`rigSwims(sp)`; `{fps, wl, frames}`, `wl` the cell row of the waterline, written by `rigtogame.py --swim <clip> --waterline <row>`) is drawn in the water: `rigSvg` clips the parts at `wl` (`.rig`), draws a faint ghost of what is under it (`.rig-ghost`) and a ripple line on it in the exhibit's water colors (`.rig-wake`, from `WATER_TEX`; dashes drift in `rigStep`), and in `rigStep` any walking in water becomes the swim clip (strokes by distance, `rswim` in `SPRITES` is its stride like `rstep`) and any standing a slow float; the waterline rises over `RIG_BLEND` as it sinks in (`g.wlk`, `rigWl`). `swimCheck` in map.js (every frame, not only while moving) flips `h.swim` and redraws it. A species with a `swim` strip but no swim clip keeps the strip in water (`spriteSvg` skips the rig when `h.swim`), going back to the rig on the bank. Its part pictures (`sprites/plat/*.png`) and strips are shaded by `tools/pixelplat_shade.py` (keeps its navy, tan and teal; a lit top-left edge, shadow under and right, scale-row dashes, belly scutes; each part in its own frame, run twice gives the same result). `tools/rigtogame.py` writes flat parts, so run the shade script after it.

The Platyhystrix is a vivarium animal, so it has no `RIGS` entry: `tools/rigger/platyhystrix/gen-strips.js` renders the hand-made `platyhystrix-rig.json` clips (`idle`, `dynamic idle`) into `sprites/plhy.png` and `plhy-idle.png` (base loop of 7 frames, then two `dyn` clips: the jaw gape and the leg shuffle).

## Sprite rigger (tools/rigger)
A standalone page for animating sprites from parts, with no build step: `tools/rigger/index.html`. It is NOT part of the game (not in `index.html`, not in the smoke test). Also published as an Artifact, https://claude.ai/artifact/Es5nLZcG73gpnDT71tbLgi (capability `downloads`); the artifact copy is the repo file with the `<!doctype>`/`<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper stripped (the repo copy keeps it, so it opens from disk), and it can't use `alert`/`confirm`/`prompt` or `<a download>` (it has `ask()` and the `downloads` capability instead). Republishing it is separate from publishing the game.
- **Model.** Each part is one PNG, a full cell in size with the part drawn where it belongs. A part ("node") has a joint `px,py` (pixel in the image it turns about), a `parent`, and `lx,ly`, its joint's place in the parent's frame (the world for a root). Pose per frame is `{nodeId:{r,dx,dy}}`: `r` degrees (positive turns clockwise on screen), `dx,dy` shift in the parent's frame. World matrix = parent world · translate(lx+dx, ly+dy) · rotate(r); the image is drawn at (-px,-py) (`flip` mirrors it about the joint). The empty pose is the rest pose, the standing picture. List order is draw order (last is in front) and is separate from parenting. `mirror` pairs two parts (near and far leg). A part's `off` (Rig mode: "Hidden unless a frame shows it") hides it in every frame unless the frame's pose has `v:1`; `v:0` hides any part for one frame (Pose mode: the Show box or V; `shownIn`, `showPart`); tweens keep the first frame's `v`. Its `hidden` (the eye in the Parts list) only hides it while editing, and `rigtogame.py` still exports it. Replace picture… (`swapPicture`) swaps a part's PNG and keeps its joint, parent, place and poses; other parts sharing the old picture (a duplicated leg) keep it.
- **Project JSON** (Save/Load project): `{name, cell:{w,h}, ground, seq, assets:{id:dataURL}, nodes:[{id,name,asset,px,py,parent,lx,ly,flip,hidden,mirror}], ref, clips:[{name,fps,frames:[pose]}]}`. One rig, several clips (walk, idle...). Export strip gives `<name>-<clip>.png`, the frames side by side at `cell` size, the game's walk format. Export standing gives the rest pose. Export GIF (`makeGif`, own LZW encoder in the page: no library or worker, which an artifact can't load) writes the clip as an animated GIF at its fps (honoring ping-pong), 1× to 8× with no smoothing, the palette taken from the frames (index 0 is clear when Transparent is on, more colors than a GIF holds are merged to the closest), and delays spread so the average is exactly the fps. "Whole pixels" rounds each part's position to the pixel grid, rotation is nearest-neighbor (no smoothing).
- **Tools.** New project (top bar) clears every part and animation after a confirm; Undo brings the old project back (its pictures stay in memory, and the autosave and Save project leave out what nothing uses). Rig mode: Move, Joint (click the pivot; the picture and the children stay put), parent, Mirror partner, Duplicate (pairs the copy). Pose mode: drag the orange knob (or the part) to rotate about its joint, Shift snaps 5°, Move shifts, arrows nudge. The Parts list is the draw order (top row drawn last, in front): drag a row (a mouse from anywhere on it, a finger or pen from its handle) to reorder, with an accent line showing where it will land, or use the arrows; Undo puts the order back. Part picking: a click within a few screen pixels of a thin part counts, pressing on the selected part keeps working on it (a drag turns or moves that part, never a neighbor in front), while a plain click picks the topmost part under the pointer, and Alt+click cycles to the part beneath; the rotation knob wins only within about 7 screen pixels of itself. Timeline: copy frame, rest frame, reorder, Fill to next / first (tween), Mirror pose, Mirror clip, Half-cycle partner (copies the selected part's motion to its partner half a cycle later), onion skin, play with ping-pong.
- **Driving it headless** (how the Plateosaurus walk was made): load it in Playwright and call page globals: `openProject(json)`, `P`, `frames()`, `worlds(pose)`, `setParent(node, id)`, `setMirror`, `halfCycle`, `drawPose(ctx, pose, 1, snap)`, `stripCanvas(clip())`, `pruned()` (the project JSON), `save(true)`. `tools/rigger/prionosuchus/gen-clips.js` is the model for a short-legged animal (the hand-made rig is `prionosuchus-rig.json`; it first shades the flat parts with `tools/pixelprio_rig_shade.py` (the strips' rules from `tools/pixelprio_shade.py`, run per part; edges on joints skipped), then builds a lateral-sequence walk with no IK, an idle jaw gape and a lie-down rest with the chin lifted and the forelegs reaching forward, and a swim clip (body low, legs tucked, tail wave; run `rigtogame.py ... --swim swim --waterline 40`), and writes the project, contact sheets and GIFs). `tools/rigger/plateosaurus/gen-walk.js` is the model for a long-legged one: it sets the frames of a clip from code, with 2-bone IK on each hind leg (stance foot planted and sliding back at body speed, swing foot lifted, far leg half a cycle behind), body bob, and tail, neck and arm counter-motion, then writes the strip, a contact sheet and the project. Its node ids, ground height and gait numbers (`N`, `ds`, `S`, `af`, `lift`, `B`) are for the Plateosaurus, and its paths are for the session it was written in, so adapt them. Check a walk by looking at zoomed frames, not the numbers alone.
- **Workflow for an animal.** Cut it into flat base-color parts (no shading or texture), rig and animate, export the strip and the standing picture, then run the shading and detail pass on the strip (like the `tools/pixel*_shade.py` scripts). Shading baked into parts would rotate with them, turning the top-left light and breaking rows of dashes. Then add the `SPRITES` entry in data.js (`ratio:100/75, walk:N, step:`about `1/N`, and a standing `sprites/<id>.png` whose feet are on the same row as the walk frames) and copy the PNGs to `sprites/`. 4 to 8 frames suits a walk; the game only moves the viewport per frame, so the count costs nothing in speed.
- **Into the game.** `python3 tools/rigtogame.py PROJECT.json <id> --walk <clip> --idle <clip> [<clip> ...] --rest <clip> --rest-end <n>` writes `sprites/<id>/<part>.png` (each part cropped to its pixels) and the `RIGS.<id>` line in js/rigs.js; then add `rig:1` to the species' `SPRITES` entry (data.js) and publish `js/rigs.js`, the PNGs and the changed JS through `files`. The rig is drawn by turning each part's picture at screen size, so it is not baked to the 1x grid: details stay whole, but a turned part's pixels are tilted, and at the game's far zoom (under one screen pixel a sprite pixel) it reads the same as a baked frame. The walk clip must be one loop (the last frame leads back into the first); it is tweened, not stepped, so fewer keyframes are fine. The idle clip plays at its fps.
- **Known limits.** Rotated pixel art gets uneven edges, and big angles break texture and leave wedge gaps at joints (hide them with a small joint-cap part). Not built yet: IK in the tool (only in scripts), RotSprite-style pixel-clean rotation, replacing a part's image while keeping the rig, a darker tint for far-side limbs.

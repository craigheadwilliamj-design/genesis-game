# Landscape, biomes, themes, ground paint

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- **Biome names:** the keys `temperate` and `boreal` (sprites, patterns, plant tables, saves) are shown to players as "Seasonal Forest" and "Coniferous Forest" (`BIOMES[k].label`). Don't rename the keys.
- **Animal biomes:** each species has two biomes in `SPECIES_BIOMES` (data.js), equally at home in either, and the order means nothing. `biomeFit` is "home" (either biome, `BIOME_HAPPY.home`) or "away" (`BIOME_HAPPY.away`). The one place both matter is `waterWant`: an animal is a dry-ground one only when both its biomes are scrubland or desert.
- **Fern Prairie** (`fern`): the open ground of the eras before grass, with plants Permian through Paleogene only (grassland is Neogene on). Its art is still scrubland's copied under the `fern` names (see tools/BIOME_ART.md), and the Permian plant names are still placeholders (Triassic through Paleogene have real names).

- `js/landscape.js`: water, rocks, groves and shelters inside open exhibits. Water is laid as chunk boxes (`e.water`, `WATER`, `waterProblem`/`waterHost`/`addWater`; the `water` tool in map.js drags a box in whole 5 m chunks like paths: `wdrag`, `waterBox`, `endWaterDrag`; each box is a 4-corner rectangle, `rectOfPts`/`waterHit` treat edge-touching rectangles as not overlapping, and `mergeWater` joins a new one to a neighbour of the same width or height; water is not reshaped or moved by hand: `reshapeables` (the Move tool's corners) lists only exhibit fences, and Move shifts an exhibit that holds water in whole 5 m steps (`moveStep`), so water stays on the chunk grid. In an exhibit a chunk is cut to the fence with `waterClip(host, box)` (half-meter cells wholly inside the fence with `WATER.margin` = 0.5 m to spare, joined into rectangles: a straight wall gives one box, a slanted one steps; pieces under `WATER.minArea` are dropped; no host, the park, keeps the box); the `water` tool lays each part, and the ghost shows them. `insideFence` refuses a shore closer than the margin, and `reshapeProblem` uses it, so a fence can't be pulled onto water. Exhibit water is drawn in map.js right after the floor and before the fence (`landSvg` no longer draws it), so posts and rails stand over the bank. `state.waterGrid5` marks saves already through `snapWaterToGrid` (called from `upgradeSave`): older water (corner-drawn, or moved in 1 m steps) becomes whole chunks, each kept when the body covers at least half of what its host leaves of the chunk, then cut by `waterClip`, with no money changing hands; water has no bulldoze pick, `watersSvg` draws it with no hit shape and `bulldozeTap` ignores it: ground paint over it takes it away, see the paint paragraph. `cutWater(host, rect)` cuts a box out of one host's water (`null` is the park's `state.water`) and returns `{m2, left}`, with the same piece maths `layWater` uses to make room for a new box; `cutWaterAll(rect)` cuts every host, joins the leftovers with `mergeWater` and pays `waterCutRefund(m2)` (build price at `COST.refundShare`); `waterCutM2(rect)` is the dry run for the cost line), and old saves' round ponds become drawn water in `upgradeSave`. The rest are placed with a tap (`e.land`, `LAND`, `HAB`, `likesOf` in data.js). `habitatOf`/`habitatScore` feed `exhibitReport`, and each species wants its own water share (`waterWant` in data.js: 25% semi-aquatic fish eaters, 5% for a scrubland or desert home biome, 8% for the rest), rocks and plants (`wantsOf` vs `haveOf`, `speciesFit`; `likesOf`, `BIOMES[x].rock`/`plants`, `HAB.waterMax`; too much water counts against it too), `thirsty` feeds `illChance`. Groves (`LAND[x].flora`) from an animal's era add happiness, `browseShare` cuts what `eatTick` drains, and `grassShare(e, s)` (0 to 1: Cenozoic plants as a share of the exhibit's plants, a grassland biome, or grass hay in place of Paleoflora) is how much of an old grazer's food is grass it never evolved for: it feeds `grassSick` (illness, `GRASS_INTOLERANT`) and the happiness hit in `exhibitReport`. Landscape plants are generated from `PLANT_TABLE` in data.js: one small, medium and large per period and biome (keys like `jur-tropical-small`, `PLANTS_OF[period][biome]`), so a biome that didn't exist in a period has none (no grassland before the Neogene). They carry `period` and `biome` and `landProblem` only lets them go in an exhibit of that biome. An animal counts plants from its own period in full toward what it wants, and other periods' plants of the exhibit's biome up to `HAB.offEra` (a third) of what it wants (`haveOf(e, s)`, `plantSuits`), and wants none where its period had no plants in the exhibit's biome (`wantsOf`). The build menu has a dropdown per period and the exhibit panel lists the biome's plants by period. Each Mesozoic or Paleozoic plant uses one plant of its period and size (`LAND[x].size`) from `state.ceres.pots`, which the Botany Lab grows in batches (`CERES_GROW.plant`). Biomes (`BIOMES`, `SPECIES_BIOMES`/`biomesOf` in data.js): each open exhibit has `e.biome` (`biomeOf` falls back to `DEFAULT_BIOME`), and each open-exhibit species has a home biome and one it gets by in. `biomeFit` feeds `habitatScore` (`BIOME_HAPPY`), wetland adds to `wet` in `habitatOf`. map.js fills open exhibits with the biome color and its `#b-<biome>` pattern from index.html (vivariums keep the period tint; animal dots stay period colors). Weather comes from the park's climate: `CLIMATE[state.biome]` has, per month, the temperature range `t` (°F, coldest night to hottest afternoon), the night `drop`, and odds for `cloud`, `precip`, `storm` and `wind`; `WX` holds the day-to-day rules. `state.weather` is {today, next, from, t, tm, fc}: `t` is today's weather {hi, lo, sky, ev}, `tm` tomorrow's, `fc` the forecast of tomorrow (right about the sky or event `WX.accuracy` of the time, temperatures always a little off; `forecastOf`). `wxDays()` makes them on first use (older saves too), `rollWxDay` rolls a day from the one before (the high drifts back toward the month's middle by `WX.pull` and wanders by `WX.swing`; a storm or rain on a freezing day is snow, no rain over `WX.rainMax`, a windy day in the desert is a sandstorm; events before `from` are off), and `rollWeather` in `endDay` (after the night functions so today's weather counts for tonight's illness) moves tomorrow to today and rolls the day after. The day has three temperatures by `wxPhase` (`WX.phases` on the clock): the high by day, the low at night, halfway at dawn and dusk; `wxNow()` is the weather right now, where rain at or under `WX.freeze` falls as snow. `today`/`next` are `WEATHER.kinds` keys from `wxKindOf`: storm and sandstorm days are those kinds, every other day is fair; heat and cold are per animal (below). The map overlay (`drawWeather`, `WX_FX` in map.js) follows `wxNow()`: the event, else a heat wave (`WX.heatWave`) or cold snap, else the sky. Temperature tolerance: `TOLERANCE` (data.js) gives each biome a comfortable range and how many degrees each step of too hot (`heat`) or too cold (`cold`) is; `toleranceOf(s)` takes the colder low and hotter high of a species' two biomes, each with its own step, and `tempStress(s, t)` gives {side, level 0 to 3} at temperature t (the one the top bar shows now by default). `coverWhy(s, t)` is why an animal wants cover: a storm or sandstorm day for anything out of the water, else being too hot or too cold, weighted by `TEMP_HARM.weights[level]`. `coverOf(e, t)` hands shelters out per animal (biggest first; canopies, `noCold`, are no help in the cold), then grove shade covers the rest, fully from heat and partly from storms (`WEATHER.kinds[why].grove`). `exposures(e, t)` gives, per reason, the share of the herd left out (`share`), that weighted by level (`x`) and the worst level; `exhibitReport` takes `WEATHER.kinds[why].happy * x` off happiness for each ("Too hot (98°F). 40% of the herd has no shade."), `illChance` takes the worse of the day's high and low, `injuryChance` (storms) and the behavior use `exposure(e)`, the plain share out right now. The behavior side: an animal too hot or cold goes in to shelter like in a storm (more of them the worse it is), naps more and gets thirstier on a day too hot for it (`hotDayFor`); frozen treats melt on a heat-wave day. `wearPerDay` uses the day's kind, and `weatherNow().guests` scales demand. `landSpot`/`placeLand`/`landSvg` do placement and drawing. The tools (`land-rock`, `land-cycads`, ...) are handled in map.js (`landHover`/`landTap`), and water and land features move, reshape-check and bulldoze with their exhibit (water on its own, no). Vivarium scenes: a vivarium takes no plants; it has a scene, `e.vivScene = {era, biome}` (null for none; an optional `period` picks a period scene once one is listed in `VIV_SCENE_PERIODS`), set in the panel (`vivSceneHtml` in ui.js: an Era select and a Biome select over all of `BIOMES`, action `vivScene`; the scene's biome is also the case's biome for `biomeOf`) by `setVivScene`/`vivSceneProblem` for the flat `VIV_SCENE.price` (clearing it refunds `COST.refundShare`). `sceneFit(e, s)` is half for the right era (or period) and half for one of the species' biomes; `haveOf` gives a vivarium `sceneFit` times what the species wants in plants, so `speciesFit`, the `VIV_PLANT.bonus` in `habitatScore` and the sim.js own-era check run off the scene. Old saves turn a case's plants into the scene of its `e.biome` and its animals' era (`upgradeSave`, `s.vivScene`). The pictures are drawn by `vivScene34` in map.js (art.md). Vivarium fittings (vivarium animals only; `LAND` items with `vivToy`, `addVivToy`/`vivToyProblem`, panel section `vivToysHtml` in ui.js: Fittings and Enrichment for everyone): the food dish (see keepers.js), a Basking Lamp (`lamp`, `VIV_LAMP`; `wantsHeat(s)` is every pre-Cenozoic species not in `WARM_BLOODED`; one lamp warms `per` animals, `heatShare(e)`: happiness up and down in `habitatScore`, comfort in `comfortOf`, illness down and a cold snap hurts the unlit in `illChance`, upkeep via `LAND.upkeep` in `dailyCosts`) and a Pool (`pool`, `poolM2` of water, `VIV_POOL`: counts as water in `haveOf`, ends `thirsty` for water lovers, which a vivarium now is without one, and `behaviorCtx` water). Fittings spread over the whole floor: `vivPlantSpot(e, key, skip, k)` puts the first at the middle of the case (`VIV_PLANT.shift` toward the front) and each next at the spot with the most room to the others (up to `VIV_PLANT.apart`), keeping the sill margin (`VIV34.sill / TILT + .15`) so nothing hides behind the front frame; `relayViv(e)` lays a case's fittings out again that way, run once per save by `upgradeSave` (`state.vivLay`). Animals gather: `vivHomes(e, s)` (lamp glow for heat lovers, pool for water lovers) feed `vivHomeSpot` in map.js `syncAnimals`, `regatherViv` re-places them when a fitting comes or goes, and one standing in a pool (`vivPoolAt`, `h.sub`) is drawn cut at the waterline with a ripple (in a case the cut sits lower for low-slung pictures like the Tiktaalik, so their body still shows; the ripple stays at the waterline). Drawn by `vivPropSvg`/`lampLightSvg` from `sprites/viv/<key>.png` (`VIV_SPRITES` in data.js: pixel size, `flat` for the pool, dish and light, which sort under the animals at `data-y` minus 1000; made by `tools/pixelviv.py`, the five old props and the new ones in the flat style).
Aquariums (dev mode only for now: `aquaS/M/L` in `DEV_BUILDINGS`): a vivarium with `e.aqua`, for species with `aqua` (sea animals; `viv` is the tank size they need). `AQUARIUMS` in data.js holds their price, upkeep and label (`caseOf(e)` in sim.js picks the right table); their shape is `VIVARIUMS[size]`, so everything that sizes a case works unchanged. `fitsHabitat` keeps sea animals in aquariums and everything else out, and `exhibitReport` says why. Sea animals have no biome (`SPECIES_BIOMES` leaves them out), want no plants (`wantsOf`), never get thirsty, and an aquarium has no dirt (`dirtTick` skips it) and no lamps or pools: `caseTakes(e, key)` lets aquarium fittings (LAND `aqua`) only into aquariums and the rest only into vivariums, the food dish into both. Water (landscape.js, `AQUA` in data.js): `aquaLoad(e)` is the animals' food a day, `aquaClean(e)` what the filters (LAND `filter`: `afilter`, `asump`, each `clean` a day and `kw` of power) clean while powered, and `e.wq` (0 to 100, `wqOf`) moves toward `wqAim(e)` = 100 x clean / load at `AQUA.rate` a day (`aquaTick`, every sim tick). `habitatScore` gives clean water a bonus and murky water a penalty, `illChance` raises illness under `AQUA.foul`, and `filterText(e)` is the short status ("No filter.", "No power for the filters.", "Not enough filtering."). The panel's Habitat Items tab opens with a Water section (`aquaWaterHtml` in ui.js) and the Overview has a Water line. map.js draws the water in `viv34` (`AQUA34`: its far faces behind the animals, its near faces and surface over them, from `AQUA34.clean` to `AQUA34.foul` by quality, up to `aquaLine(e)`) on a sand floor, and the animals drift like fish (`aquaSwim`, `AQUA_SWIM`: steer with momentum, `h.vel`, toward a random spot at a random depth, `h.lift`, kept under the waterline by `aquaTop`, easing in, hovering a moment, `h.hover`, then off again, with a slow bob, `h.bob`, added in `animalUp`; species with `floor` crawl the sand slowly instead; resting ones drift near the bottom; turning round squashes the picture through head-on over `AQUA_SWIM.turn` and a swimmer pitches nose up or down with its climb (`h.turn`, `h.pitch`, about `sprMid`; the transform is kept in `h.sprTf` so `spriteSvg`/`rigSvg` rebuild the picture with it and the once-a-second redraw doesn't pop it flat; `poolSwim` keeps its squash there too); a rigged one strokes its walk clip by distance above `AQUA_SWIM.stroke` speed and hovers on its stand loop below, see docs/rigs.md). Fittings are drawn by `tools/pixelaqua.py`. Backdrops: an aquarium's Landscaping tab (`aquaSceneHtml`, action `aquaScene`) sets `e.vivScene = {sea}`, one of `AQUA_SCENES` in data.js (Cambrian, Paleozoic for the Ordovician to the Permian, Mesozoic, Cenozoic; each lists its periods, and `seaSceneOf(s)` is a species' own), through the same `vivSceneProblem`/`setVivScene` and `VIV_SCENE.price`; `vivSceneKey` gives `sea-<key>`, `sceneFit` counts a sea animal's own backdrop in full (no biome half), and `habitatScore` adds `VIV_PLANT.bonus` times that fit. The pictures are `sprites/vivscene/sea-<key>-<size>-<wall|floor>.png` from `tools/pixelaquascene.py` (it borrows `pixelvivscene.py`'s canvas, shading and sizes), drawn by `vivScene34` like a vivarium's; the water's surface takes only `AQUA34.surface` of its thickness so the floor shows through. Paludariums (dev mode only for now): a vivarium's Landscaping tab has a Layout section (Dry or Paludarium, `paluProblem`/`setPalu`, action `palu`, `PALU` in data.js) that sets `e.palu`: the front `PALU.water` of the floor (from `paluY(e)`, `paluWet`, `paluPoly` in map.js) is flooded `PALU.depth` of the glass deep (`paluLine`). `wetCase(e)` (aquarium or paludarium) is what water quality, filters (`caseTakes` lets filters in, and no pool: "It has water already."), power and the Water panel key off; a paludarium's load is only its swimmers' food at `PALU.load`. Swimmers (`paluSwims(s)`: semi-aquatic species, and ones with `wade`, the amphibians that do fine dry but swim when they can) get `PALU.happy` by their share of the case and the water-quality penalty by the same share, need no pool, and move with `aquaSwim` (h.swimmer): `AQUA_SWIM.palu` of their trips are to the water, where they paddle at the surface (`paluTop` is 0) cut at the waterline like a pool wader (`drawAnimal` in map.js, the picture builder `syncAnimals` uses, redraws one as it crosses the shore), the rest to the bank at crawl speed. The water is on the floor: with a scene, `vivScene34` swaps the scene's floor for `<scene>-<size>-palu.png` (tools/pixelpaluscene.py: the scene's floor with its front flooded, a wet strip and a row of shore stones, banded water with ripple rows, lily pads in wetland, tropical and temperate); without one, `paluWater34` draws flat water with a shore row and ripples. Fittings: `vivPlantSpot` puts a paludarium's filters in the water and everything else on the bank, and `setPalu` re-lays a case's fittings (`relayViv`) when it's flooded or drained. `viv34` then lays only a film of `AQUA34.foul` over it as it fouls, a darker water band on the front glass and a waterline, not the aquarium's raised surface. Everyone else keeps to the bank (`vivSpot` refuses the water to them). A species with `palu` (Enaliarctos) lives only in a paludarium (`fitsHabitat`), and a case can't be drained with one in it or with filters fitted.
Pools (dev mode only for now, `poolsOn()`; plan and roster in POOLS_PLAN.md): an open exhibit inside a lagoon wall (`lagoonWalled`, docs/fences.md), flooded with sea water, is home to the `pool` species (big marine animals). Sea water goes nowhere else. A lagoon's panel tab is Aquascape (`aquascapeHtml`, ui.js): one button per `lagoonWaters` type that refills it with `fillLagoon`; rocks and corals are meant to go there later. Sea water is two water types, not paintable: the water tool refuses lagoon-walled exhibits, and putting the gate on one asks which water (`askLagoonWater`: a dropdown of the two sea waters and every biome's fresh water, `lagoonWaters`) and `fillLagoon` floods the whole exhibit polygon, right to the fence line, as one body of water, drawn with `watersSvg(..., plain)`: no shoreline, mud or damp bank in a lagoon: Shallow sea (`sea`) and Deep sea (`deep`), `SEA_WATER` in data.js for their labels and prices (times `WATER.perSqM`; every water price goes through `waterRate(kind)`, and `cutWater`/`waterCut` return `worth`, what the cut water cost, so refunds match). Their textures are `WATER_TEX.sea`/`deep` (a sand shore line, none where shallow meets deep) and `WATER_TILES.sea`/`deep` (`sprites/water/sea-<n>.png`, `deep-<n>.png` from `tools/pixelseawater.py`: plain, a detail tile (sand ripples / dark troughs), three glints). Rules (`POOL`, landscape.js): `poolWater(e, s)` is the sea water (any water for a `fresh` species), `poolFit(e, s)` is `wet` (that water's share of the floor against `POOL.need`) times `deep` (deep sea's share of it against `POOL.deepNeed`, for `deep` species); `habitatScore` adds `POOL.happy` times the fit and takes `POOL.lack` times the shortfall ("No sea water", "Not enough water", "Too shallow", "Open water to swim in.") and, when every animal is a pool animal, skips the land checks. `exhibitReport` counts a pool animal's room in water m² (`wet`, `wetNeed`), and its plants check leaves them out; `likesOf` is `{water:1, rock:0}`, `waterWant` is `POOL.need`, `wantsOf` wants no plants or rocks, `thirsty` is false, `coverOf` gives them no cover need, and `escapeRisk` is null. Drawing and movement are in map.js: `poolSwim` (`POOL_SWIM`) steers toward spots in its water (`poolSpot`: in the water with room for a third of its dot each way; `poolClear`: a straight run through water), `h.dive` fades the body (`poolBodySvg`'s `.dive` group) as it goes down, all the way in deep sea and to `POOL_SWIM.shallow` elsewhere (`poolDepth`), a `.ripple` ring shows while it is up, and a `breach` species leaps (`h.lift`) now and then as it surfaces. A pool animal is placed in its water (`syncAnimals`) and put back in when its water moves. Viewing Galleries (the `gallery` building) are in docs/guests.md.
  Water tiles: a biome in `WATER_TILES` (data.js) has its `#w-<biome>` pattern rebuilt by `buildTiles` (biomepaint.js, the ground tile builder with its own salt) from 1 m tiles `sprites/water/<biome>-<n>.png`: `plain` tiles are one solid color, `detail` tiles carry a mark, `share` of each 5 m chunk's cells take a detail tile, `turn: false` mirrors but never turns. `glint` extra tiles are an animation: the first `glint` of `frames` frames on every plain cell (each cell at its own random start, or with `waves` (a list of [period in frames, wavelength in cells]; wavelengths must divide the 100 m pattern) by its x + y, so bands of glints sweep down and right, and waves of different periods drift in and out of step (the loop is their least common multiple, `frames` is ignored), `fps` frames a second, the pattern's image swapped by one interval; `sub` splits every frame into that many steps, multiplying the periods and fps and holding each glint tile `sub` steps, so bands sweep at the same speed in smaller steps (every water biome uses `sub: 2`, a 48 frame loop at 8 fps)), the other frames the plain tile (glints are never mirrored or turned, so they always travel one way); wetland: 3 glint tiles (`wetland-3..5`), waves [12 frames, 10 cells] and [24 frames, 25 cells] (a 24 frame loop), 4 fps. Wetland is the first (one solid tile, one clump tile with 1 or 2 a chunk, three glint tiles); Fern Prairie uses the same recipe with two glint tiles (`fern-1..4`, same waves) and a 1 px shore line (`WATER_TEX.fern.shore` `#988B4A`, `shoreW` 1, no bank; the colour is from the artist's N/S/E/W edge tiles, which aren't loaded as files); Tundra uses it too (`tundra-1` plain, `tundra-2` an ice floe detail tile (1 or 2 a chunk), `tundra-3..5` glints, same waves, shore `#EBF5F7` (from the artist's edge tiles), 1 px, no bank); Boreal uses it too (`boreal-1` plain, `boreal-2..4` glints, no detail tile, same waves, shore `#4C4A44` (from the artist's edge tiles), 1 px, no bank); Tropical uses it too (`tropical-1` plain, `tropical-2..4` glints, no detail tile, same waves, shore `#D9C08E` (from the artist's edge tiles), 1 px, no bank); Grassland uses it too (`grassland-1` plain, `grassland-2..4` glints, no detail tile, same waves, shore `#7A6246` (from the artist's edge tiles), 1 px, no bank); Seasonal Forest (`temperate`) uses it too (`temperate-1` plain, `temperate-2..4` glints, no detail tile, same waves, shore `#5C4A3D` from the artist's edge tile, 1 px, no bank); Scrubland uses it too (`scrubland-1` plain, `scrubland-2..4` glints, no detail tile, same waves, shore `#B59A6D`, 1 px, no bank); A water kind with an `#r-<kind>` pattern in index.html (`sea`, `deep`) also gets a ripple layer from the same frames: tile 1's one color keyed out of every tile, so only the marks and glints are left on clear ground (the water frame is then that color with the ripples drawn on it). `ripplesSvg` (landscape.js) draws it over a pool's water as the last item of its 3/4 depth group (`data-y` 1e9), so `place34` puts pool animals under it and the ripples pass over them; `tileFill("r", kind)` fills with it. the older biomes still use a 144 px `sprites/water/<biome>.png` from `tools/pixelwater_flat.py`. The shore: a `WATER_TEX` biome with `shore` (wetland: `#544636`, the 1 px north/south/east/west edge tiles) has `waterMask` draw that color on every edge that touches land, in place of the lit and dark rims, and a biome without `damp`/`mud` has no bank around the water. `softenShore` (landscape.js, called from `waterMask`) makes that shore less square for biomes with `shore`: pixels next to land are eroded 0, 1 or 2 px by smooth noise of the world position (`SHORE_NOISE`, `SHORE_ERODE`), then convex corners are chipped `SHORE_CHIP` times. The shore itself is a band `shoreW` px wide (wetland 2), measured inward from the land after the softening, so it follows the stair-steps and corners. It removes only that kind's pixels that touch land, never water of another kind, so joins stay closed, and the same shape always gives the same edge. It is opt-in (only a biome with `shore`), drawing only, and capped at 2 px of erosion so it stays inside the 0.5 m fence margin; its constants are shared by every biome.
  Moving land: the Move tool picks up any rock, plant, shelter or statue (`moveTarget` takes kind `land`; the Move tool turns on the bulldoze hit shapes, `pick`, for land). It drags with `footSnap` and the item rides along live while `footGhostSvg` shows the footprint (a rock's rects) it will take (red when it won't fit). `landMoveSpot(m, wx, wy)` finds the target (the open exhibit under the spot, else the park; a vivarium fitting stays in its own case and nothing else goes in one) and checks it with `landFit` (exhibit) / `decorFit` (park) / the glass rule for a case, each with the item itself skipped. `landProblem` and `decorProblem` are those fit checks plus the research, the Botany Lab, pot and price checks, which a move skips: moving is free and keeps the item's id. `dropLand` (map.js) splices it between `e.land` and `state.decor`, pins a rock's `biome` to the one it had so it keeps its stone, resets the walking animals' paths in both exhibits, calls `regatherViv` for a vivarium and `afterChange`. Shelters in another exhibit are dropped by `shelterStep` (behavior.js).
  Park landscaping (outside exhibits) lives here too. `landSpot` falls back to `decorProblem` when the point isn't in an open exhibit, and `placeLand(null, ...)` puts the item in `state.decor`. Water drawn outside exhibits goes in `state.water` (`waterProblem(pts, null)` → `parkWaterProblem`, `addWater(null, pts)`), and old open fence lines in `state.fences` (`fenceProblem`, `addFenceLine`, `fenceLineCost` in sim.js; kind `"fence"` in map.js for select/move/bulldoze; nothing builds new ones now, the grid fence in fences.js does). `parkFences()` is both those and the loose grid fence, and is what paths, buildings, water, decor and the guests' hedge cheer check. New paths, buildings and exhibits must keep clear of all of it: `shapeHitsLandscape(pts)` and `lineHitsLandscape(pts, hw, bridge)`; only a path with `type:"bridge"` (`isBridge`, `BRIDGE` in data.js) crosses park water. Named modern plants (`PARK_PLANTS` → `LAND["q-<name>"]`, `PARK_PLANTS_OF[biome]`) are Quaternary plants usable anywhere, with `look`/`accent` for drawing (`plantSvg`) and `wet`/`aquatic` for water. Other periods' plants need Research Lab tech `sterile` to go outside exhibits. Statues (`STATUES` → `LAND["st-<id>"]`, `statue` field) are dev-mode only for v1.0 (`statuesOn()` in data.js = `devMode()`: with it off the Statues dropdown is hidden, `statueOpen` is false so `decorProblem` refuses them, and `grantStatue(g)` is null so grants and the award toast never mention or unlock one; statues already in a save keep loading, drawing and cheering guests) and go outside exhibits only; a few are `free`, the rest unlock with a grant's `statue` field (`statueOpen`, `statueGrant`, shown by `grantPrize`). A statue's plinth uses its theme's path color (`statueSvg`). Guests: `indexDecor(nodes)` (called from `buildGuestGraph`) marks each guest-path node with `n.decor` (0 to 1, `DECOR`) and `n.statues`; `guestsTick` turns decor into mood and the "pretty" thought, and `statueSeen` gives learning (people's statues teach far more). Drawing: `waterSvg`, `featSvg`, `parkWaterSvg`, `decorSvg`, `fenceLineSvg`, `hedgeSvg` (hedge rows, `BARRIERS.hedge`, are drawn as real hedges in exhibits too).
- `js/themes.js`: themes (`THEMES`, `THEME` in data.js).  Zones can carry a `theme` (`zoneThemePlan`, `restyleZone`, `themedZoneAt`): it tints the map under the zone (`THEMES[x].ground`), new builds inside use it, and a panel button restyles what is already there (UI in logiui.js `zoneThemeHtml`). Genesis (the default, no `theme` field) is a full theme too: a conservation campus of forest green, matte gold and warm concrete. `roofs` sort buildings by job (green standing seam for public ones, pale flat lab roofs with `units` for Research Lab/Time Travel Lab/Genetics Lab/Botany Lab/PMC, dark slate for utilities), exhibits get the steel rail or panel wall with amber post lights while an electric fence is powered (map.js). Paths, buildings and exhibits carry an optional `theme` (none means Genesis) that recolors them (`themeOf`, `themeFill`). `state.themes` holds the build `brush` and the unlocked `have` list; `checkThemes` (called from `checkGoals`) unlocks them by play (Gilded Age by the `gilded` Research Lab tech, Bayou by keeping a water-loving animal, Volcanic by a 4-star rating). `themeZone(e)` checks that the guest paths and shops and restrooms within `THEME.radius` of an exhibit all match it (non-Genesis only), which adds appeal in `recompute`, and `themeFitShare` (species in `THEMES[x].fits`) lifts happiness in `exhibitReport`. Changing a theme costs `fee` of the item's price (`themeFee`, `themeProblem`); new builds use the brush (`themeNew`). The pickers are in ui.js (`themeHtml`, `themesOverview`). Loads after landscape.js.
- `js/biomepaint.js`: paintable biome ground outside exhibits, loaded just before map.js. **Ground tiles:** a biome listed in `GROUND_TILES` (data.js: `{plain, detail, share}`) has 8 x 8 px, 1 m tiles, `sprites/ground/<biome>-<n>.png` (8 px a meter, so 0.125 m a pixel, like the 18 m `<biome>.png` tiles): the first `plain` are plain ground, the next `detail` carry a mark. `buildTiles` (end of biomepaint.js) composes a 100 m square with one tile per 1 m cell: in each 5 m chunk about `share` of the cells (the lowest hashes, `groundPicks`) get a detail tile, the rest plain, and each cell is turned and mirrored at random by `cellHash` (except tiles in `still`, like boreal's lit stone; all three wetland, and temperate and tropical tiles are still, and tundra's first two; grassland's second), so it is fixed between draws and reloads. The result goes into both the `#p-<biome>` (park ground, paint outside exhibits) and `#b-<biome>` (exhibit floors) patterns' images. Desert and scrubland are not in `GROUND_TILES`: each floor is one seamless 288 px tile (36 m, so their two patterns in index.html are 36 m wide) from `tools/pixelground_calm.py`, a pale flat floor with faint specks and a few small low-contrast details (desert: dune arcs, tufts, twigs, pebbles; scrubland: sage hardpan with cracks, red sandstone chips, twigs and dead brush), because some details are wider than a 1 m tile. The Paleopedia card reads that width off the pattern (`ppBg`). Building is lazy: every fill with a `#p-`, `#b-` or `#w-` pattern goes through `tileFill(kind, biome)`, which queues that biome's ground or water the first time it is drawn (until then the pattern shows its single fallback tile from index.html). Frames are drawn one per idle callback (every biome's first frame ahead of anyone's animation frames) and saved with `toBlob` as blob URLs, so nothing blocks the load; a water animation starts once all its frames are in. Jobs run several to an idle period, and at least one every `TILE_WAIT` ms when the page never goes idle, so a busy park still gets its water moving. A new place that fills with one of these patterns must use `tileFill`, or the biome never builds there. To add or replace tiles, see `tools/BIOME_ART.md` section 1b. A tiled biome's park ground and paint skip the dark-mode dimming layer (`groundShade`, biomepaint.js), so they match the exhibit floors, which are never dimmed. `state.biomePaint` is a list of `{id, biome, r:[x0,y0,x1,y1]}` patches on the 1 m grid, drawn in list order by `paintSvg` (cached by `paintVer`; `#p-<biome>` pattern over the biome's `park` color, clipped to `#plotClip`) in `render` just above the park ground and under paths, buildings and exhibits. It is decoration only; `state.biome` stays locked. `paintRect(biome, box)` deletes patches the new one fully covers, trims partly covered ones when one rectangle is left, merges same-biome neighbours when nothing above them overlaps, and ignores a repaint of what is already on top; `eraseRect` splits patches, `erasePaintAt`, `repickPaint(id, biome)` (tap a patch with the tool). The `paint` tool (Landscaping > Paint Ground; `paintBar`, `endPaintDrag`, `pdrag`, `paintPick` biome or "erase" in map.js, biome rows, plus Erase, listed under Paint Ground in the Landscaping tray (`data-paint`)) drags a box in whole 5 m chunks like the path tools (`pdrag` holds chunk coords, `paintBox(a, b)` turns two chunks into meters, `paintBoxW` does it from world points; a tap paints or erases one chunk; old patches keep their odd sizes); Bulldoze on bare ground also takes paint off (`bulldozePaint`). `upgradeSave` defaults the list. Inside open exhibits (not vivariums) the same paint is drawn over the floor (`exhibitPaintSvg`, clipped to the exhibit) and decides the exhibit's biome: `biomeOf(e)` (landscape.js) is `paintedBiome(e)`, the biome with the most sampled ground (`paintShare`, cached per exhibit), where unpainted ground counts as `e.biome` and a tie keeps it. Painting inside exhibits costs `exhibitPaintCost` (the biome's `perSqM` for each m² not already that biome); out in the park it's free. The exhibit panel's Biome section (`biomeHtml`) shows the split and a Paint ground button. A vivarium has no Biome section: its biome is its scene's (`biomeOf` reads `e.vivScene.biome`, else the `e.biome` it was built with), set from the Scene panel.

# Art: sprites, footprints, buildings, fence pictures, art direction

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- Human sprites: keepers are drawn as pixel people (`HUMAN_SPRITES` in data.js; `humanSvg`, `humanStep` in map.js, used by `drawKeepers`). Strips in `sprites/human/` are frames of 16 x 32 px side by side, facing right: `keeper-walk.png` (4 frames, advances by distance walked, frame 0 when still) and `keeper-work.png` (4 frames, plays on the clock, `ms` a frame, while a keeper stands still with a job in `work.jobs`: mucking, filling) and `keeper-wait.png` (4 frames, `ms` a frame, standing with nothing to do) and `keeper-carry.png` (4 frames, advances by distance walked like the walk strip, shown while a keeper walks with food in hand (`c.carry`); the feed crate is drawn in front of the hands by `carryBox` in map.js, in every pose (walk, work, wait, carry) while `c.carry` holds food, so it always looks the same). The figure is mirrored when it walks left. They are drawn about 3.6 m tall (a little over true pixel size so they read) and never under 24 screen px. Guests have their own kinds, `HUMAN_SPRITES.guest`, `guest2` to `guest5` (`guest[2-5]-walk.png` and `-wait.png`, 4 frames each, no work or carry strips; `guestKind(p)` picks one by party id, add a kind to `GUEST_KINDS` for more): `drawParties` draws each party as a pooled `guestEls` sprite (`humanSvg`/`humanStep`, standing frames while still) with a pip under its feet in the personality (or mood) color once a guest is `GUEST_SPRITE_PX` (10) screen px tall, and as the old dots when zoomed further out. Vets are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.vet`, `vet-walk/work/wait/carry.png`, green scrubs; work plays while `checking`, `treating`, `darting` or `splicing`; carry is unused, vets hold nothing). Custodians are pixel people (`HUMAN_SPRITES.custodian`, `custodian-*.png`; work plays while `scrubbing`, `emptying` or `sweeping`, and the carry pose shows the crate in the stock's color). Mechanics are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.mechanic`, `mechanic-*.png`, orange coveralls and hard hat; work plays while `inspecting` or `repairing`, carry is unused). Guards are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.guard`, `guard-walk/wait/escort.png`, navy uniform and cap; no carry strip; the escort strip is its `work` strip and plays while `c.job === "escorting"`). Catching a party (a vandal, or one the player called security on) or reaching one that waits (`escortOut`, personas.js) sets `c.escort = p`: the guard stands 1.2 m beside the party (`keeperPos`) until `p.gone`, can't catch or take another meanwhile, and the party walks home unhappy and no longer a vandal. Mechanics and vets are covered above.
- Animal size: `animalRadius(sp)` (map.js) is `sqrt(space)/9` held to 1.2..6. The picture is 2.5 radii wide (4 in a vivarium) times the entry's `size` (`rsize` for a rig), so to enlarge one species give it `rsize` and scale its `rstep` by the same factor (the Dimetrodon: 1.6).
- Animal sprites: `sprites/<species id>.png` standing (side-on, facing right, transparent), listed in `SPRITES` in data.js with `ratio` (width over height) and `walk`, the frame count of `sprites/<id>-walk.png` (frames side by side, each the standing picture's size, feet on the same row). `spriteSvg` in map.js draws one in place of the dot (`foot` in the entry is the share of the picture's height where the feet are, for a picture with empty rows under it: the Tiktaalik's is .57, a couple of rows under its feet, so it stands on its spot instead of floating) (open exhibits, once it's at least 6px across); in `animateAnimals` it flips to face the way it walks and `setPose` steps the walk frame by distance walked (`h.stride`), back to standing (or, if the entry also has `idle`, cycling `sprites/<id>-idle.png` via `restPose`; `setPose` frames: -1 stands, 0+ walk, -2 and down idle) while it waits. Dynamic idles (`idleFrame` in map.js; the Tiktaalik): with `base` (frames that loop) and `dyn` (`[[first, count], ...]`, clips that take turns), `dynMs` and `dynGap` ([min, max] seconds), the strip's other frames play once now and then, a small movement, and it settles back. The Tiktaalik's strip is 4 basic frames, a look-around and a push-up, built from its rig by `tools/rigger/tiktaalik/gen-idles.js` (it writes `sprites/tikt.png` and `sprites/tikt-idle.png`; the rig is only used to make the pictures, it isn't in `rigs.js`). Vivarium animals don't walk: they stay on their spot (spaced by `cramped`) and, if the entry has `idle` (frame count of `sprites/<id>-idle.png`, same strip format) and optionally `ms` (per frame), cycle it; with no idle strip they show the standing picture, and with no `SPRITES` entry the dot. Run `tools/packsprites.py` after adding PNGs (see Sprite pack). A species can instead be drawn from a rig (js/rigs.js, `rig:1`), which keeps the strips as the fallback. Every species is drawn from its PNGs (there used to be coded copies of the Coelophysis, Dimetrodon and Tiktaalik in `js/pixelart.js` that the game painted instead; they're gone, so a change to a PNG is what shows). Each has a shading pass over its PNGs: `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py`, `pixelprio_shade.py` (also sways the walk strip's tail), `pixeltikt_shade.py`, `pixelcoel_shade.py`, `pixeldime_shade.py`; all but the Dimetrodon's give the same result when run twice. The Lystrosaurus (`sprites/lyst*.png`, drawn at `size:.7`) is shaded by `tools/pixellyst_shade.py` (smooth leathery hide, no scales: creased thigh and shoulder, wrinkles, sparse grain; it also cuts `lyst.png` from the walk strip). Its 8-frame idle strip (`idle:8`: lowers its head and sniffs the dirt, then plays back to standing) is built by `tools/pixellyst_idle.py` from hand-posed pictures in `tools/lyst-idle/` (cells sampled off their 13.55 px grid, snapped to the palette, shaded with the same rules, the whole animal moved 2 px left so the lowered snout fits); run the shade script first. Cotylorhynchus keeps its own palette and body shape; `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py` adds the flat-style shading and texture to `sprites/coty*.png` in place and redraws its head (`reshape_head`: a small blunt-snouted caseid skull, per frame offset, eye and mouth). Its idle strip is 8 frames (`idle:8` in `SPRITES`): it dips and blows a bubble, the bubble pops, it is surprised, settles and stands (`IDLE_EXPR`, `SPECKS`); the first run grew it from 6 frames. It reads shaded colors back first, so re-running is safe, and it asserts nothing left of the head moved.
- Footprints and tap shapes: a plant takes a SQUARE of whole 1 m cells on the ground, a pictured rock or boulder a few whole-cell rects cut from its silhouette (see below), each standing on its picture's base: the box's front (bottom) edge is at the picture's bottom, `f.y`, and it runs back up the map from there (`footBased`; pictured plants and rocks only, others stay centered; `footSnap` puts `y` on a grid line for them; old saves slide down by half a side once, flagged `state.footBase`, so no box moves; a plant listed in `FOOT.rect` (data.js) has a real base instead of a square: `footW` its base width to the half meter, `footD` `FOOT.depth` of that, snapped to half meters by its edges, so things can stand close in front, behind and beside it and layer by depth; add a plant's key to `FOOT.rect` when its picture is redrawn with a real base, and the rest keep their square) (`FOOT` in data.js: `range` per kind, small 1 to 2 m, medium 2 to 4, large 3 (trees, so a small no-go zone); `plain` is the middle for a picture-less one; `crown` factor; `rockCover`/`rockWide` for rocks; `side` overrides one picture by hand with a square). `SPRITE_HIT` (made by `tools/spritehit.py`, re-run it after changing a plant or rock picture) gives each picture's base and whole width; landscape.js turns that into `LAND[plant].footSide` (a plant's base width, or `FOOT.crown` of its crown if more, rounded and held to the range) and `ROCK_FOOT[type][biome][picture]` = `{w, rects}` (`rockFootOf`: the picture's bands, mirrored so a flipped rock takes the same ground as its ghost, with the 4 px it sinks below its point left out, rasterised on the 1 m grid; a cell is taken when the silhouette covers `FOOT.rockCover` of it, each row runs first to last taken cell, equal rows join into one rect, so a rock is usually 1 or 2 rects, tall ones narrow and tall; `w` is the width in whole cells, odd centred on a cell and even on a grid corner; a rock with no picture to read, or a `FOOT.side` override, gets a square). `footSideFor(key, pic, biome)` is the side (a rock's `w`), `footRects(f, e)` a placed thing's rects `[[x0, y0, x1, y1], ...]` (e: its exhibit, null out in the park; a rock's picture is `f.pic` or the id-based pick, `rockPicIndex`; anything that isn't a pictured rock is one box), `footRectsAt(key, pic, biome, x, y)` a new one's, `footBox`/`footBoxAt` the one box around the rects, and `footSnap` puts a point on the grid so the square covers whole cells (odd sides or widths on a cell center, even on a grid corner; `landSpot` does it, from the raw pointer spot `landPoint`). `rockPicFor(type, biome)` is the picture the next rock gets (the picked one, else a roll made ahead in `rockRoll` and redone after each placement, so the ghost shows and measures what a tap will place; `landSpot` returns it as `pic` for `placeLand`). Everything else in `LAND` (barns, caves, trays, toys) has no `footSide` and takes a box `2 * footR(t)` wide. All ground-space checks use the rects, not a circle, and a thing touching only along an edge is fine; the `rects*` helpers (`rectsHit`, `rectsHitPoly`, `rectsInside`, `rectsHitLine`, `rectsDist`, `rectsOnChunk`) fold these over each rect: `boxesHit` (two boxes), `boxHitsPoly`/`boxInside` (water, buildings, platforms, exhibit outlines; `boxInside` lets the square sit flush against its fence), `lineHitsBox(box, pts, hw)` (fence lines and pieces, old paths with their half width; with no width a line along the edge is allowed, one through the inside is not), `boxPointDist` (gate, entrance), `boxOnChunk` (path chunks); the users are `landProblem`, `decorProblem`, `waterProblem`/`parkWaterProblem`, `shapeHitsLandscape`, `lineHitsLandscape`, `fenceProblem`, `pieceSpotProblem` (fences.js), `pathCtx`/`pathChunkProblem` (pathcells.js) and `reshapeProblem` (map.js). Derived `d` path strips are skipped in `decorProblem` (the chunks are checked directly). Animals walk around them: `walkArea` (map.js) obstacles are rounded boxes (`hx`, `hy` half sides, `c` margin; `obsDist`, `segBoxDist`): a rock keeps off each rect of its footprint, a tree its trunk (about a third of the square), low plants are walked through. The placement ghost draws the footprint on the grid (`footGhostSvg`: a square, or one dashed outline round a rock's rects). `r` still sizes the drawing and the habitat math (`haveOf`, `wantsOf`, `landM2`), so happiness and food don't depend on the footprint. Old saves: `snapFootprints(s)` (landscape.js, last step of `upgradeSave`) snaps every plant and rock to its footprint and, where two now overlap, nudges one up to a cell (preferring spots still inside its fence); it never removes anything and does nothing to a thing already on a square. Bulldozing and tapping use the sprite's silhouette (`spriteHitPath`, `hitShape`, the same bands that cut the rects), not the footprint. Vivarium fittings are unchanged (`f.k`, their own placement).
- Landscape sprites: plants are `sprites/plants/<LAND key>.png` (upright, base at the bottom middle), listed in `PLANT_SPRITES` in data.js (`ratio`, `size`); `plantSpriteSvg` in landscape.js draws one in `featSvg`, flipped by its seed, and plants with no entry keep their SVG. Rocks are `sprites/rocks/<type>-<biome>-<n>.png`, listed in `ROCK_SPRITES` (`[width, height]` per picture, `ROCK_MPP` meters per pixel); `rockSpriteSvg` picks one by the rock's id. Ground is `sprites/ground/<biome>.png` (scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal so far), the tile behind the `#b-<biome>` and `#p-<biome>` patterns in index.html; scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal use one 18 m tile for both (boreal's `color` and `park` are #587260, from `boreal_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: cold conifer-forest floor of brown litter dashes, moss and dark humus patches, thin twigs, flecks and small wet stones, no grass, flowers or modern plants; its park used to be the paler #8FA694 and its exhibit color #5E8070, so boreal parks look darker and browner-green now; tundra's `color` and `park` are #B3BDA3, from `tundra_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: frost-heaved grit with pale and dark flecks, frost cracks, pebbles and tiny lichen specks, no grass, flowers or plants; its park used to be the paler #CBD2C2, so tundra parks look a little darker and greener now; grassland's `color` and `park` are #C3CC76, from `grassland_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: sunlit loam with pale silt, clay and soil flecks, dry-earth dashes and small pebbles, no grass or flowers; its park used to be the paler #B7C995, so grassland parks look a little greener now; temperate's `color` and `park` are #7DAA68, from `temperate_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: loam, moss and humus flecks, no grass), and their `park` color equals their `color` (tropical's is deep forest-floor green #002104, from `tropical_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: dark forest green with brown humus patches, fallen brown leaves, leaf litter, twigs and damp pebbles, no grass or flowers; desert's is dune sand #DFC48A, from `desert_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: wind ripples, pebbles and sandstone chips; wetland's is the muted marsh green #6F9A7A; the wetland tile adds mud, algae film and small shallow pools, from `wetland_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`). Water: `WATER_TEX` in landscape.js lists the biomes whose water is a pixel tile (`sprites/water/<biome>.png`, the `#w-<biome>` pattern in index.html, made by `tools/pixelwater_flat.py`, one palette per biome; wetland, scrubland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal so far (boreal: dark cold tannin-tinted forest pool, kept clean (ripples and glints only), moss damp ring, dark peat bank, pale rim; tundra: cold clear glacial blue-grey, kept clean (ripples and glints only), pale frosted damp ring, ice-white rim in place of mud; grassland: clear green-teal river, kept clean like temperate (ripples and glints only), olive damp ring, tan mud bank; tropical: deep blue-green jungle pool, two-tone wash, bright glints, dark leaf-litter mud bank), each with its four bank colors in `WATER_TEX[biome]`, with a stair-stepped shore cut to the pixel grid by `waterMask`: a light shallows rim inside and a wet-mud (scrubland: salt-crust) bank outside, cached by shape); `waterSvg` takes the biome (the exhibit's, or the park's for water outside) and other biomes keep the flat blue. Pictures are generated by `tools/pixelplants.py` (older shaded plants, superseded), `tools/pixelplant_flat.py` (the large Permian plant, in the flat style: the model for new plants) and `tools/pixelplants_flat.py` (the other Permian and Triassic scrubland plants, same style, shared helpers), `tools/pixelplants_scrubland.py` (the other 18 scrubland plants: Devonian and Jurassic to Quaternary, helpers copied in), `tools/pixelrocks.py` (older, superseded), `tools/pixelrocks_tropical.py` (the tropical rocks, seen 3/4 like the rest but not stepped tiers: each stone is m rings of a rounded dome from a lumpy harmonic outline, so it has a vertical front wall, a rounded shoulder and a lit top, flat tone bands from the light, moss only on the shoulder and top, and two-stone clusters; the model for rounder, less repetitive shapes) and `tools/pixelrocks_flat.py` (the scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, grassland, tundra and boreal rocks, flat style; boreal's are rounded dark rain-wet granite (many sides, small jitter) in plain, moss-capped, rust-stained and pale weathered variants; tundra's are frost-shattered grey slabs stacked in shifted plates, with yellow-green, orange and pale grey lichen-colored caps, a dark slate and a pale granite; grassland is rounded grey fieldstone, a lichen-capped pair, an iron-stained one and pale limestone; desert's banded sandstone passes `render` a list of ramps, one per strata layer) `tools/pixelplants_grassland.py` (the six grassland plants, Neogene and Quaternary only: Bluegrass, Kangaroo Grass, Pampas Grass, Fescue, Grama, Bluestem, plus the Prairie Wildflowers Park Plant) `tools/pixelplants_tundra.py` (the six tundra plants, Neogene and Quaternary only: Cottongrass, Crowberry, Arctic Willow, Saxifrage, Mountain Avens, Dwarf Birch, plus the Labrador Tea Park Plant) `tools/pixelplants_boreal.py` (the 18 boreal plants, Permian onward, plus the Black Spruce and Quaking Aspen Park Plants; common names from the Paleogene: Royal Fern / Birch / Larch, Blueberry / Alder / Spruce, Reindeer Lichen / Marsh Rosemary / Pine) `tools/pixelplants_wetland.py` (all 27 wetland plants, nine periods, plus the six wetland Park Plants `q-bald-cypress` ... `q-cypress-knees`, same helpers copied in) `tools/pixelground.py` (older, superseded) and `tools/pixelground_flat.py` (the scrubland ground, flat style). `tools/pixeldime_shade.py` is the shading pass over `PIXEL_ART.dime`; `tools/pixeldime.py` built the original. Shelters and trays: `sprites/shelter/<LAND key>.png` and `sprites/tray/<key>.png` (made by `tools/pixelshelter.py`), sized in `SHELTER_SPRITES` (`w`, `h` px, `b` the base in radii below the spot) and `TRAY_SPRITES` (`well`: the hollow `[cx, cy, rx, ry]`) in data.js; `shelterSpriteSvg` and `traySpriteSvg` (landscape.js) draw them in `featSvg` before the vector drawings, which stay only for `f.k` items. A shelter stands with its base `b` radii under the spot and is never flipped (a barn's open front faces the viewer); a tray lies flat, and the food is drawn into its well as stepped rows in `FOOD_COLOR`, its area `trayHas / tray` (min 2 px). `spritehit.py` also scans these folders, so they have tap shapes and a `footSide` (the picture's width in whole meters); `snapFootprints` leaves them where old saves put them, and `wallStrips34` (map.js) hides what stands behind a barn's rectangle or a cave's dome. Run `tools/packsprites.py` after adding PNGs (see Sprite pack). Rock shape picker: the player picks a rock or boulder picture in the build menu's Landscaping group (`refreshShapes` in menu.js: a Random button and one `image-rendering:pixelated` thumbnail per `ROCK_SPRITES[biome][type]` picture, for `rockBiomeNow()`: the biome tab, the exhibit under the cursor, or `parkBiome()`; the choice is `rockPick[type]`, null for random, and `rockPicOf` drops it when the biome has fewer pictures). `placeLand(..., pic)` stores it as `f.pic` (saved in `e.land`/`state.decor`; absent on old rocks, which keep the id-based pick in `rockSpriteSvg`, so `upgradeSave` needs no default), and the map ghost draws that picture. Footprints: placement and the ghost ring use the per-type average `LAND[x].fr`, while the tap shape and bulldoze hit use the picked picture's own `SPRITE_HIT` entry (`<type>-<biome>-<n>`).
- Building doors: `building34` and `block34` draw the door, sign and windows on the wall that `b.face` names (`faceEdge`), not the widest wall. A wall the camera can't see (north is hidden, east and west are edge-on) gets `faceMark34` instead: facing north, a cream name plaque stands on the roof's far edge with a dark door tab (gold handle pixel) hung under it; facing east or west, a door column on that side edge and the name plaque on the visible front wall's top band. All flat fills snapped by `flatSnap34`, no outlines. Graffiti stays on the widest visible wall. Carts and tram stops have no door, so they keep the widest wall; the campground and platform have their own fronts.
- Flat buildings (map.js, before `building34`): `FLAT34` palette, `flatSnap34`, `flatWalls34`, `flatWc34`, `flatRoof34`, `FLAT_FONT34`/`FLAT_ICON34` (pixel lettering and pictograms). `building34` uses them for the Genesis restroom only, except that the restroom is now drawn as one picture, `sprites/bld/restroom-front.png` or `restroom-back.png` (80 x 80 px at `ROCK_MPP`, `restroomSprite34`): the back when the door wall faces away from the camera (its normal points up the screen), else the front, which also stands in for the side views until they exist; the 61 px wall is the 8 m footprint and the base slab sits on the footprint's front edge (the flat pieces stay in the file for the sides); every other building and theme still has the older vector drawing. Vivariums are flat too (`viv34`, `vivFloor34`, `VIV34`, `VIV_GLASS34` in map.js, 3/4 view only; the flat top-down view keeps its old glass polygon): `vivFloor34` is the substrate floor (the biome color mixed with soil, rows of dark and light dashes, a lit strip on the far sides), `viv34(e, "back"/"front")` the far panes, posts and sill, and after the contents the near panes (semi-transparent with glare streaks), sill (the solid base), and a clear glass lid. A case with a scene (`e.vivScene`, landscape.md) draws `vivScene34(e, "floor")` in place of the substrate and `vivScene34(e, "wall")` over the back pane from the top of the sill to the top of the glass, before the contents: `sprites/vivscene/<scene>-<S|M|L>-<wall|floor>.png`, `<scene>` being `vivSceneKey` (`<era>-<biome>`, or `<period>-<biome>`). `tools/pixelvivscene.py` makes all of them from shared parts (era palettes and plant shapes, biome ground, sky, tints and floor decals, a stepped hill skyline), sized from each case (0.13 m a pixel across; the wall is the pane height above the sill, the floor's rows squashed by TILT), as palette PNGs to keep the sprite pack small; `--sheet` writes a contact sheet. A new scene, size or period is a table entry there, never a hand-drawn picture. The whole case is glass above its base, with a one-pixel see-through light blue border (floor and lid edges, corner edges); no frame, posts or lamp bars. The case is a plain glass box: no corner posts, middle post or lamp bars, so nothing hides the animals. S has a light aluminium base, M dark wood, L dark aluminium. Vivarium animals are placed by `vivSpot` (map.js: the picture's width clears the side walls and posts, the feet stay behind the sill) and re-placed when the case moves. Frame colors are one flat tone a face, selected/doomed show an outline only. Path objects (bin, bench, picnic, lamp, camera, donation box, info sign, Do Not Feed sign) are in the flat pixel style too, in the 3/4 view, in `js/propart.js` (loaded after map.js): `propArt(bl, inv, on, dead)` builds each from a few boxes (`pbox`: a tone a face, left lightest, front base, right darkest, top lightest, snapped by `flatPoly34`; `pbits` paints pictograms like the sign's `i` and the Do Not Feed ring, `PROP_ICONS`), colors from the item's `themeFill` and theme edge, outlined only when selected or doomed, dark red when broken. Each faces along +v of its footprint (`propFront(b)`, read off the points, which are `rectPts` in order, so it needs no saved field): bin opening, bench seat side, picnic long side with the gold plate, lamp lens, camera lens, donation slot, sign face; the other sides differ (backrest, rails, bare grey sign back, lock plate). Rotation: R / the Rotate button turn the item being placed in 45° steps (`rot` in map.js, ghost drawn by `propArt` with an arrow out of its front); at rot 0 it faces the path it stands by; the count is saved as `b.rot` (`upgradeSave` defaults old ones to 0; the Move tool's Rotate bumps it). The flat top-down view keeps its old shapes plus a white front bar (`propFrontMark`). Nothing in the sim reads the facing yet; hooks are commented at `hasSign` (guests.js) and `onCamera` (security.js).
- Fence pictures (map.js, `fence34`): the metal bar fence (`BARRIERS.bars`, `FENCE34.bars.sprite`) is drawn from `sprites/fence/bars-<pristine|degrading|broken>.png`, one 3 m section of 23 x 20 px at `ROCK_MPP` (made by `tools/pixelfence_bars.py`, the generator holds the art; `FENCE_SPRITES`, `FENCE_LOOK` in data.js). `fenceState(cond)` picks the picture from the exhibit's condition (`o.cond`, passed in `fo34` by `knownCond(e)`): broken at 0 (also every breakout, which sets cond to 0), degrading below `FENCE_LOOK.worn` (60, the panel's "good" line), pristine above and for loose fence (no condition). Broken is a trampled hole with the middle flattened, by design the same for neglect and for a breakout. `fenceSpriteSvg` lays sections along a side left to right (never mirrored), each a nested `<svg>` cropped to its width, turned along the side by a shear `matrix` (pixel `ROCK_MPP` across and `ROCK_MPP / TILT` up, like `lift34`), the last one cut short and a post at the far end. A side running mostly north-south (under `FENCE_LOOK.minRun` of its length across the screen) is seen edge-on, so it returns null and `fence34` calls `fenceEdgeSvg` instead: a column `FENCE_LOOK.edge` pixels wide (lit left, base middle, shaded right, `FENCE_EDGE` colors) and as tall as the fence, with a cap and footing at every post, rust blocks when degrading, and a low heap with snapped post stubs when broken. Condition is per exhibit, so every bar side shows the same state. The keeper gate has its own picture per fence kind (`FENCE_SPRITES[kind].gate`, `sprites/fence/bars-gate.png`, same generator): a section wide, a heavy post with a gold ball at each end, a double leaf and a gold latch plate. Gates never wear or break, so there are no worn states, but it swings open: frames `bars-gate-1/2/3.png` (each leaf shortens toward its hinge post, the latch goes). `drawGates(now)` (map.js, every frame from `step`) moves `gateOpen[id]` (0 shut to 1 open, not saved) toward 1 while a keeper, vet or mechanic is within `GATE_OPEN.reach` of `e.gate` (else toward 0, at `GATE_OPEN.speed` a second) and sets the picture on the `[data-gate]` elements the render drew; `render()` draws the current frame (`gateFrameOf`), so a redraw doesn't flicker it shut. `gateSide34(e, kinds)` finds the side the gate (`e.gate`) is on, `fence34` takes it as `o.gate` {i, p}, `fenceSpriteSvg` runs the fence up to the gate's posts and sets the picture on top, and an edge-on side gets posts, finials and a latch, which become a leaf swung out from each post when open (`fenceEdgeSvg`, `GATE_GOLD`). A gate on a fence with no gate picture (and in the flat view) is still the gold square marker, which a picture-gate only shows in red when `gateCheck` finds a problem. The wooden post-and-rail fence (`FENCE34.wood.sprite`) works the same way from `sprites/fence/wood-*.png` (23 x 12 px, made by `tools/pixelfence_wood.py`): warm brown pristine, silvered grey with moss, a sagging rail and rusty nails when degrading, a smashed gap with boards on the ground when broken, plus its own gate (gold cap posts, diagonal braces, same swing frames). `FENCE_EDGE[kind]` holds each kind's edge-on colors (`steel` is its own material, `rust` its worn look). ## Art direction (locked)
The Dimetrodon (`sprites/dime*.png`, shaded by `tools/pixeldime_shade.py`) is the reference: flat, simple, cartoony pixel art.
- **Shapes and color:** flat fills, no outlines, stair-stepped silhouettes, readable at game size. About 8 base colors a sprite, each with a lighter and a darker shade of the same hue. Palette family: olive greens (97,111,34), rust orange (178,78,0), yellow-olive and warm browns; buildings use their theme's colors.
- **Detail budget:** flat shapes plus about 30%, all structured: a lit top-left edge, shadow on undersides and the right side, rhythmic texture (rows of short dashes, grooves beside ribs, scutes, a lighter rib line). Never random scatter, noise or heavy dithering; it reads as sandpaper.
- **Light** comes from the top left everywhere.
- **Exhibits are full 2D:** flat ground and upright sprites, y-sorted. Animals are side-on facing right (mirrored by `face`). Don't render animals from 3D models; that was tried, cost a lot, and looked worse than simple hand-made shapes.
- **Buildings are 3/4 and front-facing**, extruded from the footprint by `building34`, so they rotate. Flat style: one flat tone per face (left-facing lightest, front the base tone, right-facing darkest), no strokes except for selected, doomed and unreachable states, every point snapped to the pixel grid (`flatSnap34`: `ROCK_MPP` = 0.13 m a pixel, with the vertical step divided by `TILT` so pixels are square after the squash), and pixel-built text and pictograms. Use the game's own names ("Restrooms", never "WC"). Keep the snap's tie-break (the `+ 1e-4`), or exact half-pixel positions round at random and drop columns.
- **Pixel size** is the same everywhere: 0.13 m a pixel.
- **Ground tiles** wrap and repeat, so keep them free of large or distinctive features, and of anything from the wrong era or biome (no grass or sage in Permian scrubland).
- **Check at game zoom and zoomed in.** A blurry screenshot hides bugs; reconstruct the drawn pixels (count them, or print them as ASCII) when something looks off.
- **Status:** in this style so far: the Dimetrodon, the Cotylorhynchus (shading and a new head, own palette and body), the Coelophysis rig (shading and texture only, own colors), all 24 scrubland landscape plants, the scrubland rocks, boulders, ground and water, the temperate rocks and boulders (grey, moss-capped, stained and leaf-litter), the wetland ground, rocks, boulders, all 27 plants and the six wetland Park Plants, the three Quaternary temperate plants and the seven temperate Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate.py`), the temperate Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_old.py`) and Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_meso.py`) and Paleogene and Neogene plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_ceno.py`, so all 27 temperate plants are done), all 27 desert plants (`tools/pixelplants_desert_old.py`, `_meso.py`, `_ceno.py`; ## Sprite pack (read before adding any sprite)
The artifact holds at most 511 files, so the ~480 PNGs are NOT published one by one. `tools/packsprites.py` packs every `sprites/**/*.png` into `js/spritepack.js` (data URIs, generated: never edit it), and every drawn picture goes through `spriteUrl(path)` (defined there; a short `blob:` URL when blobs load, else the data URI, with the path kept as a `#fragment`). Index.html's ground and water tiles use `data-sp` for the same reason. **After adding or changing any PNG, run `python3 tools/packsprites.py`, commit the new `js/spritepack.js`, and publish it.** New code that puts a sprite path in an `<image href>` or `<img src>` must wrap it in `spriteUrl(...)`. Keep PNGs in git as the source; only the pack is published. Merge conflicts in `js/spritepack.js`: take either side and re-run the tool.

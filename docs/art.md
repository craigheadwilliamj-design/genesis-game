# Art: sprites, footprints, buildings, fence pictures, art direction

Moved out of CLAUDE.md. Update this file (not CLAUDE.md) when the code it describes changes.

- Human sprites: keepers are drawn as pixel people (`HUMAN_SPRITES` in data.js; `humanSvg`, `humanStep` in map.js, used by `drawKeepers`). Strips in `sprites/human/` are frames of 16 x 32 px side by side, facing right: `keeper-walk.png` (4 frames, advances by distance walked, frame 0 when still) and `keeper-work.png` (4 frames, plays on the clock, `ms` a frame, while a keeper stands still with a job in `work.jobs`: mucking, filling) and `keeper-wait.png` (4 frames, `ms` a frame, standing with nothing to do) and `keeper-carry.png` (4 frames, advances by distance walked like the walk strip, shown while a keeper walks with food in hand (`c.carry`); the feed crate is drawn in front of the hands by `carryBox` in map.js, in every pose (walk, work, wait, carry) while `c.carry` holds food, so it always looks the same). The figure is mirrored when it walks left. They are drawn about 3.6 m tall (a little over true pixel size so they read) and never under 24 screen px. Guests have their own kinds, `HUMAN_SPRITES.guest`, `guest2` to `guest5` (`guest[2-5]-walk.png` and `-wait.png`, 4 frames each, no work or carry strips; `guestKind(p)` picks one by party id, add a kind to `GUEST_KINDS` for more): `drawParties` draws each party as a pooled `guestEls` sprite (`humanSvg`/`humanStep`, standing frames while still) with a pip under its feet in the personality (or mood) color once a guest is `GUEST_SPRITE_PX` (10) screen px tall, and as the old dots when zoomed further out. Vets are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.vet`, `vet-walk/work/wait/carry.png`, green scrubs; work plays while `checking`, `treating`, `darting` or `splicing`; carry is unused, vets hold nothing). Custodians are pixel people (`HUMAN_SPRITES.custodian`, `custodian-*.png`; work plays while `scrubbing`, `emptying` or `sweeping`, and the carry pose shows the crate in the stock's color). Mechanics are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.mechanic`, `mechanic-*.png`, orange coveralls and hard hat; work plays while `inspecting` or `repairing`, carry is unused). Guards are pixel people too (`HUMAN_SPRITES.guard`, `guard-walk/wait/escort.png`, navy uniform and cap; no carry strip; the escort strip is its `work` strip and plays while `c.job === "escorting"`). Catching a party (a vandal, or one the player called security on) or reaching one that waits (`escortOut`, personas.js) sets `c.escort = p`: the guard stands 1.2 m beside the party (`keeperPos`) until `p.gone`, can't catch or take another meanwhile, and the party walks home unhappy and no longer a vandal. Aquarists (keeper walkers with `role:"aqua"`) use `HUMAN_SPRITES.aquarist`: `aquarist-walk/work/wait/carry.png` (wetsuit, mask and tank; work plays while `mucking` or `filling`, and the carry pose holds the feed crate like a keeper's). Mechanics and vets are covered above.
- Animal size: `animalRadius(sp)` (map.js) is `sqrt(space)/9` held to 1.2..6. The picture is 2.5 radii wide (4 in a vivarium) times the entry's `size` (`rsize` for a rig), so to enlarge one species give it `rsize` and scale its `rstep` by the same factor (the Dimetrodon: 1.6).
- Animal sprites: `sprites/<species id>.png` standing (side-on, facing right, transparent), listed in `SPRITES` in data.js with `ratio` (width over height) and `walk`, the frame count of `sprites/<id>-walk.png` (frames side by side, each the standing picture's size, feet on the same row). `spriteSvg` in map.js draws one in place of the dot (`foot` in the entry is the share of the picture's height where the feet are, for a picture with empty rows under it: the Tiktaalik's is .57, a couple of rows under its feet, so it stands on its spot instead of floating) (open exhibits, once it's at least 6px across); in `animateAnimals` it flips to face the way it walks and `setPose` steps the walk frame by distance walked (`h.stride`), back to standing (or, if the entry also has `idle`, cycling `sprites/<id>-idle.png` via `restPose`; `setPose` frames: -1 stands, 0+ walk, -2 and down idle) while it waits. Dynamic idles (`idleFrame` in map.js; the Tiktaalik): with `base` (frames that loop) and `dyn` (`[[first, count], ...]`, clips that take turns), `dynMs` and `dynGap` ([min, max] seconds), the strip's other frames play once now and then, a small movement, and it settles back. The Tiktaalik's strip is 4 basic frames, a look-around and a push-up, built from its rig by `tools/rigger/tiktaalik/gen-idles.js` (it writes `sprites/tikt.png` and `sprites/tikt-idle.png`; the rig is only used to make the pictures, it isn't in `rigs.js`). Vivarium animals don't walk: they stay on their spot (spaced by `cramped`) and, if the entry has `idle` (frame count of `sprites/<id>-idle.png`, same strip format) and optionally `ms` (per frame), cycle it; with no idle strip they show the standing picture, and with no `SPRITES` entry the dot. Run `tools/packsprites.py` after adding PNGs (see Sprite pack). A species can instead be drawn from a rig (js/rigs.js, `rig:1`), which keeps the strips as the fallback. Every species is drawn from its PNGs (there used to be coded copies of the Coelophysis, Dimetrodon and Tiktaalik in `js/pixelart.js` that the game painted instead; they're gone, so a change to a PNG is what shows). Each has a shading pass over its PNGs: `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py`, `pixelprio_shade.py` (also sways the walk strip's tail), `pixeltikt_shade.py`, `pixelcoel_shade.py`, `pixeldime_shade.py`; all but the Dimetrodon's give the same result when run twice. The Lystrosaurus's strips (`sprites/lyst-walk.png`, `lyst-idle.png`, drawn at `size:.7`, the fallback when rigs are off) are shaded by `tools/pixellyst_shade.py` (smooth leathery hide, no scales: creased thigh and shoulder, wrinkles, sparse grain; it also cuts `lyst.png` from the walk strip, which would overwrite the picture now drawn from the rig, docs/rigs.md). Its 8-frame idle strip (`idle:8`: lowers its head and sniffs the dirt, then plays back to standing) is built by `tools/pixellyst_idle.py` from hand-posed pictures in `tools/lyst-idle/` (cells sampled off their 13.55 px grid, snapped to the palette, shaded with the same rules, the whole animal moved 2 px left so the lowered snout fits); run the shade script first. Cotylorhynchus keeps its own palette and body shape; `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py` adds the flat-style shading and texture to `sprites/coty*.png` in place and redraws its head (`reshape_head`: a small blunt-snouted caseid skull, per frame offset, eye and mouth). Its idle strip is 8 frames (`idle:8` in `SPRITES`): it dips and blows a bubble, the bubble pops, it is surprised, settles and stands (`IDLE_EXPR`, `SPECKS`); the first run grew it from 6 frames. It reads shaded colors back first, so re-running is safe, and it asserts nothing left of the head moved.
- Footprints and tap shapes: a plant takes a SQUARE of whole 1 m cells on the ground, a pictured rock or boulder a few whole-cell rects cut from its silhouette (see below), each standing on its picture's base: the box's front (bottom) edge is at the picture's bottom, `f.y`, and it runs back up the map from there (`footBased`; pictured plants and rocks only, others stay centered; `footSnap` puts `y` on a grid line for them; old saves slide down by half a side once, flagged `state.footBase`, so no box moves; a plant listed in `FOOT.rect` (data.js) has a real base instead of a square: `footW` its base width to the half meter, `footD` `FOOT.depth` of that, snapped to half meters by its edges, so things can stand close in front, behind and beside it and layer by depth; add a plant's key to `FOOT.rect` when its picture is redrawn with a real base, and the rest keep their square) (`FOOT` in data.js: `range` per kind, small 1 to 2 m, medium 2 to 4, large 3 (trees, so a small no-go zone); `plain` is the middle for a picture-less one; `crown` factor; `rockCover`/`rockWide` for rocks; `side` overrides one picture by hand with a square). `SPRITE_HIT` (made by `tools/spritehit.py`, re-run it after changing a plant or rock picture) gives each picture's base and whole width; landscape.js turns that into `LAND[plant].footSide` (a plant's base width, or `FOOT.crown` of its crown if more, rounded and held to the range) and `ROCK_FOOT[type][biome][picture]` = `{w, rects}` (`rockFootOf`: the picture's bands, mirrored so a flipped rock takes the same ground as its ghost, with the 4 px it sinks below its point left out, rasterised on the 1 m grid; a cell is taken when the silhouette covers `FOOT.rockCover` of it, each row runs first to last taken cell, equal rows join into one rect, so a rock is usually 1 or 2 rects, tall ones narrow and tall; `w` is the width in whole cells, odd centred on a cell and even on a grid corner; a rock with no picture to read, or a `FOOT.side` override, gets a square). `footSideFor(key, pic, biome)` is the side (a rock's `w`), `footRects(f, e)` a placed thing's rects `[[x0, y0, x1, y1], ...]` (e: its exhibit, null out in the park; a rock's picture is `f.pic` or the id-based pick, `rockPicIndex`; anything that isn't a pictured rock is one box), `footRectsAt(key, pic, biome, x, y)` a new one's, `footBox`/`footBoxAt` the one box around the rects, and `footSnap` puts a point on the grid so the square covers whole cells (odd sides or widths on a cell center, even on a grid corner; `landSpot` does it, from the raw pointer spot `landPoint`). `rockPicFor(type, biome)` is the picture the next rock gets (the picked one, else a roll made ahead in `rockRoll` and redone after each placement, so the ghost shows and measures what a tap will place; `landSpot` returns it as `pic` for `placeLand`). Everything else in `LAND` (barns, caves, trays, toys) has no `footSide` and takes a box `2 * footR(t)` wide. All ground-space checks use the rects, not a circle, and a thing touching only along an edge is fine; the `rects*` helpers (`rectsHit`, `rectsHitPoly`, `rectsInside`, `rectsHitLine`, `rectsDist`, `rectsOnChunk`) fold these over each rect: `boxesHit` (two boxes), `boxHitsPoly`/`boxInside` (water, buildings, platforms, exhibit outlines; `boxInside` lets the square sit flush against its fence), `lineHitsBox(box, pts, hw)` (fence lines and pieces, old paths with their half width; with no width a line along the edge is allowed, one through the inside is not), `boxPointDist` (gate, entrance), `boxOnChunk` (path chunks); the users are `landProblem`, `decorProblem`, `waterProblem`/`parkWaterProblem`, `shapeHitsLandscape`, `lineHitsLandscape`, `fenceProblem`, `pieceSpotProblem` (fences.js), `pathCtx`/`pathChunkProblem` (pathcells.js) and `reshapeProblem` (map.js). Derived `d` path strips are skipped in `decorProblem` (the chunks are checked directly). Animals walk around them: `walkArea` (map.js) obstacles are rounded boxes (`hx`, `hy` half sides, `c` margin; `obsDist`, `segBoxDist`): a rock keeps off each rect of its footprint, a tree its trunk (about a third of the square), low plants are walked through. The placement ghost draws the footprint on the grid (`footGhostSvg`: a square, or one dashed outline round a rock's rects). `r` still sizes the drawing and the habitat math (`haveOf`, `wantsOf`, `landM2`), so happiness and food don't depend on the footprint. Old saves: `snapFootprints(s)` (landscape.js, last step of `upgradeSave`) snaps every plant and rock to its footprint and, where two now overlap, nudges one up to a cell (preferring spots still inside its fence); it never removes anything and does nothing to a thing already on a square. Bulldozing and tapping use the sprite's silhouette (`spriteHitPath`, `hitShape`, the same bands that cut the rects), not the footprint. Vivarium fittings are unchanged (`f.k`, their own placement).
- Landscape sprites: plants are `sprites/plants/<LAND key>.png` (upright, base at the bottom middle), listed in `PLANT_SPRITES` in data.js (`ratio`, `size`); `plantSpriteSvg` in landscape.js draws one in `featSvg`, flipped by its seed, and plants with no entry keep their SVG. Rocks are `sprites/rocks/<type>-<biome>-<n>.png`, listed in `ROCK_SPRITES` (`[width, height]` per picture, `ROCK_MPP` meters per pixel); `rockSpriteSvg` picks one by the rock's id. They're made by `tools/pixelrocks_flat.py` (every biome but tropical: faceted ellipsoids, ray cast, flat tones per facet) and `tools/pixelrocks_tropical.py`, both with a 1 px near-black outline tinted to the stone, like the plants. Ground is `sprites/ground/<biome>.png` (scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal so far), the tile behind the `#b-<biome>` and `#p-<biome>` patterns in index.html; scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal use one 18 m tile for both (boreal's `color` and `park` are #587260, from `boreal_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: cold conifer-forest floor of brown litter dashes, moss and dark humus patches, thin twigs, flecks and small wet stones, no grass, flowers or modern plants; its park used to be the paler #8FA694 and its exhibit color #5E8070, so boreal parks look darker and browner-green now; tundra's `color` and `park` are #B3BDA3, from `tundra_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: frost-heaved grit with pale and dark flecks, frost cracks, pebbles and tiny lichen specks, no grass, flowers or plants; its park used to be the paler #CBD2C2, so tundra parks look a little darker and greener now; grassland's `color` and `park` are #C3CC76, from `grassland_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: sunlit loam with pale silt, clay and soil flecks, dry-earth dashes and small pebbles, no grass or flowers; its park used to be the paler #B7C995, so grassland parks look a little greener now; temperate's `color` and `park` are #7DAA68, from `temperate_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: loam, moss and humus flecks, no grass), and their `park` color equals their `color` (tropical's is deep forest-floor green #002104, from `tropical_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: dark forest green with brown humus patches, fallen brown leaves, leaf litter, twigs and damp pebbles, no grass or flowers; desert's is dune sand #DFC48A, from `desert_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`: wind ripples, pebbles and sandstone chips; wetland's is the muted marsh green #6F9A7A; the wetland tile adds mud, algae film and small shallow pools, from `wetland_tile` in `tools/pixelground_flat.py`). Water: `WATER_TEX` in landscape.js lists the biomes whose water is a pixel tile (`sprites/water/<biome>.png`, the `#w-<biome>` pattern in index.html, made by `tools/pixelwater_flat.py`, one palette per biome; wetland, scrubland, temperate, desert, tropical, grassland, tundra and boreal so far (boreal: dark cold tannin-tinted forest pool, kept clean (ripples and glints only), moss damp ring, dark peat bank, pale rim; tundra: cold clear glacial blue-grey, kept clean (ripples and glints only), pale frosted damp ring, ice-white rim in place of mud; grassland: clear green-teal river, kept clean like temperate (ripples and glints only), olive damp ring, tan mud bank; tropical: deep blue-green jungle pool, two-tone wash, bright glints, dark leaf-litter mud bank), each with its four bank colors in `WATER_TEX[biome]`, with a stair-stepped shore cut to the pixel grid by `waterMask`: a light shallows rim inside and a wet-mud (scrubland: salt-crust) bank outside, cached by shape); `waterSvg` takes the biome (the exhibit's, or the park's for water outside) and other biomes keep the flat blue. Pictures are generated by `tools/pixelplants.py` (older shaded plants, superseded), `tools/pixelplant_flat.py` (the large Permian plant, in the flat style: the model for new plants) and `tools/pixelplants_flat.py` (the other Permian and Triassic scrubland plants, same style, shared helpers), `tools/pixelplants_scrubland.py` (the other 18 scrubland plants: Devonian and Jurassic to Quaternary, helpers copied in), `tools/pixelrocks.py` (older, superseded), `tools/pixelrocks_tropical.py` (the tropical rocks, seen 3/4 like the rest but not stepped tiers: each stone is m rings of a rounded dome from a lumpy harmonic outline, so it has a vertical front wall, a rounded shoulder and a lit top, flat tone bands from the light, moss only on the shoulder and top, and two-stone clusters; the model for rounder, less repetitive shapes) and `tools/pixelrocks_flat.py` (the scrubland, wetland, temperate, desert, grassland, tundra and boreal rocks, flat style; boreal's are rounded dark rain-wet granite (many sides, small jitter) in plain, moss-capped, rust-stained and pale weathered variants; tundra's are frost-shattered grey slabs stacked in shifted plates, with yellow-green, orange and pale grey lichen-colored caps, a dark slate and a pale granite; grassland is rounded grey fieldstone, a lichen-capped pair, an iron-stained one and pale limestone; desert's banded sandstone passes `render` a list of ramps, one per strata layer) `tools/pixelplants_grassland.py` (the six grassland plants, Neogene and Quaternary only: Bluegrass, Kangaroo Grass, Pampas Grass, Fescue, Grama, Bluestem, plus the Prairie Wildflowers Park Plant) `tools/pixelplants_tundra.py` (the six tundra plants, Neogene and Quaternary only: Cottongrass, Crowberry, Arctic Willow, Saxifrage, Mountain Avens, Dwarf Birch, plus the Labrador Tea Park Plant) `tools/pixelplants_boreal.py` (the 18 boreal plants, Permian onward, plus the Black Spruce and Quaking Aspen Park Plants; common names from the Paleogene: Royal Fern / Birch / Larch, Blueberry / Alder / Spruce, Reindeer Lichen / Marsh Rosemary / Pine) `tools/pixelplants_wetland.py` (all 27 wetland plants, nine periods, plus the six wetland Park Plants `q-bald-cypress` ... `q-cypress-knees`, same helpers copied in) `tools/pixelground.py` (older, superseded) and `tools/pixelground_flat.py` (the scrubland ground, flat style). `tools/pixeldime_shade.py` is the shading pass over `PIXEL_ART.dime`; `tools/pixeldime.py` built the original. Shelters and trays: `sprites/shelter/<LAND key>.png` and `sprites/tray/<key>.png` (made by `tools/pixelshelter.py`), sized in `SHELTER_SPRITES` (`w`, `h` px, `b` the base in radii below the spot) and `TRAY_SPRITES` (`well`: the hollow `[cx, cy, rx, ry]`) in data.js; `shelterSpriteSvg` and `traySpriteSvg` (landscape.js) draw them in `featSvg` before the vector drawings, which stay only for `f.k` items. A shelter stands with its base `b` radii under the spot and is never flipped (a barn's open front faces the viewer); a tray lies flat, and the food is drawn into its well as stepped rows in `FOOD_COLOR`, its area `trayHas / tray` (min 2 px). `spritehit.py` also scans these folders, so they have tap shapes and a `footSide` (the picture's width in whole meters); `snapFootprints` leaves them where old saves put them, and `wallStrips34` (map.js) hides what stands behind a barn's rectangle or a cave's dome. Run `tools/packsprites.py` after adding PNGs (see Sprite pack). Rock shape picker: the player picks a rock or boulder picture in the build menu's Landscaping group (`refreshShapes` in menu.js: a Random button and one `image-rendering:pixelated` thumbnail per `ROCK_SPRITES[biome][type]` picture, for `rockBiomeNow()`: the biome tab, the exhibit under the cursor, or `parkBiome()`; the choice is `rockPick[type]`, null for random, and `rockPicOf` drops it when the biome has fewer pictures). `placeLand(..., pic)` stores it as `f.pic` (saved in `e.land`/`state.decor`; absent on old rocks, which keep the id-based pick in `rockSpriteSvg`, so `upgradeSave` needs no default), and the map ghost draws that picture. Footprints: placement and the ghost ring use the per-type average `LAND[x].fr`, while the tap shape and bulldoze hit use the picked picture's own `SPRITE_HIT` entry (`<type>-<biome>-<n>`).
- Building doors: `building34` and `block34` draw the door, sign and windows on the wall that `b.face` names (`faceEdge`), not the widest wall. A wall the camera can't see (north is hidden, east and west are edge-on) gets `faceMark34` instead: facing north, a cream name plaque stands on the roof's far edge with a dark door tab (gold handle pixel) hung under it; facing east or west, a door column on that side edge and the name plaque on the visible front wall's top band. All flat fills snapped by `flatSnap34`, no outlines. Graffiti stays on the widest visible wall. Carts and tram stops have no door, so they keep the widest wall; the campground and platform have their own fronts.
- Flat buildings (map.js, before `building34`): `FLAT34` palette, `flatSnap34`, `flatWalls34`, `flatWc34`, `flatRoof34`, `FLAT_FONT34`/`FLAT_ICON34` (pixel lettering and pictograms). `building34` uses them for the Genesis restroom only, except that the restroom is now drawn as one picture, `sprites/bld/restroom-<front|back|left|right>.png` (80 x 80 px at `ROCK_MPP`, drawn by `bldSprite34` from its `BLD_PICS` entry), picked by the door wall's outward normal on screen: down is front, up is back, left or right is the side picture with the door at that end; the front and back walls are 61 px (the 8 m door wall), the side pictures' walls 46 px (the turned footprint's 6 m front) centered on column 40, with the eave on row 53 in every view so the wall height doesn't change as it turns; every view has the same see-through shadow (rows 71-74). The Genesis trash bin and bench follow the restroom's style as a template for the next pass at buildings and props (same greens, cream plaster, browns, dark edges and see-through shadow; the palette is shared in `tools/genesis_style.py`): `propSprite34` in `js/propart.js` draws `sprites/bld/<type>-<front|back|left|right>[-full|-broken].png` for every type in `PROP_PICS`, picked by where the front looks (`propFront`), centered across, with the base's bottom (the third row from the bottom, above two shadow rows) a set distance in front of the middle, scaled by the same `k` as the drawn props. The bin (14 x 21 px, `tools/pixelbin.py`): full shows a heap of litter, broken the lid knocked off. The bench (`tools/pixelbench.py`): wooden planks in the base browns on green end frames; front 26 x 16 and back 26 x 12, while its sides (12 x 15) cheat the projection: an end-on bench is drawn as its end profile with a short top, because the true view (the seat and backrest tops as tall strips running back) read as two posts; broken has planks missing. Other themes keep the drawn boxes and the image has `preserveAspectRatio="none"` so it fills its box instead of being letterboxed, and the bottom of the base slab (picture row 71; the 4 shadow rows below it don't count) sits on the footprint's front edge (the flat pieces stay in the file for the sides); every other building and theme still has the older vector drawing. Vivariums are flat too (`viv34`, `vivFloor34`, `VIV34`, `VIV_GLASS34` in map.js, 3/4 view only; the flat top-down view keeps its old glass polygon): `vivFloor34` is the substrate floor (the biome color mixed with soil, rows of dark and light dashes, a lit strip on the far sides), `viv34(e, "back"/"front")` the far panes, posts and sill, and after the contents the near panes (semi-transparent with glare streaks), sill (the solid base), and a clear glass lid. A case with a scene (`e.vivScene`, landscape.md) draws `vivScene34(e, "floor")` in place of the substrate and `vivScene34(e, "wall")` over the back pane from the top of the sill to the top of the glass, before the contents: `sprites/vivscene/<scene>-<S|M|L>-<wall|floor>.png`, `<scene>` being `vivSceneKey` (`<era>-<biome>`, or `<period>-<biome>`). `tools/pixelvivscene.py` makes all of them from shared parts (era palettes and plant shapes, biome ground, sky, tints and floor decals, a stepped hill skyline), sized from each case (its `SIZES` must match `VIVARIUMS` in data.js: change a case size there, then rerun it, `pixelaquascene.py` and `pixelpaluscene.py` and repack; 0.13 m a pixel across; the wall is the pane height above the sill, the floor's rows squashed by TILT), as palette PNGs to keep the sprite pack small; `--sheet` writes a contact sheet. A new scene, size or period is a table entry there, never a hand-drawn picture. The whole case is glass above its base, with a one-pixel see-through light blue border (floor and lid edges, corner edges); no frame, posts or lamp bars. The case is a plain glass box: no corner posts, middle post or lamp bars, so nothing hides the animals. S has a light aluminium base, M dark wood, L dark aluminium. Vivarium animals are placed by `vivSpot` (map.js: the picture's width clears the side walls and posts, the feet stay behind the sill) and re-placed when the case moves. Frame colors are one flat tone a face, selected/doomed show an outline only. Path objects (bin, bench, picnic, lamp, camera, donation box, info sign, Do Not Feed sign) are in the flat pixel style too, in the 3/4 view, in `js/propart.js` (loaded after map.js): `propArt(bl, inv, on, dead)` builds each from a few boxes (`pbox`: a tone a face, left lightest, front base, right darkest, top lightest, snapped by `flatPoly34`; `pbits` paints pictograms like the sign's `i` and the Do Not Feed ring, `PROP_ICONS`), colors from the item's `themeFill` and theme edge, outlined only when selected or doomed, dark red when broken. Each faces along +v of its footprint (`propFront(b)`, read off the points, which are `rectPts` in order, so it needs no saved field): bin opening, bench seat side, picnic long side with the gold plate, lamp lens, camera lens, donation slot, sign face; the other sides differ (backrest, rails, bare grey sign back, lock plate). Rotation: R / the Rotate button turn the item being placed a quarter turn (`rot` in map.js, ghost drawn by `propArt` with an arrow out of its front); at rot 0 it faces the path it stands by; the count is saved as `b.rot` in 45° units, always even (`upgradeSave` defaults old ones to 0 and turns any prop left on a diagonal about its middle to the nearest of north, south, east or west; the Move tool's Rotate bumps it by 2). The flat top-down view keeps its old shapes plus a white front bar (`propFrontMark`). Nothing in the sim reads the facing yet; hooks are commented at `hasSign` (guests.js) and `onCamera` (security.js).

## Art direction (locked)
The Dimetrodon (`sprites/dime*.png`, shaded by `tools/pixeldime_shade.py`) is the reference: flat, simple, cartoony pixel art.
- **Shapes and color:** flat fills, no outlines, stair-stepped silhouettes, readable at game size. About 8 base colors a sprite, each with a lighter and a darker shade of the same hue. Palette family: olive greens (97,111,34), rust orange (178,78,0), yellow-olive and warm browns; buildings use their theme's colors.
- **Detail budget:** flat shapes plus about 30%, all structured: a lit top-left edge, shadow on undersides and the right side, rhythmic texture (rows of short dashes, grooves beside ribs, scutes, a lighter rib line). Never random scatter, noise or heavy dithering; it reads as sandpaper.
- **Light** comes from the top left everywhere.
- **Exhibits are full 2D:** flat ground and upright sprites, y-sorted. Animals are side-on facing right (mirrored by `face`). Don't render animals from 3D models; that was tried, cost a lot, and looked worse than simple hand-made shapes.
- **Buildings are 3/4 and front-facing**, extruded from the footprint by `building34`, so they rotate. New park buildings, props and paths follow the park style below instead (pictures in the Genesis restroom's look); the extruded flat style stays for what hasn't been redone. Flat style: one flat tone per face (left-facing lightest, front the base tone, right-facing darkest), no strokes except for selected, doomed and unreachable states, every point snapped to the pixel grid (`flatSnap34`: `ROCK_MPP` = 0.13 m a pixel, with the vertical step divided by `TILT` so pixels are square after the squash), and pixel-built text and pictograms. Use the game's own names ("Restrooms", never "WC"). Keep the snap's tie-break (the `+ 1e-4`), or exact half-pixel positions round at random and drop columns.
- **Pixel size** is the same everywhere: 0.13 m a pixel.
- **Ground tiles** wrap and repeat, so keep them free of large or distinctive features, and of anything from the wrong era or biome (no grass or sage in Permian scrubland).
- **Check at game zoom and zoomed in.** A blurry screenshot hides bugs; reconstruct the drawn pixels (count them, or print them as ASCII) when something looks off.
- **Status:** in this style so far: the Dimetrodon, the Sacabambaspis (one standing picture drawn in the pixel editor, `size:.55` so a school fits a small aquarium, textured in place by `tools/pixelsaca_shade.py`: lit back, shaded underside, tubercle rows on the head shield, its front edge lit against the seam and the trunk shaded under its rear rim, staggered scale dashes on the trunk, ray streaks on the tail fin, plate rows on the belly; ## Park style (buildings, props, paths): the restroom is the template
Everything guests walk past (buildings, path props, paths) is being redone to match the Genesis restroom (`sprites/bld/restroom-*.png`). Done so far: the restroom, trash bin, bench, guest path, the build menu's vivarium thumbnails (`tools/pixelvivicon.py`, which added the glass colors `e` `k` `g` `n` to `PAL`) and the food cart (`kiosk`, user-drawn in its own blue, 40 x 40 px, base bottom on row 35 front and back, row 37 on the sides (`sideBase`), the right side the left mirrored) and the gift cart (`cart`, the same picture in pink with a GIFT sign, sharing the food cart's `BLD_PICS` entry and footprint). Animals and landscape keep the Dimetrodon direction above; this replaces only its "no outlines" and "one flat tone per face" rules for park things.

**The look**
- **Palette:** only the colors in `tools/genesis_style.py` (`PAL`), one letter each, so every picture shares them. Greens (roofs, lids, metal frames): `A` #122213 dark edge, `L` #3F7C45 light, `H` #305E32 mid, `F` #2B542D fascia. Walls: `a` #E3DDC3 plaster, `Z` #C6BC93 plaster shade, `T` #6A644D soffit, `C` #1C1A10 dark edge, `D` #211A0E dark inside. Browns (bases, wood, curbs): `X` #A57D4A light, `V` #84653B, `W` #8F6D40, `U` #6E5531, `M` #42351D, `G` #2C2313 darkest. Shadow `S` #1D2B22 at alpha 100. Small accents (a sign's blue, litter) are fine; add them to `PAL` with a comment rather than inlining a color. A new material gets its own small set the same way: a dark edge, a light, a mid and a shade.
- **Edges:** every shape has a one-pixel dark edge tinted to its material (dark green round greens, dark brown round plaster and wood), never pure black. Inside the edge, a lit row or column on the top and left, a shade row on the bottom, and a shade column down the right of walls.
- **Texture is structured:** tile courses with staggered joints (roofs, lids), joints every few pixels along a base (`M` in the browns), plank rows with a dark gap, paving slabs with a lit top row and left column. No noise, scatter or dithering. Big areas (a path, a roof) get calmer, larger units: at 1 m the path slabs read as a brick wall under the tilt, at 2 m as paving.
- **Parts that repeat across the set:** a green tiled top (roof, lid), cream plaster body, dark soffit band under the top, brown tiled base slab with a dark bottom row, and the see-through shadow: two rows (a building's four) of `S` under the base, the second row inset a pixel at each end.
- **Light** from the top left, as everywhere. When a picture is mirrored for the other side, keep the shading lit on the left instead of mirroring it.

**Scale and projection (0.13 m a pixel)**
- Across: 1 m = 7.7 px. Up (height): 1 m = 7.7 px. Back (depth, on the ground): 1 m = `TILT` / 0.13 = 5.4 px up the picture. So a 0.9 m deep lid shows about 5 rows of top, and a wall 2 m tall is about 15 rows.
- Draw at true size. A thing that looks too small at true size is usually right (a bin is about 8 px across); don't scale it up.
- A long, low thing seen end-on is the one place to cheat: its true end-on view (the top running back as a tall strip) reads as posts, so draw its end profile with a few rows of top instead (the bench's side views).
- Ground textures are squashed by the tilt on screen, so a square tile shows as a flat oblong.

**Views and facing**
- Everything faces north, south, east or west (no 45° turns). A building or prop that can turn has four pictures, `<name>-front|back|left|right.png`: front is the door or front looking down the screen, back looking up, left and right the front at that end. Make the side views from the turned footprint (an 8 x 6 m building turned is 6 m across), not by reusing the front's width.
- Every building has a footprint whose width and depth are multiples of 5 m (`w` and `d` in `BUILDINGS`, data.js). Path props (`prop:true`: bins, benches, lamps, signs) are exempt. Placing, dragging or turning a building snaps its box to the 5 m grid (`onChunkGrid`, map.js), so it never straddles a path chunk; props, vivariums, platforms and galleries keep their own snapping.
- Keep the walls, eaves and base at the same rows in every view, so nothing jumps when it turns. Center each view on the same column.
- State variants are extra pictures, not tints: `-full` (a bin's heap of litter), `-broken` (lid knocked off, planks missing).

**Picture layout (what the code expects)**
- A prop state with its own size has its own `PROP_PICS` entry, `"<view>-<state>"` (the bin's knocked-over `-broken` pictures, 21 x 21 px); a view with no picture of its own shows the front (`propSprite34`). The bin's `-full` and `-broken` pictures are hand-drawn in the pixel editor, so `tools/pixelbin.py` writes only the plain views. The lamp has one picture, its front, shown from every side (its broken views are the same picture).
- Props: centered across; the bottom of the base is the third row from the bottom, with the two shadow rows under it. `PROP_PICS` in `js/propart.js` lists each type's views as `[width, height, front, row]`, where `front` is how far in front of the footprint's middle (in meters) the base row stands and the optional `row` is that row (default the third from the bottom). `propSprite34` draws them, Genesis theme only; other themes keep the drawn boxes.
- Buildings: one picture per view at a fixed size; `bldSprite34` in map.js draws every type in `BLD_PICS` (size, the row under the base, which views exist, animation frames), anchoring that row on the footprint's front edge. A `card` type (the food and gift carts: flat pictures with no roof depth drawn) may lie `backOver` meters (0.6) over a path at its box's back (north) side (`pathSolid`, `bldOnPath`, pathcells.js), and its back view is drawn that much higher in its box, so a cart backing onto a path meets it. `hit` gives each view's opaque box, drawn as a transparent tap target over the footprint so the whole picture selects it. The restroom is the model: 80 x 80 px, base bottom on row 70, four shadow rows.
- Animation: extra frames are `<name>-<view>-2.png` and so on, the same size. They play at 3 a second, one frame shown at a time (never stacked: the pictures have see-through windows and shadows, so a frame over another ghosts), switched by CSS visibility, not opacity, which would hand the picture to the compositor and blur it (`@keyframes frame<f>of<n>` in index.html; add a set for each new frame count), phased off the clock and the building's id so redraws don't restart them and neighbors don't bob in step. The food cart has two: the vendor shifts behind the counter.
- Paths: a seamless 96 px tile (12.48 m) per kind in `sprites/path/`, made by `tools/pixelpath_flat.py`, plus the edge colors in `PATH_TEX` (`js/pathart.js`). The guest path's edge is a brown curb (`X` lit, `M` dark). Its slabs are `Z` with a lighter top row, a softer shade and a joint mixed between `Z` and `T`: the one place tones step off the palette, because a big area needs gentler steps than a small picture.

**Making one**
1. Write a generator in `tools/` (`tools/pixel<name>.py`) that builds each view from text grids of `PAL` letters, using `put`, `image` and `sheet` from `tools/genesis_style.py`. Copy the layout of `tools/pixelbin.py` or `tools/pixelbench.py`. Never hand-edit the PNGs; change the script and re-run it.
2. Run it with `--sheet <scratch>/sheet.png` and look at the views at 8x side by side: edges closed, shading lit on the left, rows lined up between views.
3. Add the type to `PROP_PICS` (or `BLD_PICS` for a building), run `python3 tools/packsprites.py`, and wrap every path in `spriteUrl(...)`.
4. Look at it in the game at normal zoom and zoomed out, next to the restroom, the bin and the bench, so it reads as part of the set. Place one of each facing and state; a headless screenshot is enough (`chromium`, then build a few items with `rectPts` and `centerOn`).
5. Add a smoke check that the picture follows the facing and the states (see `binSpriteByFacing`), run `node tests/smoke.js`, and add the item to the Status list above and to this section's "Done so far".

## Pixel editor (js/pixeled.js)
An in-game pixel editor for hand-drawn sprites, in place of Piskel. Dev mode only: the HUD's **Pixels** button (`#pixelBtn`, hidden otherwise) runs `openPixelEditor(path?)`, a full-screen `#dlgPixel` built by `pxBuild`. Its state is the global `px` (the open path, frame size `fw`/`fh`, `frames` as RGBA arrays, tool, colors, selection, undo).
- **Pictures are strips.** Open lists every path in `SPRITE_PACK` (no cap, with a match count; the search box remembers its last text); `pxGuess` splits a strip into frames (an animal's `<id>-<x>.png` by the width of its `<id>.png`, `sprites/human/` by 16 px, else one frame), and "Strip of N frames" re-splits it. Saving joins the frames side by side again (`pxPng`), the layout the game reads. New makes a blank picture at a path; Import reads a PNG from disk (Piskel exports included).
- **New art:** New offers the sizes the game expects (`PX_TEMPLATES`: animal standing 100 x 75, its walk and idle strips, keeper or guest strip, plants, a prop view, a building view) and fills in the path. Open has a tab per category (Animals, Plants, Buildings, Rocks; `pxDrawn` sorts the pack's paths into them) listing everything drawn plus what's missing, and an **Undrawn only** checkbox (remembered) that keeps just the missing ones. The undrawn animals are every species still drawn as a dot (`pxNeedArt`, the launch roster first); picking one starts `sprites/<id>.png`, with another animal's standing picture faint underneath for size and feet (the reference, below). Try in park on a species with no picture adds a stand-in `SPRITES[id]` (`tried:1`, with `walk` or `idle` from a strip, and frame 0 as the standing picture if there isn't one), so its animals draw from it at once. Pulling a new species' pictures means adding its `SPRITES` entry in data.js (`ratio:100/75`, `walk`, `idle`, `ms`), and a standing `<id>.png` if only a strip was sent.
- **Plants, buildings, props and rocks** have Needs art tabs too (`pxNeeds`): flora in `LAND` with no `PLANT_SPRITES` entry (sized by its small, medium or large key), every `BUILDINGS` type with no `BLD_PICS` or `PROP_PICS` entry (canvas sized from its footprint at 0.13 m a pixel, plus the restroom's margin; four views, `PX_VIEWS`; vivariums, the platform, tram station and campground are left out, `PX_SKIP_BLD`, as they're drawn with what's inside or from parts), and one more shape for each biome's rock and boulder. Building and prop views show the base row as gold dashes as wide as the footprint in that view (`pxGuideFor`, `px.guideW`: the sides use the turned footprint; `BLD_PICS` base, else 9 rows up; a prop's third row from the bottom). Try in park gives each a stand-in entry marked `tried`: `PLANT_SPRITES` (ratio from the picture) and a `SPRITE_HIT` measured in the browser (`pxHit`, the same numbers as `tools/spritehit.py`); the rock as that biome's nth `ROCK_SPRITES` picture; `BLD_PICS` with the views tried so far (others fall back to the front); a prop's `PROP_PICS` with this view standing in for the ones not drawn and for its broken state. Buildings and props only draw from pictures in the Genesis theme. Pulling them means adding those entries in code (`PLANT_SPRITES` and `ROCK_SPRITES` in data.js, `BLD_PICS` in map.js, `PROP_PICS` in propart.js) and running `python3 -I tools/spritehit.py` for plants and rocks.
- **Plant and rock footprints:** on a plant or rock picture the gold dashed box (`pxBoxType`, `pxBoxDims`, `pxBoxRect`; shown always) is its ground box, standing on the base row (the picture's bottom for a plant, 4 rows up for a rock, which sinks below its point) and running up. With **Adjust footprint** on, drag its sides for the width and its top edge for the depth (`pxBoxHit`, `pxBoxDrag`): half meters for a plant, whole meters for a rock (a rock's several rects become one box). It writes `FOOT.box[picture name] = [width, depth]` live through `setFootBox` (landscape.js, which redoes that plant's `footW`/`footD` or that rock picture's `ROCK_FOOT` entry; placed things keep their spots), at the picture's own scale (a plant's `2 r size / width` meters a pixel, a rock's `ROCK_MPP`). The draft keeps it (`box` in `pxDraft`, restored by `pxOpen`; Revert goes back to `pxBoxOrig`), Send and Send all drafts put `box:{n, w, d}` on the queue row, and `tools/pixeledpull.py` writes it into the `box:{}` line of `FOOT` in data.js and lists a plant's key in `FOOT.rect`. A plant with no `FOOT.box` entry keeps the box the game works out.
- **Footprint:** on a building or prop view the gold guide always shows; ticking **Adjust footprint** (`px.footEdit`, off by default and not remembered) makes it draggable (`pxFootHit`, `pxFootDrag`; the cursor turns to ↔ or ↕; nothing is written over the picture). Its end handles set the footprint, kept centered: the width (`BUILDINGS[k].w`) on front and back, the depth (`.d`) on the sides, at 0.13 m a pixel to the 0.1 m. The line itself sets the base row (`pxSetBase`): a building's `BLD_PICS` `base` (front and back share it) or `sideBase` (the sides), a prop view's optional 4th `PROP_PICS` number (else its third row from the bottom). Both change the game live (placed buildings keep their footprint points but redraw on the new row). The draft keeps them (`foot` and `base` in `pxDraft`, restored by `pxOpen`; Revert goes back to the game's, `pxFootOrig`, `pxBaseOrig`), Send and Send all drafts put `foot:{k,w,d}` and `base` on the queue row, and `tools/pixeledpull.py` writes them into data.js, map.js or propart.js along with the picture.
- **Tools:** pencil and eraser (sizes 1 to 3), fill (Shift: every pixel of that color), line, rectangle and ellipse (Shift: filled), select a box and move it (M), lasso (Q: `pxLassoSel`; the drag is recorded as pixels, joined by pixel lines and closed back to the start, and the selection is that ring plus every pixel whose middle is inside it, held as a `mask` on the selection box, so its edge always follows pixel edges; it's drawn as the pixels while dragging and as a marching outline along their outer edges after, `pxMaskPath`), Copy, Cut, Paste and Select all in the top bar (Ctrl+C/X/V/A; Delete clears, the arrows nudge; a paste floats at the cursor until Enter or a click outside, works across frames, layers and pictures, and keeps a lasso's shape; Copy also puts a PNG on the system clipboard, and a picture copied in another app pastes in, unless it is one of the editor's own copies coming back (`pxSig` fingerprints), when the newest copy made here wins; a mouse-clicked button drops focus so Enter doesn't press it again), shade, color picker (or Alt+click), pan (H; a held Space or the middle button pans with any tool: `pxPans`, handled on the stage so a drag can start off the canvas; the canvas is centered with `margin:auto`, so zoomed in past the stage every edge can still be scrolled to). Mirror draws both halves. Left button draws the first color, right the second. Shade nudges a pixel's lightness a little (`PX_SHADE_STEP`, 3% of the range) lighter (left) or darker (right) per stroke, keeping its hue; it does not jump to palette colors.
- **Palettes:** the picture's own colors (with a count that turns amber past 28, the art direction's budget), the park style `PAL` (`PX_PARK`, copied from `tools/genesis_style.py`: keep the two in step) and the Dimetrodon reference. Double-click a swatch to change that color everywhere.
- **Layers** run through every frame: `px.frames[frame][layer]` holds the pixels, `px.layers` each layer's name, `show` and `alpha`, `px.lay` the one being drawn on (`pxFrame()`). Add and Copy go above the current layer; Delete, move up and down, Merge down (paints it onto the one under it at its opacity), rename in place, the eye shows or hides it (a hidden layer can't be drawn on), and a slider sets its opacity. `pxComp(i)` stacks frame i's visible layers; everything that shows or leaves the editor uses it (the canvas, onion skin, preview, thumbnails, the picker, Try in park, Send, Download, `pxHit`), so the game always gets the flattened strip. Flips work on every layer; Shift+arrows nudge only the current one. Import of a picture the size of the open strip adds it as a new layer (to trace or combine); any other size replaces the picture.
- **To and from the sprite rigger:** Send to rigger (`pxToRigger`, `pxRigFile`) saves `<name>-parts.json`, a rigger project with this frame's layers as parts (empty layers skipped): each a full-cell picture with the part where it is drawn, named after its layer, its joint in the middle of its pixels, bottom layer first (the rigger's draw order), the ground line under the lowest pixel. In the rigger, Load project starts a rig from it, and Update parts gives an existing rig's parts the new pictures by name and keeps the animation (docs/rigs.md). Import takes a rigger project (`.json`, `pxFromRigger`): each part becomes a layer, drawn where it stands in the rest pose (flipped parts mirrored about their joint), so the parts of a rig can be touched up here and sent back. Draw an animal for rigging with one body part a layer (head, neck, body, each leg segment, tail pieces), in flat base colors: shading goes on after animating (see the rigger's workflow).
- **Reference:** a guide picture in its own row of the Layers panel, never part of the picture or anything sent (`pxDrawRef`, outside `pxComp`). Upload… takes any picture (kept at most 1536 px on its long side and shrunk in halving steps; PNG, or white-backed JPEG if big, `pxRefUpload`) and fits it whole inside the canvas, scaled to the tighter of width and height and standing on the bottom middle (`pxRefBox`), drawn smooth; or pick an animal that has art, drawn at its own size, pixel sharp. It has its own eye, an opacity slider and "over the layers" for tracing. **Drag the reference** makes canvas drags slide it (half-pixel steps, `px.refX/refY`); the size slider scales it about its centre (`px.refK`); Reset goes back to the bottom middle at full size. The draft keeps all of it (`ref` in `pxDraft`). **Match pixels** (`pxRefMatch`) finds a blown-up pixel-art reference's block size and grid start (`pxRefGrid`: the biggest size up to 32 that 85% of same-color runs are a multiple of) and scales and shifts it so each block lands on one canvas pixel. **To layer** (`pxRefToLayer`) samples the reference as it sits into a new top layer on the current frame ("From reference", undoable): each pixel takes the biggest pool of near colors under it (transparent if clear wins), then colors within 14 levels merge into the most used and the closest pairs merge down to 32; with the park or Dimetrodon palette picked, every color snaps to that palette instead.
- **Frames:** add, copy, delete, reorder, onion skin (the previous frame faded), an animated preview at the set speed and at game size.
- **Save** (top bar, or Ctrl+S; `pxSaveNow`) saves the draft at once and says so; drafts also save themselves 600 ms after each change. A picture that would go out blank (`pxEmptyWhy`: no frame shows a pixel, naming the hidden layers that hold some) gets a warning on Save, isn't sent by Send, and is left out of Send all drafts (`pxPngInked`), which lists it.
- **Drafts:** every change is kept in localStorage (`genesis-pixel-d:<path>` holds each layer as its own strip, `pxDraft`; `genesis-pixel-drafts`, `genesis-pixel-last`), so closing or reloading loses nothing; Revert drops the draft. In Open, a card with a draft has a × in its corner (`pxDelDraft`, after a confirm) that deletes the draft for good; on the open picture it goes back to the game's copy, or to an empty editor if the game has none.
- **Seeing the tools:** the tool buttons are 46 px with heavier icons; the canvas cursor (`pxCursor`) is a crosshair with a white halo, its hot spot on the pixel being drawn, with the tool's icon beside it; the hovered pixel gets a white and black ring drawn just outside it; the checkerboard is kept pale so all of these read against it.
- **Try in park** puts the picture in the running game until reload (`spriteUrl.swap`, written by `tools/packsprites.py`). A new size or frame count still needs its `SPRITES` (or `BLD_PICS`, `PROP_PICS`) entry changed in code. The note under the button (and the toast, which `ui.toast` moves inside an open modal dialog so it shows over the editor) says so; for a building or prop picture `pxTryWhere` adds which facing, state and theme shows it, since a placed prop only draws the one view it faces.
- **Send to repo** writes `{path, png, w, h, frames, fps, at}` to the game artifact's db at `pixeled/<path with / as ~>` (shared, so anyone with edit access sees the queue in the editor). **Send all drafts** (`pxSendAll`) sends every picture whose draft is newer than its queue row and doesn't look just like the game's picture (`pxSameNow`, cached per draft save in `pxGameEq`: opening a picture or toggling a layer saves a draft too), flattening each draft's layers (`pxDraftPng`); its label shows the count (`pxUnsent`). To pull them: read the collection with ArtifactData (`list`, collection `pixeled`) on the game artifact, save the result to a scratch JSON, run `python3 -I tools/pixeledpull.py <that json>` (it writes only `sprites/**.png` paths, only real PNGs of the row's size; `--dry` to look first), then `python3 tools/packsprites.py`, look at the pictures, commit, and mark the rows you pulled: `update` each with `pulled` (the time) and `png` deleted. Don't delete them: a draft with no newer row counts as unsent, so Send all drafts would send it again. Pulled rows count as sent but leave the editor's waiting list. Open lists drafts too, including ones of pictures the game doesn't have yet (Try in park's stand-in views are not drafts: they last until reload). **Download** saves the PNG through the `downloads` capability (or a plain link off claude.ai).
- Hand-drawn pictures skip the generator scripts, so a sprite that has one (a park-style prop, a shaded animal) will be overwritten the next time its script runs: port the change into the script or retire the script for that picture.

## Sprite pack (read before adding any sprite)
The artifact holds at most 511 files, so the ~480 PNGs are NOT published one by one. `tools/packsprites.py` packs every `sprites/**/*.png` into `js/spritepack.js` (data URIs, generated: never edit it), and every drawn picture goes through `spriteUrl(path)` (defined there; a short `blob:` URL when blobs load, else the data URI, with the path kept as a `#fragment`). Index.html's ground and water tiles use `data-sp` for the same reason. **After adding or changing any PNG, run `python3 tools/packsprites.py`, commit the new `js/spritepack.js`, and publish it.** New code that puts a sprite path in an `<image href>` or `<img src>` must wrap it in `spriteUrl(...)`. Keep PNGs in git as the source; only the pack is published. Merge conflicts in `js/spritepack.js`: take either side and re-run the tool.

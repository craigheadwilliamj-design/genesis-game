# PALEO-TYCOON (repo: Genesis Park Tycoon)

Browser park-builder game (Jurassic Park style). Plain JS, no build step, no modules: every file is a classic `<script>` sharing one global scope. Published as a claude.ai Artifact.

## Files (script load order, from index.html; pathcells.js follows fences.js, menu.js and rigs.js sit just before map.js)
- `js/data.js`: all tuning constants and content (`SPECIES`, `BUILDINGS`, `BARRIERS`, `TECH`, `GOALS`, `KEEPER`, `MAINT`, `POWER`, `ESCAPE`, `HEALTH`, `MEDICINE`, `VET`, ...). Balance changes go here.
- Calendar: `state.day` is the counter everything uses; `CALENDAR` (data.js) and `dateOf`/`fmtDate(day)` (sim.js) turn it into "3 Mar Y2" for every player-facing date (HUD, history, toasts, game over). Nothing is saved for it.
- `js/spritepack.js` (generated by `tools/packsprites.py`, loads first): `SPRITE_PACK` and `spriteUrl(path)`; see Sprite pack.
- `js/geometry.js`: pure shape math (`area`, `perimeter`, `inPoly`, `segProj`, `shapesOverlap`, ...).
- `js/sim.js`: the core loop `tick(dtMin)` → `endDay()`, plus guest demand, money (`spend`/`earn`/`canAfford`), happiness (`exhibitReport`), rating, species pull on guests (`speciesDraw`: appeal stretched by `GUEST.drawPower` around `GUEST.drawPivot`, so showstoppers dominate and low-appeal animals draw little), goals, and save migration (`newPark`, `upgradeSave`). The park's own biome is `state.biome` (`parkBiome()`, picked on the start screen's New Park form (`#dlgStart`, main.js `biomeCards`/`pickBiome`/`showStart`) and locked for the park's life: the park office only shows it, and old saves keep theirs): the ground is drawn in `BIOMES[x].park` with the `#p-<biome>` pattern, and new exhibits start on it. Also land for sale (`PARCELS` in data.js, `state.parcels`, `ownsParcel`, `parcelPrice`, `buyParcel`, `ownedRects`/`ownedEdges`/`inOwned`; the Buy Land tool `parcels` and `insidePlot` are in map.js, and `state.boundary` is just the home plot now). The entrance (`state.gate`) is on the park's south edge, so land only grows east, west and north: `PARCELS.ys` ends at the entrance line (`SOUTH_LINE` in sim.js), nothing south is listed, drawn as for sale (`SALE_CELLS` is what the overlay, tap and menu use) or buyable (`parcelSouth`, `parcelProblem`). The south edge is a hard wall drawn in `render` (map.js, open at the gate), and since nothing south is ever owned, `insidePlot` rejects every build, path, fence, water and decor past it; `clampView` stops the camera scrolling far past the land. At most `MAX_PARCELS` (15) plots can be bought (`parcelsBought`, shown as "x of 15" in the Buy Land bar). `LEGACY_SOUTH_YS` (data.js) are cell rows south of the line that exist only so old saves that bought them keep that land (`PARCEL_CELLS` includes them, `SALE_CELLS` doesn't); `upgradeSave` never removes owned parcels. Also shared helpers: `uid`, `dept`/`hasDept` (is a science building up), `money`, `fmtArea`, `fmtClock`, `SPECIES_BY_ID`.
- Dev-mode: `devMode()` (sim.js) reads localStorage `genesis-dev` (off by default, never in `state`); click the HUD version label (`#verLabel`) 5 times in a row to toggle it (main.js).
- `js/science.js`: ORACLE/GHOST/TAR/CERES logic, loaded right after sim.js. Every scientist is one bay holding one job or none (`BAY_JOBS`, `bayCount`, `freeBay`, `fireScientist`; each job carries its `bay`), so hiring is the only way the work lists grow; `baysHtml` in scienceui.js draws them in a fixed order. Everything is timed in park minutes (`nowMin()`, jobs carry `start`/`end`) and finishes mid-day in `scienceTick(dtMin)`, not at the rollover. `tick(dtMin)` (sim.js) cuts a step at the daily rollover (the rest carries into the next day) and at `scienceNext()`, the next job end, so a job completes the minute its `end` passes whatever the step size; `tickStep` is the single step. ORACLE projects (`startProject`, kinds `tech`/`refine`/`species`, one per researcher; a researcher with no project is on general research and earns `RESEARCH_PER_PALEO` points a day, `stopProject` puts one back, and guests add `RESEARCH_GUEST` points times what they learned as they leave in guests.js; species unlocks get dearer by `UNLOCK_GROWTH` per animal already unlocked, and a project remembers its `cost` for refunds), GHOST trips (`tripOdds`, `launchTrip`, `tripReturns`; a genome takes 2 to 8 trips by size), TAR clones (`orderClone`, upgrades `fast1/2`), and CERES growing beds (`growBatch`: landscape plants by period and size into `ceres.pots` (keys like `Jurassic-large`), medicine batches; plant DNA is one genome per Mesozoic/Paleozoic period, `PLANT_DNA[period]`, which GHOST collects once ORACLE has researched the era's flora). CERES's daily Paleoflora food output is NOT here: it's in keepers.js.
- Genome completeness is the only genome stat (`sc.dna[id] = {genome}`; there is no separate quality, and `upgradeSave` drops old `quality` fields and refunds the removed GHOST quality upgrades `ghostq1/2`). TAR clones once a genome is `CLONE_GENOME.min` (60) % complete (`cloneOpen`), and the clone's DNA quality `a.q` (`cloneQuality`, `CLONE_GENOME.q0` at the minimum up to 100 when whole) sets how healthy it is (sickly under 70, frail under 50, see `HEALTH`). Genome therapy lifts a clone toward `cloneQuality` of the lab's current genome. A partial genome can also fail in the incubator (`cloneFailChance`: `CLONE_GENOME.failMax` at the minimum, 0 at 100%, never in sandbox): `orderClone` rolls it and gives the job `fail` (the minute it dies) and `why`; `scienceTick` calls `failClone`, which sets `failed`/`stop` (progress freezes there, `cloneProgress`), keeps the bay and the fee spent, and the job sits until `discardClone` (the Discard button; `scienceNext` and the completion filter skip failed jobs). `cloneCard` (scienceui.js) is the progress card in the TAR panel and the exhibit's Cloning tab, with a red CLONE FAILED overlay when failed; `cloneLive` (main.js frame loop) moves the bars without rebuilding the panel.
- `js/keepers.js`: keepers (`hireKeeper`, `allStaffList`), food (`dailyNeed`, `shortages`, `eatTick`), CERES Paleoflora food output (`ceresTick`, `ceresRate`, `ceresCap`, `greenhouses`, `paleofloraReady`), cleaning, animal transfers (`requestMove`, `keeperMoveProblem`), depots and ATVs (`depots`, `syncAtvs`, `onAtv`), and the staff path graph (`buildKeeperGraph`, `walkFrom` Dijkstra). Call keeper / vet / mechanic (buttons in the exhibit panel's Keepers and food section; `CALLS` table, `callKeeper(e, kind)`, `state.staff.calls` {ex, keeper, kind}): the nearest free worker of that kind drops their job at the top of their tick loop (`takeCall`, `takeVetCall`, `takeMechCall`), unclaimed calls go to the next free one (`callJob`), and on arrival (`toCall`) a keeper hands over carried food, sets `c.focusEx` so `decide` feeds that exhibit first, and mucks it out if dirty; a vet runs a check-up then treats that exhibit's sick animals first (`focusEx`, `pickPatient(c, only)`); a mechanic inspects the fence and repairs it (maintenance.js). Escapes still come first. Food trays (`LAND` items with `tray`, `trays`, `trayRoomOf`, `trayShare`, `startFilling`/`fillStep`): a tray holds one food at a time, optionally fixed with `f.set` (picked in the exhibit's Habitat Props, Food Trays sub-tab, `propsTabs`); keepers with leftover food walk in and fill them, and `eatTick` drains trays before gate stock.
- `js/escapes.js`: barrier strength, breakouts, loose animals, guest deaths, viewing platforms (`viewFactor`), barrier upgrade costs (`upgradeCost`, `moatCost`, `aviaryCost`). Also home to a few shared helpers: `hasTech`, `isFlyer`, `nearestNode`.
- `js/power.js`: generators and electric-fence power allocation (`updatePower`).
- `js/maintenance.js`: fence/generator/depot wear, plus mechanics who inspect and repair.
- `js/medicine.js`: sickness and injuries (`illChance`, `injuryChance`, `healthNight`), the PMC ward (`admit`, `discharge`, `pmcRemoved`), vets who dart sick and escaped animals (`vetsTick`), and which medicine cures what (`canTreat`: the era's medicine plus a `ref-<Period>` refinement from ORACLE). CERES makes the doses in batches (science.js). Genome therapy (ORACLE tech `genetherapy`, `GENE` in data.js): the player queues a sickly clone (`a.gene`) from the exhibit's Health section, and a free vet (`pickGene`, jobs `toGene`/`splicing`, `geneDone`) lifts its `a.q` toward what the lab's genome gives a clone today (`labQuality` = `cloneQuality`, which GHOST raises by finding more of the genome), at most `GENE.step` a go, for `geneCost`. Also trash thrown into exhibits: `throwTick` (called from the guest loop in guests.js) lets rowdy parties, and others holding trash, throw it over an open exhibit's fence (`THROWN` in data.js; cut by working bins, guards via `guardNear`, and a `nofeed` sign within `THROWN.signReach`, `hasNoFeed`). `e.trash` counts pieces for the day, and `trashNight` (in `healthNight`) makes animals eat it: ill or dead (`trashEaten`).
- `js/landscape.js`: water, rocks, groves and shelters inside open exhibits. Water is laid as chunk boxes (`e.water`, `WATER`, `waterProblem`/`waterHost`/`addWater`; the `water` tool in map.js drags a box in whole 5 m chunks like paths: `wdrag`, `waterBox`, `endWaterDrag`; each box is a 4-corner rectangle, `rectOfPts`/`waterHit` treat edge-touching rectangles as not overlapping, and `mergeWater` joins a new one to a neighbour of the same width or height; old corner-drawn water still works and the Move tool still reshapes any water), and old saves' round ponds become drawn water in `upgradeSave`. The rest are placed with a tap (`e.land`, `LAND`, `HAB`, `likesOf` in data.js). `habitatOf`/`habitatScore` feed `exhibitReport`, and each species wants its own water share, rocks and plants (`wantsOf` vs `haveOf`, `speciesFit`; `likesOf`, `BIOMES[x].rock`/`plants`, `HAB.waterMax`; too much water counts against it too), `thirsty` feeds `illChance`. Groves (`LAND[x].flora`) from an animal's era add happiness, `browseShare` cuts what `eatTick` drains, and `grassShare(e, s)` (0 to 1: Cenozoic plants as a share of the exhibit's plants, a grassland biome, or grass hay in place of Paleoflora) is how much of an old grazer's food is grass it never evolved for: it feeds `grassSick` (illness, `GRASS_INTOLERANT`) and the happiness hit in `exhibitReport`. Landscape plants are generated from `PLANT_TABLE` in data.js: one small, medium and large per period and biome (keys like `jur-tropical-small`, `PLANTS_OF[period][biome]`), so a biome that didn't exist in a period has none (no grassland before the Neogene). They carry `period` and `biome` and `landProblem` only lets them go in an exhibit of that biome. An animal counts plants from its own period in full toward what it wants, and other periods' plants of the exhibit's biome up to `HAB.offEra` (a third) of what it wants (`haveOf(e, s)`, `plantSuits`), and wants none where its period had no plants in the exhibit's biome (`wantsOf`). The build menu has a dropdown per period and the exhibit panel lists the biome's plants by period. Each Mesozoic or Paleozoic plant uses one plant of its period and size (`LAND[x].size`) from `state.ceres.pots`, which CERES grows in batches (`CERES_GROW.plant`). Biomes (`BIOMES`, `SPECIES_BIOMES`/`biomesOf` in data.js): each open exhibit has `e.biome` (`biomeOf` falls back to `DEFAULT_BIOME`), and each open-exhibit species has a home biome and one it gets by in. `biomeFit` feeds `habitatScore` (`BIOME_HAPPY`), wetland adds to `wet` in `habitatOf`, and `regradeProblem`/`regradeCost` back the panel's Biome picker. map.js fills open exhibits with the biome color and its `#b-<biome>` pattern from index.html (vivariums keep the period tint; animal dots stay period colors). Weather (`state.weather` {today, next, from}, `WEATHER` in data.js) is rolled each night by `rollWeather` in `endDay`, after the night functions so today's weather counts for tonight's illness. `exposure(e)`, the share of the herd without cover from shelters (`LAND[x].slots`) or grove shade, feeds happiness, `illChance`, `injuryChance` (storms) and `wearPerDay`, and `weatherNow().guests` scales demand. `landSpot`/`placeLand`/`landSvg` do placement and drawing. The tools (`land-rock`, `land-cycads`, ...) are handled in map.js (`landHover`/`landTap`), and water and land features move, reshape-check and bulldoze with their exhibit.
  Park landscaping (outside exhibits) lives here too. `landSpot` falls back to `decorProblem` when the point isn't in an open exhibit, and `placeLand(null, ...)` puts the item in `state.decor`. Water drawn outside exhibits goes in `state.water` (`waterProblem(pts, null)` → `parkWaterProblem`, `addWater(null, pts)`), and old open fence lines in `state.fences` (`fenceProblem`, `addFenceLine`, `fenceLineCost` in sim.js; kind `"fence"` in map.js for select/move/bulldoze; nothing builds new ones now, the grid fence in fences.js does). `parkFences()` is both those and the loose grid fence, and is what paths, buildings, water, decor and the guests' hedge cheer check. New paths, buildings and exhibits must keep clear of all of it: `shapeHitsLandscape(pts)` and `lineHitsLandscape(pts, hw, bridge)`; only a path with `type:"bridge"` (`isBridge`, `BRIDGE` in data.js) crosses park water. Named modern plants (`PARK_PLANTS` → `LAND["q-<name>"]`, `PARK_PLANTS_OF[biome]`) are Quaternary plants usable anywhere, with `look`/`accent` for drawing (`plantSvg`) and `wet`/`aquatic` for water. Other periods' plants need the ORACLE tech `sterile` to go outside exhibits. Statues (`STATUES` → `LAND["st-<id>"]`, `statue` field) are dev-mode only for v1.0 (`statuesOn()` in data.js = `devMode()`: with it off the Statues dropdown is hidden, `statueOpen` is false so `decorProblem` refuses them, and `grantStatue(g)` is null so grants and the award toast never mention or unlock one; statues already in a save keep loading, drawing and cheering guests) and go outside exhibits only; a few are `free`, the rest unlock with a grant's `statue` field (`statueOpen`, `statueGrant`, shown by `grantPrize`). A statue's plinth uses its theme's path color (`statueSvg`). Guests: `indexDecor(nodes)` (called from `buildGuestGraph`) marks each guest-path node with `n.decor` (0 to 1, `DECOR`) and `n.statues`; `guestsTick` turns decor into mood and the "pretty" thought, and `statueSeen` gives learning (people's statues teach far more). Drawing: `waterSvg`, `featSvg`, `parkWaterSvg`, `decorSvg`, `fenceLineSvg`, `hedgeSvg` (hedge rows, `BARRIERS.hedge`, are drawn as real hedges in exhibits too).
- `js/fences.js`: fence laid piece by piece on the 1 m grid, Zoo Tycoon style, loaded after landscape.js. Pieces live in `state.fenceGrid`, keyed `"x,y,d"` (d is `h`, `v`, or the diagonals `d`/`a` of the square at x,y; `pieceKey`, `pieceEnds`, `pieceBetween`, `pieceLen`), each `{b: barrier}` (empty means wood). A drag lays a straight or 45° run (`pieceLine`); `planPieces` checks it (`pieceSpotProblem` per piece: no crossing diagonals, paths, buildings, water, decor, old polygon exhibits, or anything inside a grid exhibit; `faceProblem` per region it closes: nothing fenced in, no pen floating inside another) and prices it (pieces plus `COST.landPerSqM` on new exhibit ground, `newGround`); `layPieces`/`liftPieces` do it. `fenceFaces` finds every closed loop (planar face walk over the pieces, dead ends trimmed; `straighten` keeps a corner wherever the fence kind changes, so each side of `e.points` is one kind, `sideKinds`). `syncFenceExhibits` runs after every change and matches loops to grid exhibits (`e.grid`) by shared ground (`faceSamples`, `FENCE_GRID.keepShare`): a loop nobody takes is just a pen (`penFaces`) until the Gates tool puts a gate on its fence, which buys the ground and makes it an exhibit (`adoptPen`, `newGridExhibit`, `penCost` in map.js); an exhibit whose ground went to another merges into it (`mergeExhibit`: animals, land, water, platforms), and one with no loop left gets `e.open` (it stays an exhibit; only taking out its gate, via Bulldoze on the gate, ends it: `removeItem("exhibit", e, {keepFence:true})` sells the contents and leaves the fence, while bulldozing the exhibit itself lifts its fence too) (a gap: `isBreached`, `effectiveStrength` 0, `escapeRisk` says so) and keeps its last shape until it's closed again; `rehomeContents` moves or sells plants and water left outside. `e.barrier` is the weakest piece (`weakestOf`); the panel's wall picker calls `refenceExhibit`. Drawing: `gridFenceSvg` (a closed exhibit's sides), `looseRuns`/`fenceRunSvg` (everything else, including an open exhibit's pieces), and `fence34` takes a kind per side. Bulldozing a grid exhibit lifts the fence no other exhibit shares (`ownPieces`, priced in `refundFor`); grid exhibits can't be moved or reshaped. Decoration fences (`BARRIERS[x].deco`: hedge and rope) can't hold animals: `planPieces` won't let them replace an exhibit's wall, `penProblem` stops a gate turning a pen of them into an exhibit (`findGateSpot`), and the panel's wall picker only lists them for an old exhibit that already has one. Vivariums and exhibits from older saves are still polygons with one barrier and keep working as before.
  Two fences meeting: a wall two pens share can hold a different fence on each side. A piece is `{b, b2}`: `b` is the side an exhibit sees when its loop runs the piece from its first end to its second (`pieceEnds(k)[0]` to `[1]`, `stepFwd`), `b2` the other side, and no `b2` means one fence for both (`pieceSplit`, `pieceKind(p, fwd)`, `pieceKinds`, `mkPiece`, `pieceWorth` for refunds). `loopSteps(pts)` is a loop's pieces with the way it runs each, and everything that read a loop's pieces now reads its own side: `sideKinds`, `weakestOf(pts)` (so `e.barrier` is the weakest fence on the exhibit's own side), `penProblem`/`decoSteps`, `claimedSides` (`"k|1"`/`"k|0"`), `refenceExhibit` (the panel's wall picker changes the exhibit's side only), `releaseSides`/`ownSides` (bulldozing a pen takes its side of a shared wall and leaves the neighbor's), and `straighten` (a side is one kind, all shared or all not, so a corner stays where either changes). `faceDirs()` says which pieces two pens share (bits 1 and 2). `planPieces(keys, barrier, o)` takes `o.dir` (the run's direction) and `o.side` (1/-1: which side of the run the pointer is on, 0 within `FENCE_GRID.sideDead` of the wall) and, on a shared or already split wall, changes only the pointer's side (`fenceOpts` in map.js reads the live pointer; the ghost is nudged to that side); `o.meet` (the fence bar's "Over fence" button, `fenceMeet`) leaves other fence where it stands and lays only on free edges, so the new fence meets the old. A split wall is drawn as two walls each nudged `FENCE_GRID.sideGap` into its own pen (`sideOffsets` into `fence34`'s `o.off`; `looseRuns` runs carry `off` for a split piece whose other side is still loose). Pieces and runs of different kinds along one line meet at a post as before.
  Bordering paths and water: a piece may run along the edge of a path chunk or a water box, never through it. `pieceOnPath(k)` checks the chunk grid (a piece inside a path chunk, or on an edge two path chunks share, is refused; the derived `d` strips in `state.paths` are skipped), water uses `lineThroughShape` (geometry.js: crosses the shore or its middle is inside, touching is fine), and `faceProblem` tests chunk centers so a pen can close right up against them. Park water, plants, rocks and statues a pen closes around are not an error: `absorbPark(e)` (adoptPen and every `syncFenceExhibits`) moves them into the exhibit's `e.water` and `e.land`, silently (`waterProbe` finds points really inside a body of water). Water inside an exhibit may sit flush against its fence (`WATER.margin` is 0; `insideFence` lets a shore touch the fence but not poke out).
- `js/pathart.js`: the path layer (P4), loaded just before map.js. It reads ONLY `pathSquares()` (axis-aligned squares in meters), never `state.pathChunks`, so the chunk model can change under it. `pathMask(squares)` cuts the union of the squares at the pixel grid (`ROCK_MPP`, like `waterMask`; square edges are rounded the same way for neighbours so touching squares share pixels with no gap): per kind a body, a lit top row and left column, a dark bottom row and right column (cut against the SAME kind only, so where a guest and a staff path or a bridge touch each shows its own edge, a two pixel seam), plus a `PATH_CAST` shadow pixel on bare ground below and right. Runs of equal rows are merged into tall rects. `pathLayerSvg()` returns the whole layer as one `<g id="pathLayer">` (flat kind color, then the `#pt-<kind>` texture pattern, then the edges), cached until `pathVer`, the chunk count or the chunks object changes (`pathLayer.builds` counts rebuilds). `render` (map.js) puts it first in the path pass, before tram track, exhibits, fences, props, plants, buildings and every walker, so anything standing on or beside a path draws whole over it. Textures: `tools/pixelpath_flat.py` writes `sprites/path/guest.png` (plain flat concrete with a few faint dashes, no slabs or joints), `staff.png` (asphalt with gravel dashes and pour seams) and `bridge.png` (planks), each 96 px = 12.48 m at 0.13 m a pixel, patterns `#pt-g/s/b` in index.html via `data-sp`; edge colors are `PATH_TEX`. Path chunks carry no theme, so every theme shows the same paths.
- `js/pathcells.js`: paths as 5 m chunks (P1b of the paths rework, plan in PATHS_PLAN.md; P3 to P6 are still to do). A path is a 5 m x 5 m chunk in `state.pathChunks`, keyed `"cx,cy"` (`chunkKey`; chunk cx,cy is the square from 5cx,5cy to 5cx+5,5cy+5, `CHUNK` = 5, `chunkAt(wx, wy)` gives the chunk of a point) with a kind: `g` guest, `s` staff, `b` wooden bridge (`PATH_CELLS` in data.js: label, color and the per-meter `perMeter`/`upkeepPerMeter` rates; a chunk is 5 m of path so it costs `pathChunkCost(k)` = rate x 5, upkeep `pathChunkUpkeep`). Side-by-side chunks merge into a wider path: there is no wide path and no per-path object. `pathKind(cx, cy)`, `pathAt(wx, wy)`, and `pathSquares()` (every chunk as `{cx, cy, x, y, w, h, kind, fixed}`, the helper the renderer reads). `state.pathFixed` is a set of chunk keys (the entrance walk, `mainWalkChunks`: chunk column 41, x 205 to 210, rows 47 to 60, y 235 to 305) that Bulldoze refuses. The entrance is on the chunk grid: `state.gate` is `[ENTRANCE_X, 305]` (`ENTRANCE_X` = 207.5, the middle of chunk column 41; sim.js), so the entrance building, its walk and the south wall opening are centered on one chunk; all parcel borders (`PARCELS.xs/ys`, `PARK_PLOT`, `LEGACY_SOUTH_YS`) are multiples of 5 so a chunk never straddles a plot edge. The path tools `path`/`service`/`bridge` (`PATH_TOOLS` in map.js maps tool to kind) are drag-and-drop like the fence tool: `pathRun(a, b)` is the straight or 45 degree run of chunks from chunk a toward chunk b (a diagonal adds the chunk beside each step, so the stair stays edge to edge and is two chunks wide), `planPath(a, b, kind)` returns {chunks (the whole run), todo (chunks that change), bad (key to reason), cost, why}, `layPath` spends and writes. Per chunk (`pathChunkProblem`, plus `fenceInChunk`/`fenceOnChunkEdge`): owned land only (`inOwned`), not through an exhibit, a building (path props with `onPath` are fine), park water (a bridge crosses it), plants, rocks or statues, or fence (a diagonal piece or a straight piece inside the chunk, or a straight piece on an edge it shares with another path chunk, old open fence lines too). A staff path never replaces a guest path or bridge; a guest path or bridge replaces staff chunks; the entrance walk is never replaced. Paths may start at the entrance. The map side (`cdrag`, `pathBar`, `pathDragStat`, `pathGhostSvg`, `endPathDrag`, `chunkAtEv`, hover `chover` in map.js): the ghost is the chunks the run will lay at true size (green lays, red is refused, grey is already there; the bar shows the first reason), releasing buys it, a tap or zero-length drag lays one chunk. Drag-box bulldoze (`fdrag.box`, started in Bulldoze on bare ground, shared with the fence lift and paint erase): `chunksInBox(a, b)` is every chunk the box overlaps (a tap takes the one under the pointer), `bulldozeChunkKeys` drops the entrance walk, `renderOverlay` lights exactly those red as the box grows, and `bulldozeChunks` (from `endFenceDrag`) removes them with `removeChunks` in one refund (`COST.refundShare`) and one toast ("Removed 6 path chunks, refunded $..."); fence pieces in the same box still go through their usual confirm, and a box with no path or fence falls through to paint erase. Chunks are drawn by `pathLayerSvg` (js/pathart.js, see its entry), under tram track and legacy paths. `pathVer` must go up whenever chunks change by hand. Migration (`upgradeSave`): saves with 1 m `pathCells` or `crossingsV` become chunks via `chunksFromCells` (a chunk exists where more than half of its area was path, or at least a fifth was staff path since staff roads used to be 3 m wide; a crossing cell counts as staff; kind is the one with most cells, guest/bridge over staff on a tie); old line paths are rasterized by `migratePaths`/`rasterPath` and then chunked, guest over staff, only on owned land; path themes are not carried over. A save whose gate isn't at `ENTRANCE_X` (the old 205) gets the gate moved there and `pathFixed` set to the new walk chunks (laid as guest path if missing); nothing else the player built moves.
  Connectivity (`pathReach()`, cached by `pathVer`, the chunk count and the chunks object) floods 4-connected chunks (edges, never corners) from the chunk just north of the gate, once for guests (`.G`: guest `g` and bridge `b` chunks only) and once for staff (`.A`: every chunk, so a staff chunk that touches a guest or bridge chunk, or the entrance walk, is joined for staff automatically). There is no Crossing tile any more. Guests never use staff chunks: a guest chunk that only a staff chunk touches is not a guest path. `chunkReached(cx, cy, guests)`, `pointReached`, `chunksJoined(keys, guests)` and `nearCells(shape, d, guests, any)` (a shape within d m of a joined chunk; `any` counts unjoined chunks too, guests then means g/b only) are the helpers. `recompute` (sim.js) reads exhibit and building reach through `nearCells` at `REACH - PATH_HALF_WIDTH` (3.5 m from a chunk edge, the same as 6 m from a strip's middle): exhibits and guest buildings need guest-joined chunks, `dept` buildings staff-joined ones; `gateCheck` (keepers.js) needs a staff-joined chunk within `GATE_REACH - 2.5` (1.5 m) of the gate (a gate right on a chunk's edge counts) and says whether the path touches at all or just doesn't join; the Move tool's "Keep it on a path" for path props uses chunks.

## Key globals
- `state.science`: `points`, `crew`, `tech` (TECH ids, plus `ref-<Period>`), `projects`, `unlocked` (animal ids), `dna` (animal and plant genomes, `PLANT_DNA`), `trips`, `clones`. `state.ceres`: Paleoflora `stock`, `meds`, landscape plants `pots` (keyed `<period>-<size>`), `beds`, `auto`.
- `state`: the whole saved game (JSON-serialized). **Anything that must survive a reload lives in `state`.**
- `derived`: computed from `state` by `recompute()` (reach, reports, demand, rating parts). Call `recompute()` after layout changes.
- `state.zones` / `state.logi`: work zones and logistics bookkeeping. Food is physical: it lives in `b.store` of stations, docks, warehouses, cold stores, and farms, and the PMC holds medicine. Exhibits only get what keepers carry. Stands and shops sell from their own `b.store` of guest goods (snacks, drinks, merch) once `state.logi.guestFrom` has passed; custodians carry it to them.
- `kGraph`: the staff walking graph. Rebuild it with `buildKeeperGraph()` after paths, buildings or gates change.
- `crew` / `mcrew` / `vcrew` / `ccrew` / `gcrew`: live keeper, mechanic, vet, custodian, and guard walkers. These are NOT saved, so mirror anything persistent onto `state.staff.*` (see `setCarry`, `transfer.cargo`).
- `parties` / `gGraph`: live guest parties and their footpath graph (rebuild with `buildGuestGraph()` alongside `buildKeeperGraph()`). Parties are NOT saved. Guests pay where they're served, and their mood when they leave goes into `state.today.moodSum`/`moodN` and `state.guestLog`, which set guest comfort and word of mouth.
- `events`: hooks the sim calls (`toast`, `changed`, `dayEnded`, `gameOver`), wired in main.js.
- Time runs around the clock. The sim counts in PARK minutes: `DAY_MIN` (720) of them make one day and `MINUTES_PER_SECOND` a second on screen (a day is 240 s), so per-minute and per-day rates are unchanged. The 24 h clock is only how a park minute is shown: `CLOCK_SCALE` (2) clock minutes each. `state.minute` is park minutes since the daily rollover (0 to `DAY_MIN`), which happens at `ROLLOVER_HOUR` (4 AM): `endDay()` and every `*Night()` run then (bills, `rollWeather`, `staffNight`, `lodgingNight`), and `state.minute` goes back to 0. `clockMin(m)` (sim.js) gives the clock minute of the day for a `state.minute`, `minuteAtClock(c)` goes back, and `fmtClock` takes a clock minute (the HUD shows `fmtClock(clockMin())`). Science stamps are continuous park minutes (`nowMin() = (day - 1) * DAY_MIN + minute`), shown as clock time by `whenText`/`spanText`. Daylight is `DAWN_CLOCK`/`DUSK_CLOCK` (6 AM, 8 PM; `DAWN_MIN`/`DUSK_MIN` as `state.minute` values, `isNight` in behavior.js): `awake`/`napLull` read the clock hour (nocturnal animals are up all night), and `nightDark()` + `NIGHT` (data.js) drive the `#nightWash` map overlay (one `opacity` change per HUD update, only when the step changes). Guests: the day's crowd arrives in a hump from `GUEST.arriveFrom` to `arriveTo` (`arrivalShare`), plus a thin round-the-clock trickle (`GUEST.trickle` a day at 5 stars, `trickleLow` at 0, `trickleRate()`; it is carved out of `derived.demand`, never on top of it). Nobody is flushed at closing any more: most daytime parties plan to be gone by `GUEST.curfew`, a few (`overnight`) stay on, and ones arriving after hours stay at most `nightStay` park minutes, so the park is never fully empty. Guest timers are `state.minute` stamps (`until`, `cool`, `linger`, `readyAt`, ...), so `shiftStamps(DAY_MIN)` (guests.js, called by `endDay`) moves them back a day at the rollover; any new guest timestamp must be added there. Closing: `state.parkClosed` (saved, default false; the Gate section of the Park Office's Park tab, action `toggleClosed`): `guestsArrive` and hotel guests do nothing, every party still inside gets `goHome`, the HUD shows `#closedTag`; wages and upkeep continue. Old saves (no `clockV`) are shifted by the old opening time in `upgradeSave`, so science stamps stay exact.

## Art direction (locked)
The Dimetrodon (`sprites/dime*.png`, shaded by `tools/pixeldime_shade.py`) is the reference: flat, simple, cartoony pixel art.
- **Shapes and color:** flat fills, no outlines, stair-stepped silhouettes, readable at game size. About 8 base colors a sprite, each with a lighter and a darker shade of the same hue. Palette family: olive greens (97,111,34), rust orange (178,78,0), yellow-olive and warm browns; buildings use their theme's colors.
- **Detail budget:** flat shapes plus about 30%, all structured: a lit top-left edge, shadow on undersides and the right side, rhythmic texture (rows of short dashes, grooves beside ribs, scutes, a lighter rib line). Never random scatter, noise or heavy dithering; it reads as sandpaper.
- **Light** comes from the top left everywhere.
- **Exhibits are full 2D:** flat ground and upright sprites, y-sorted. Animals are side-on facing right (mirrored by `face`). Don't render animals from 3D models; that was tried, cost a lot, and looked worse than simple hand-made shapes.
- **Buildings are 3/4 and front-facing**, extruded from the footprint by `building34`, so they rotate. Flat style: one flat tone per face (left-facing lightest, front the base tone, right-facing darkest), no strokes except for selected, doomed and unreachable states, every point snapped to the pixel grid (`flatSnap34`: `ROCK_MPP` = 0.13 m a pixel, with the vertical step divided by `TILT` so pixels are square after the squash), and pixel-built text and pictograms. Use the game's own names ("Restrooms", never "WC"). Keep the snap's tie-break (the `+ 1e-4`), or exact half-pixel positions round at random and drop columns.
- **Pixel size** is the same everywhere: 0.13 m a pixel.
- **Ground tiles** wrap and repeat, so keep them free of large or distinctive features, and of anything from the wrong era or biome (no grass or sage in Permian scrubland).
- **Check at game zoom and zoomed in.** A blurry screenshot hides bugs; reconstruct the drawn pixels (count them, or print them as ASCII) when something looks off.
- **Status:** in this style so far: the Dimetrodon, the Cotylorhynchus (shading and a new head, own palette and body), the Coelophysis rig (shading and texture only, own colors), all 24 scrubland landscape plants, the scrubland rocks, boulders, ground and water, the temperate rocks and boulders (grey, moss-capped, stained and leaf-litter), the wetland ground, rocks, boulders, all 27 plants and the six wetland Park Plants, the three Quaternary temperate plants and the seven temperate Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate.py`), the temperate Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_old.py`) and Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_meso.py`) and Paleogene and Neogene plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_ceno.py`, so all 27 temperate plants are done), all 27 desert plants (`tools/pixelplants_desert_old.py`, `_meso.py`, `_ceno.py`; the Cenozoic ones use common names), the four desert Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_desert_park.py`), the six tropical Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_tropical_park.py`: Strangler Fig, Mahogany, Bamboo, Banana, Giant Tree Fern, Hibiscus), the nine tropical Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian plants (`tools/pixelplants_tropical_old.py`) and the nine Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous ones (`tools/pixelplants_tropical_meso.py`) and the nine Paleogene, Neogene and Quaternary ones (`tools/pixelplants_tropical_ceno.py`), so all 27 tropical plants are done, with common names from the Paleogene on, the desert ground, rocks and water, the grassland ground, rocks, water, six plants and Park Plant (`tools/pixelplants_grassland.py`; common names Bluegrass / Kangaroo Grass / Pampas Grass, Fescue / Grama / Bluestem), the tundra ground, rocks, water, six plants and Labrador Tea Park Plant (`tools/pixelplants_tundra.py`; common names Cottongrass / Crowberry / Arctic Willow, Saxifrage / Mountain Avens / Dwarf Birch), the boreal ground, rocks, water, 18 plants and the two Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_boreal.py`; ## Sprite rigger (tools/rigger)
A standalone page for animating sprites from parts, with no build step: `tools/rigger/index.html`. It is NOT part of the game (not in `index.html`, not in the smoke test). Also published as an Artifact, https://claude.ai/artifact/Es5nLZcG73gpnDT71tbLgi (capability `downloads`); the artifact copy is the repo file with the `<!doctype>`/`<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper stripped (the repo copy keeps it, so it opens from disk), and it can't use `alert`/`confirm`/`prompt` or `<a download>` (it has `ask()` and the `downloads` capability instead). Republishing it is separate from publishing the game.
- **Model.** Each part is one PNG, a full cell in size with the part drawn where it belongs. A part ("node") has a joint `px,py` (pixel in the image it turns about), a `parent`, and `lx,ly`, its joint's place in the parent's frame (the world for a root). Pose per frame is `{nodeId:{r,dx,dy}}`: `r` degrees (positive turns clockwise on screen), `dx,dy` shift in the parent's frame. World matrix = parent world · translate(lx+dx, ly+dy) · rotate(r); the image is drawn at (-px,-py) (`flip` mirrors it about the joint). The empty pose is the rest pose, the standing picture. List order is draw order (last is in front) and is separate from parenting. `mirror` pairs two parts (near and far leg). A part's `off` (Rig mode: "Hidden unless a frame shows it") hides it in every frame unless the frame's pose has `v:1`; `v:0` hides any part for one frame (Pose mode: the Show box or V; `shownIn`, `showPart`); tweens keep the first frame's `v`. Its `hidden` (the eye in the Parts list) only hides it while editing, and `rigtogame.py` still exports it. Replace picture… (`swapPicture`) swaps a part's PNG and keeps its joint, parent, place and poses; other parts sharing the old picture (a duplicated leg) keep it.
- **Project JSON** (Save/Load project): `{name, cell:{w,h}, ground, seq, assets:{id:dataURL}, nodes:[{id,name,asset,px,py,parent,lx,ly,flip,hidden,mirror}], ref, clips:[{name,fps,frames:[pose]}]}`. One rig, several clips (walk, idle...). Export strip gives `<name>-<clip>.png`, the frames side by side at `cell` size, the game's walk format. Export standing gives the rest pose. Export GIF (`makeGif`, own LZW encoder in the page: no library or worker, which an artifact can't load) writes the clip as an animated GIF at its fps (honoring ping-pong), 1× to 8× with no smoothing, the palette taken from the frames (index 0 is clear when Transparent is on, more colors than a GIF holds are merged to the closest), and delays spread so the average is exactly the fps. "Whole pixels" rounds each part's position to the pixel grid, rotation is nearest-neighbor (no smoothing).
- **Tools.** New project (top bar) clears every part and animation after a confirm; Undo brings the old project back (its pictures stay in memory, and the autosave and Save project leave out what nothing uses). Rig mode: Move, Joint (click the pivot; the picture and the children stay put), parent, Mirror partner, Duplicate (pairs the copy). Pose mode: drag the orange knob (or the part) to rotate about its joint, Shift snaps 5°, Move shifts, arrows nudge. The Parts list is the draw order (top row drawn last, in front): drag a row (a mouse from anywhere on it, a finger or pen from its handle) to reorder, with an accent line showing where it will land, or use the arrows; Undo puts the order back. Part picking: a click within a few screen pixels of a thin part counts, pressing on the selected part keeps working on it (a drag turns or moves that part, never a neighbor in front), while a plain click picks the topmost part under the pointer, and Alt+click cycles to the part beneath; the rotation knob wins only within about 7 screen pixels of itself. Timeline: copy frame, rest frame, reorder, Fill to next / first (tween), Mirror pose, Mirror clip, Half-cycle partner (copies the selected part's motion to its partner half a cycle later), onion skin, play with ping-pong.
- **Driving it headless** (how the Plateosaurus walk was made): load it in Playwright and call page globals: `openProject(json)`, `P`, `frames()`, `worlds(pose)`, `setParent(node, id)`, `setMirror`, `halfCycle`, `drawPose(ctx, pose, 1, snap)`, `stripCanvas(clip())`, `pruned()` (the project JSON), `save(true)`. `tools/rigger/prionosuchus/gen-clips.js` is the model for a short-legged animal (the hand-made rig is `prionosuchus-rig.json`; it first shades the flat parts with `tools/pixelprio_rig_shade.py` (the strips' rules from `tools/pixelprio_shade.py`, run per part; edges on joints skipped), then builds a lateral-sequence walk with no IK, an idle jaw gape and a lie-down rest with the chin lifted and the forelegs reaching forward, and a swim clip (body low, legs tucked, tail wave; run `rigtogame.py ... --swim swim --waterline 40`), and writes the project, contact sheets and GIFs). `tools/rigger/plateosaurus/gen-walk.js` is the model for a long-legged one: it sets the frames of a clip from code, with 2-bone IK on each hind leg (stance foot planted and sliding back at body speed, swing foot lifted, far leg half a cycle behind), body bob, and tail, neck and arm counter-motion, then writes the strip, a contact sheet and the project. Its node ids, ground height and gait numbers (`N`, `ds`, `S`, `af`, `lift`, `B`) are for the Plateosaurus, and its paths are for the session it was written in, so adapt them. Check a walk by looking at zoomed frames, not the numbers alone.
- **Workflow for an animal.** Cut it into flat base-color parts (no shading or texture), rig and animate, export the strip and the standing picture, then run the shading and detail pass on the strip (like the `tools/pixel*_shade.py` scripts). Shading baked into parts would rotate with them, turning the top-left light and breaking rows of dashes. Then add the `SPRITES` entry in data.js (`ratio:100/75, walk:N, step:`about `1/N`, and a standing `sprites/<id>.png` whose feet are on the same row as the walk frames) and copy the PNGs to `sprites/`. 4 to 8 frames suits a walk; the game only moves the viewport per frame, so the count costs nothing in speed.
- **Into the game.** `python3 tools/rigtogame.py PROJECT.json <id> --walk <clip> --idle <clip> [<clip> ...] --rest <clip> --rest-end <n>` writes `sprites/<id>/<part>.png` (each part cropped to its pixels) and the `RIGS.<id>` line in js/rigs.js; then add `rig:1` to the species' `SPRITES` entry (data.js) and publish `js/rigs.js`, the PNGs and the changed JS through `files`. The rig is drawn by turning each part's picture at screen size, so it is not baked to the 1x grid: details stay whole, but a turned part's pixels are tilted, and at the game's far zoom (under one screen pixel a sprite pixel) it reads the same as a baked frame. The walk clip must be one loop (the last frame leads back into the first); it is tweened, not stepped, so fewer keyframes are fine. The idle clip plays at its fps.
- **Known limits.** Rotated pixel art gets uneven edges, and big angles break texture and leave wedge gaps at joints (hide them with a small joint-cap part). Not built yet: IK in the tool (only in scripts), RotSprite-style pixel-clean rotation, replacing a part's image while keeping the rig, a darker tint for far-side limbs.

## Where the code lives (read this before every change)
- **`main` is the source of truth.** Start from it, and get changes merged into it before or right after publishing.
- Publish only from a branch that has `origin/main` merged in. If you publish from a stale branch, you overwrite other people's fixes in the live game.
- Never copy the live artifact's files back into git wholesale. If the live game seems to have something git doesn't, diff each file against `main` and port only the missing hunks. A wholesale copy once undid a fix (the animal Move dialog).
- A file's helpers aren't always where you'd guess (see the notes above). Search for `function name` or `const name =` across `js/` before adding one.

## Sprite pack (read before adding any sprite)
The artifact holds at most 511 files, so the ~480 PNGs are NOT published one by one. `tools/packsprites.py` packs every `sprites/**/*.png` into `js/spritepack.js` (data URIs, generated: never edit it), and every drawn picture goes through `spriteUrl(path)` (defined there; a short `blob:` URL when blobs load, else the data URI, with the path kept as a `#fragment`). Index.html's ground and water tiles use `data-sp` for the same reason. **After adding or changing any PNG, run `python3 tools/packsprites.py`, commit the new `js/spritepack.js`, and publish it.** New code that puts a sprite path in an `<image href>` or `<img src>` must wrap it in `spriteUrl(...)`. Keep PNGs in git as the source; only the pack is published. Merge conflicts in `js/spritepack.js`: take either side and re-run the tool.

# Genesis Park Tycoon

Browser park-builder game (Jurassic Park style). Plain JS, no build step, no modules: every file is a classic `<script>` sharing one global scope. Published as a claude.ai Artifact.

## Files (script load order, from index.html; menu.js sits just before map.js)
- `js/data.js`: all tuning constants and content (`SPECIES`, `BUILDINGS`, `BARRIERS`, `TECH`, `GOALS`, `KEEPER`, `MAINT`, `POWER`, `ESCAPE`, `HEALTH`, `MEDICINE`, `VET`, ...). Balance changes go here.
- `js/geometry.js`: pure shape math (`area`, `perimeter`, `inPoly`, `segProj`, `shapesOverlap`, ...).
- `js/sim.js`: the core loop `tick(dtMin)` → `endDay()`, plus guest demand, money (`spend`/`earn`/`canAfford`), happiness (`exhibitReport`), rating, species pull on guests (`speciesDraw`: appeal stretched by `GUEST.drawPower` around `GUEST.drawPivot`, so showstoppers dominate and low-appeal animals draw little), goals, and save migration (`newPark`, `upgradeSave`). The park's own biome is `state.biome` (`parkBiome()`, picked on the start screen and in the park office dialog, main.js `biomeCards`): the ground is drawn in `BIOMES[x].park` with the `#p-<biome>` pattern, and new exhibits start on it. Also land for sale (`PARCELS` in data.js, `state.parcels`, `ownsParcel`, `parcelPrice`, `buyParcel`, `ownedRects`/`ownedEdges`/`inOwned`; the Buy Land tool `parcels` and `insidePlot` are in map.js, and `state.boundary` is just the home plot now). Also shared helpers: `uid`, `dept`/`hasDept` (is a science building up), `money`, `fmtArea`, `fmtClock`, `SPECIES_BY_ID`.
- `js/science.js`: ORACLE/GHOST/TAR/CERES logic, loaded right after sim.js. Every scientist is one bay holding one job or none (`BAY_JOBS`, `bayCount`, `freeBay`, `fireScientist`; each job carries its `bay`), so hiring is the only way the work lists grow; `baysHtml` in scienceui.js draws them in a fixed order. Everything is timed in park minutes (`nowMin()`, jobs carry `start`/`end`) and finishes mid-day in `scienceTick(dtMin)`, not at night. ORACLE projects (`startProject`, kinds `tech`/`refine`/`species`, one per researcher; a researcher with no project is on general research and earns `RESEARCH_PER_PALEO` points a day, `stopProject` puts one back, and guests add `RESEARCH_GUEST` points times what they learned as they leave in guests.js; species unlocks get dearer by `UNLOCK_GROWTH` per animal already unlocked, and a project remembers its `cost` for refunds), GHOST trips (`tripOdds`, `launchTrip`, `tripReturns`; a genome takes 2 to 8 trips by size), TAR clones (`orderClone`, upgrades `fast1/2`), and CERES growing beds (`growBatch`: landscape plants by period and size into `ceres.pots` (keys like `Jurassic-large`), medicine batches; plant DNA is one genome per Mesozoic/Paleozoic period, `PLANT_DNA[period]`, which GHOST collects once ORACLE has researched the era's flora). CERES's daily Paleoflora food output is NOT here: it's in keepers.js.
- `js/keepers.js`: keepers (`hireKeeper`, `allStaffList`), food (`dailyNeed`, `shortages`, `eatTick`), CERES Paleoflora food output (`ceresTick`, `ceresRate`, `ceresCap`, `greenhouses`, `paleofloraReady`), cleaning, animal transfers (`requestMove`, `keeperMoveProblem`), depots and ATVs (`depots`, `syncAtvs`, `onAtv`), and the staff path graph (`buildKeeperGraph`, `walkFrom` Dijkstra). Call keeper / vet / mechanic (buttons in the exhibit panel's Keepers and food section; `CALLS` table, `callKeeper(e, kind)`, `state.staff.calls` {ex, keeper, kind}): the nearest free worker of that kind drops their job at the top of their tick loop (`takeCall`, `takeVetCall`, `takeMechCall`), unclaimed calls go to the next free one (`callJob`), and on arrival (`toCall`) a keeper hands over carried food, sets `c.focusEx` so `decide` feeds that exhibit first, and mucks it out if dirty; a vet runs a check-up then treats that exhibit's sick animals first (`focusEx`, `pickPatient(c, only)`); a mechanic inspects the fence and repairs it (maintenance.js). Escapes still come first. Food trays (`LAND` items with `tray`, `trays`, `trayRoomOf`, `trayShare`, `startFilling`/`fillStep`): a tray holds one food at a time, optionally fixed with `f.set` (picked in the exhibit's Habitat Props, Food Trays sub-tab, `propsTabs`); keepers with leftover food walk in and fill them, and `eatTick` drains trays before gate stock.
- `js/escapes.js`: barrier strength, breakouts, loose animals, guest deaths, viewing platforms (`viewFactor`), barrier upgrade costs (`upgradeCost`, `moatCost`, `aviaryCost`). Also home to a few shared helpers: `hasTech`, `isFlyer`, `nearestNode`.
- `js/power.js`: generators and electric-fence power allocation (`updatePower`).
- `js/maintenance.js`: fence/generator/depot wear, plus mechanics who inspect and repair.
- `js/medicine.js`: sickness and injuries (`illChance`, `injuryChance`, `healthNight`), the PMC ward (`admit`, `discharge`, `pmcRemoved`), vets who dart sick and escaped animals (`vetsTick`), and which medicine cures what (`canTreat`: the era's medicine plus a `ref-<Period>` refinement from ORACLE). CERES makes the doses in batches (science.js). Genome therapy (ORACLE tech `genetherapy`, `GENE` in data.js): the player queues a sickly clone (`a.gene`) from the exhibit's Health section, and a free vet (`pickGene`, jobs `toGene`/`splicing`, `geneDone`) lifts its `a.q` toward the lab's genome quality (`labQuality`, which GHOST raises), at most `GENE.step` a go, for `geneCost`. Also trash thrown into exhibits: `throwTick` (called from the guest loop in guests.js) lets rowdy parties, and others holding trash, throw it over an open exhibit's fence (`THROWN` in data.js; cut by working bins, guards via `guardNear`, and a `nofeed` sign within `THROWN.signReach`, `hasNoFeed`). `e.trash` counts pieces for the day, and `trashNight` (in `healthNight`) makes animals eat it: ill or dead (`trashEaten`).
- `js/landscape.js`: water, rocks, groves and shelters inside open exhibits. Water is drawn corner by corner like an exhibit (`e.water`, `WATER`, `waterProblem`/`waterHost`/`addWater`; the `water` draw tool in map.js, reshaped with the Move tool), and old saves' round ponds become drawn water in `upgradeSave`. The rest are placed with a tap (`e.land`, `LAND`, `HAB`, `likesOf` in data.js). `habitatOf`/`habitatScore` feed `exhibitReport`, and each species wants its own water share, rocks and plants (`wantsOf` vs `haveOf`, `speciesFit`; `likesOf`, `BIOMES[x].rock`/`plants`, `HAB.waterMax`; too much water counts against it too), `thirsty` feeds `illChance`. Groves (`LAND[x].flora`) from an animal's era add happiness, `browseShare` cuts what `eatTick` drains, and `grassShare(e, s)` (0 to 1: Cenozoic plants as a share of the exhibit's plants, a grassland biome, or grass hay in place of Paleoflora) is how much of an old grazer's food is grass it never evolved for: it feeds `grassSick` (illness, `GRASS_INTOLERANT`) and the happiness hit in `exhibitReport`. Landscape plants are generated from `PLANT_TABLE` in data.js: one small, medium and large per period and biome (keys like `jur-tropical-small`, `PLANTS_OF[period][biome]`), so a biome that didn't exist in a period has none (no grassland before the Neogene). They carry `period` and `biome` and `landProblem` only lets them go in an exhibit of that biome. An animal only counts plants from its own period toward what it wants (`haveOf(e, s)`, `plantSuits`), and wants none where its period had no plants in the exhibit's biome (`wantsOf`). The build menu has a dropdown per period and the exhibit panel lists the biome's plants by period. Each Mesozoic or Paleozoic plant uses one plant of its period and size (`LAND[x].size`) from `state.ceres.pots`, which CERES grows in batches (`CERES_GROW.plant`). Biomes (`BIOMES`, `SPECIES_BIOMES`/`biomesOf` in data.js): each open exhibit has `e.biome` (`biomeOf` falls back to `DEFAULT_BIOME`), and each open-exhibit species has a home biome and one it gets by in. `biomeFit` feeds `habitatScore` (`BIOME_HAPPY`), wetland adds to `wet` in `habitatOf`, and `regradeProblem`/`regradeCost` back the panel's Biome picker. map.js fills open exhibits with the biome color and its `#b-<biome>` pattern from index.html (vivariums keep the period tint; animal dots stay period colors). Weather (`state.weather` {today, next, from}, `WEATHER` in data.js) is rolled each night by `rollWeather` in `endDay`, after the night functions so today's weather counts for tonight's illness. `exposure(e)`, the share of the herd without cover from shelters (`LAND[x].slots`) or grove shade, feeds happiness, `illChance`, `injuryChance` (storms) and `wearPerDay`, and `weatherNow().guests` scales demand. `landSpot`/`placeLand`/`landSvg` do placement and drawing. The tools (`land-rock`, `land-cycads`, ...) are handled in map.js (`landHover`/`landTap`), and water and land features move, reshape-check and bulldoze with their exhibit.
  Park landscaping (outside exhibits) lives here too. `landSpot` falls back to `decorProblem` when the point isn't in an open exhibit, and `placeLand(null, ...)` puts the item in `state.decor`. Water drawn outside exhibits goes in `state.water` (`waterProblem(pts, null)` → `parkWaterProblem`, `addWater(null, pts)`), and open fence lines in `state.fences` (`fenceProblem`, `addFenceLine`, `fenceLineCost` in sim.js; kind `"fence"` in map.js for select/move/bulldoze). New paths, buildings and exhibits must keep clear of all of it: `shapeHitsLandscape(pts)` and `lineHitsLandscape(pts, hw, bridge)`; only a path with `type:"bridge"` (`isBridge`, `BRIDGE` in data.js) crosses park water. Named modern plants (`PARK_PLANTS` → `LAND["q-<name>"]`, `PARK_PLANTS_OF[biome]`) are Quaternary plants usable anywhere, with `look`/`accent` for drawing (`plantSvg`) and `wet`/`aquatic` for water. Other periods' plants need the ORACLE tech `sterile` to go outside exhibits. Statues (`STATUES` → `LAND["st-<id>"]`, `statue` field) go outside exhibits only; a few are `free`, the rest unlock with a grant's `statue` field (`statueOpen`, `statueGrant`, shown by `grantPrize`). A statue's plinth uses its theme's path color (`statueSvg`). Guests: `indexDecor(nodes)` (called from `buildGuestGraph`) marks each guest-path node with `n.decor` (0 to 1, `DECOR`) and `n.statues`; `guestsTick` turns decor into mood and the "pretty" thought, and `statueSeen` gives learning (people's statues teach far more). Drawing: `waterSvg`, `featSvg`, `parkWaterSvg`, `decorSvg`, `fenceLineSvg`, `hedgeSvg` (hedge rows, `BARRIERS.hedge`, are drawn as real hedges in exhibits too).
- `js/themes.js`: themes (`THEMES`, `THEME` in data.js).  Zones can carry a `theme` (`zoneThemePlan`, `restyleZone`, `themedZoneAt`): it tints the map under the zone (`THEMES[x].ground`), new builds inside use it, and a panel button restyles what is already there (UI in logiui.js `zoneThemeHtml`). Genesis (the default, no `theme` field) is a full theme too: a conservation campus of forest green, matte gold and warm concrete. `roofs` sort buildings by job (green standing seam for public ones, pale flat lab roofs with `units` for ORACLE/GHOST/TAR/CERES/PMC, dark slate for utilities), `emblem` marks the four tech buildings (and the entrance gate in map.js), exhibits get the steel rail or panel wall with amber post lights while an electric fence is powered (map.js). Paths, buildings and exhibits carry an optional `theme` (none means Genesis) that recolors them (`themeOf`, `themeFill`). `state.themes` holds the build `brush` and the unlocked `have` list; `checkThemes` (called from `checkGoals`) unlocks them by play (Gilded Age by the `gilded` ORACLE tech, Bayou by keeping a water-loving animal, Volcanic by a 4-star rating). `themeZone(e)` checks that the guest paths and shops and restrooms within `THEME.radius` of an exhibit all match it (non-Genesis only), which adds appeal in `recompute`, and `themeFitShare` (species in `THEMES[x].fits`) lifts happiness in `exhibitReport`. Changing a theme costs `fee` of the item's price (`themeFee`, `themeProblem`); new builds use the brush (`themeNew`). The pickers are in ui.js (`themeHtml`, `themesOverview`). Loads after landscape.js.
- `js/behavior.js`: animal behavior, loaded after themes.js. Each animal carries six needs in `a.need` (`NEED_KEYS`: hunger, thirst, discomfort, lonely, bored, stress; 0 is met, 100 desperate) and an act `a.act` (`ACTS` in data.js: eat, drink, rest, patrol, hunt, forage, social, play, hide, pace) with `a.actFor` minutes and, when pacing, `a.why`. `behaviorTick` (from `tick`, every `BEHAVIOR.step` minutes) runs `needsStep` then `pickAct` per animal off a per-exhibit `behaviorCtx`; `behaviorNight` resets them in `endDay`. Species traits (`traitsOf`: `active` diurnal/nocturnal/crepuscular via `awake`, `social` solitary/pair/herd/pack via `matesWanted`, `temper` skittish/curious/aggressive) come from `TRAITS` in data.js, else from diet, group and size. Enrichment (`enrichment`, `enrichNeed`, `toyPoints`; `ENRICH`, and the toy props in `LAND`, under Habitat Props' Enrichment sub-tab: `post`/`logs`/`wallow`/`ball` with flat `toy` points, and `icefish`/`icefruit`/`shank`/`buglog`/`hay` with `toyFor` points by diet (`toyFor(t, s)`, `toyPoints(e, s)`, `toyFit` scores each species against `enrichNeedOf`). Items with `lasts` (frozen treats, hay bales) count down `f.left` in `treatsNight` and vanish, frozen ones `ENRICH.melt` faster in a heat wave; hay bales also feed plant eaters (`hay` in `browseRate`), and Paleoflora bales (`pbale`) feed the older ones (`paleo`), taking `ceres` Paleoflora from `state.ceres.stock` in `placeLand` and needing the `paleoflora` tech; vivariums have their own `vivToy` items, `vbark`/`vbranch`/`vdig`/`vfeed`/`vmist`, picked from a dropdown in the vivarium's Exhibit tab, `vivToysHtml`, and set in the glass by `addVivToy`/`vivToyProblem` in landscape.js) slows boredom and lets them play. Effects: `behaviorScore` in `exhibitReport` (stress, boredom, the Pacing/Hiding tells), `liveliness` on appeal in `recompute`, `stressEscape` in `escapesTick`, `BEHAVIOR.stressIll` in `illChance`, and the `pacing`/`playful` guest thoughts in `seeExhibit`. map.js walks each act (`animalPlan`, `actGoal`, `paceRoute`, `patrolRoute`, `ACT_GAIT`): pacing animals get an amber dashed ring and hiding ones fade. The exhibit panel's Behavior tab is `behaviorHtml` in ui.js.
- `js/logistics.js`: food and medicine as goods. Stores (`b.store`, `storeOf`, `addGood`/`takeGood`), spoilage, dock orders, farms, work zones (`state.zones`, `e.zone`/`b.zone`/`k.zone`), and restock hauls (`pickHaul`, `supplyLines`).
- `js/guests.js`: guest parties. Their needs (`NEEDS`), the guest path graph (`buildGuestGraph`, `guestField` per stop), wide paths (`WIDE_PATH`, `isWide`; `node.room` doubles the `GUEST.crowd` limit on that stretch), deciding where to go (`planParty`), queues and service at food stands, shops and restrooms (`svcQ`, `serveParty`), mood, thoughts (`THOUGHTS`, `topThoughts`), and the nightly `guestsNight`. Also learning (`p.edu`, `learn`, `hasSign`): info signs, field guides and the Education Center teach guests, which lifts mood, cuts litter and vandalism, brings donations, and feeds the Education rating part (`EDU`).
- `js/personas.js`: guest personalities, loaded right after guests.js. Every party has `p.pers` (primary, secondary; `PERSONALITIES` and `PERS` in data.js, `rollPers`). The primary counts in full and the secondary `PERS.second` as much. `pm(p, key)` blends a personality's `fx` multipliers (cached per party in `p.fxc`; neutral is 1; `pneed` for need rates) and is read all over: `serveAt` (food, shop, museum, toilet, price), `learn`, donations, queue patience, litter, mood hurt (`infra`, `walk`, `decor`), fleeing. Exhibit tastes steer `pickSight` (`tasteOf`, `centerTaste`, off `exhibitTraits(e)`: danger, kids, active, hunting, quality, rare, platform) and reactions run from `seeExhibit` (`personaSee`, `PERS_SEE`) and on the way out (`personaLeaves`: conservationists sniff out a park that is all show, paleo-nerds judge the collection). Hooligans (`p.rowdy` is true for primary or secondary) may sneak in without a ticket (`sneaksIn`, `p.sneaked`, checked in `guestsArrive`) and tease animals at fences (`teaseTick`). Calling security from a guest's panel: `ejectGuest` (`ejectProblem`, `ejectTick`, `escortOut`, `partyStatus`). Ordinary parties stop (`p.hold`) until a guard comes within `PERS.escortRadius`, then are walked to the gate unhappy; primary hooligans keep moving and the guard has to catch them (`guardWatch` in security.js). There are no rides, playgrounds or petting zoos in the game yet, so those likes aren't wired. To add a personality, add an entry to `PERSONALITIES`, a `PERS_TASTE`/`PERS_SEE` function if it reacts to exhibits, and `THOUGHTS` for what it says.
  Guests walk and live at `GUEST.pace` of staff speed (`spd` includes it; `guestsTick` runs needs, mood drain, vandalism and teasing on `gdt = dt * pace`; `stay` is divided by it), so one can be clicked before it's gone while a trip still feels the same per meter. Parties are dots in their primary personality's color in `drawParties` (the mood-color toggle stays); a tap in select mode finds one by screen distance (`guestAt`) and opens `sel = {kind:"guest"}` (`selItem` reads `parties`), drawn by `guestHtml`/`guestLive` in guestui.js, which updates in place from the frame loop instead of redrawing the panel.
- `js/services.js`: guest services. Menus on food and gift shells (`b.menu`, `MENU`, `servesOf`, `serveAt`, `willPay`), benches and picnic areas, trash bins and litter (`state.litter` grid, `trashCheck`), restroom dirt, and the night cleaning crew (`servicesNight`, `cleaningBill`).
  Museum attractions (end of services.js): `MUSEUM_EVENTS` in data.js (Great Dying, K-Pg Impact, Hadean Earth, Great Oxygenation, Cambrian Explosion, Tetrapods) are recorded by GHOST under its Events tab. They reuse the genome machinery (`sc.dna[id]`, `genomeInfo(id).event`, `placeOf`/`placeName` stand in for `PERIOD_BY_ID` on a trip, gated by the `education` tech). A finished record is on offer in an Education Center's dropdown (`b.shows`, `showsReady`, `addShow`/`dropShow`, `MUSEUM.slots`); `showsLearn`/`showsJoy` add to a visit in `serveAt`, `showsAppeal` to `planParty`, `showsUpkeep` to the daily bill, all scaled by record quality (`showPower`). UI: `museumHtml` in guestui.js.
  Education Center focus and modules (also end of services.js): `b.focus` (`EDU_FOCUS`: conservation donations, spectacle appeal, family mood and learning, research ORACLE points) works at `focusPower(b)`, 1 plus the `boost` of each installed module (`b.mods`, `EDU_MODULES`: theater, fossils, auditorium, each gated by an ORACLE tech `ed*`, `EDU_CENTER.moduleSlots`). `eduLearn`/`eduJoy`/`eduAppeal`/`eduUpkeep` sum museum attractions, modules and focus, and are what `serveAt`, `planParty` and the daily bill use; `slotsOf` adds the auditorium's seats in the guests.js queue. UI: `focusHtml`/`modulesHtml` in guestui.js. Live modules (`live` in `EDU_MODULES`: labwindow, nursery, botanical, simroom, diorama, touch) scale their effects and focus boost by `modLive(id)`: how many science departments have work going (lab), or clones growing plus a hatching today (`sc.hatchDay`, set when a clone finishes) for the nursery, or finished plant DNA plus growing beds for the botanical hall, or finished GHOST event records for the simulation room, or biomes with animals in them for the diorama, or finished animal genomes for the touch gallery; `dept` lists the science buildings needed to install one. All nine planned modules are in; to add one, add an entry to `EDU_MODULES` plus a tech.
- `js/custodians.js`: custodians (`ccrew`, `state.staff.custodians`) who restock food stands and gift shops with guest goods (`pickHaul` with `GUEST_GOODS`), scrub restrooms, empty bins, and sweep litter, working from a Custodial Closet.
- `js/security.js`: vandalism by rowdy, unhappy guests, with camera posts (`camera` path prop, `SECURITY.postRadius`) and office cameras feeding `onCamera` (`vandalTick`, `vandalize`, `deterrence`, broken props via `propCond`/`isBroken`, `b.graffiti`) and security guards (`gcrew`, `state.staff.guards`) who patrol, deter, catch vandals, and answer cameras from a Security Office.
- `js/lodging.js`: hotels (`lodge`, `resort`, `isHotel`, `LODGING`). Guests book rooms overnight (`lodgingNight`, `state.lodging.stays`), and next morning they start at the hotel with no ticket (`hotelGuestsArrive`). Rooms use toiletries (merch in `b.store`, which custodians restock) and get dirty (`b.dirt`, which custodians clean). Unlocking is checked in `hotelLocked`.
- `js/staff.js`: every walking worker in one place, loaded after security.js. `STAFF_TYPES` (keeper, vet, mechanic, custodian, guard: `list`, `crew`, `hire`, `status`, `sync`, `ok`), `staffRoster`/`staffById`/`staffCrew`, `hireStaff`/`fireStaff` (fire hands back carried food, moves and hunts), `energyOf` (`s.stamina`; keepers tire walking, the rest lose `STAFF.workDrain`/`idleDrain` a minute in `staffTick`, everyone resets in `staffNight`), and morale (`s.morale`, drifts toward `moraleTarget` from `moraleFactors`, which are also the thoughts; `STAFF` in data.js). Hiring and firing happen ONLY in the park office's Staff tab (`staffTabHtml` in staffui.js); department panels just show `staffNote`. Scientists are counts, not people (`state.science.crew`), so they get hire/fire rows in the same tab but no panel.
- `js/staffui.js`: the Staff tab, the tapped-worker panel (`sel = {kind:"staff"}`, `staffHtml`/`staffLive`, found on the map by `staffAt`, ringed by `drawStaffRing`), and the Fire "Are you sure?" dialog (`staffFireDialog`, via `askConfirm`).
- Tram (no file of its own): track is a path with `type:"tram"` (`isTram` in sim.js, `TRAM` and the `tramstop` building in data.js, tech `transit`), drawn with the `tram` tool in map.js. `connectedPathIds` leaves it out, so nobody walks it and it never joins the footpath network. In `buildGuestGraph` (guests.js) each working station (`b.id + ":foot"` on the footpath, `":rail"` on the track) gets a platform link (walk plus `TRAM.wait`) and the track between rail stops becomes ride edges (`node.ride`, length over `TRAM.speedMult`, `node.rail`). `walkParty` moves by the edge's `adj` cost, `board` charges the fare (`state.tramFare`, set from the station and track panels; `tramWill` is the share who'll pay it), and riding skips tiredness. A party that can't afford the fare (or thinks it's too dear) plans with the walk-only map: `canRide`, `fieldFor`, `guestField(id, walkOnly)` and `walkFrom`'s `skip` argument. Stations wear like depots (`tramWear`, `tramStations`, `tramWorking`, `TRAM.offlineBelow` in maintenance.js): a worn one drops out of the guest graph until a mechanic repairs it. Cars are drawn by `drawTrams` (map.js, `tramStops`), from the clock, so they aren't saved.
- `js/menu.js`: the build sidebar (`BUILD_MENU`, `buildMenu`, `refreshMenu`; Park Plants and Statues dropdowns, statue locks via `data-lock`). The Park Plants and Quaternary plants dropdowns list the same 51 plants (the named park plants and the Quaternary biome plants), by design: `parkPlantKeys`, `plantRow`. Groups and dropdowns are generated from the data, so prices follow `BUILDINGS`, `BARRIERS` and `fenceRate`. Loads before map.js.
- `js/map.js`: SVG rendering, camera, build tools (including the Move tool: `moveTarget`, `moveBy`, `moveProblem`, `dropMove`; and `fenceSel`, the fence an exhibit is built with. The fence tool is the `exhibit` draw: closing on the first corner (or the Close exhibit button) builds an exhibit, tapping the last corner again builds an open fence line with `finishFence`), and drawing guests (`drawParties`). Placement checks `exhibitProblem`/`pathProblem`, `removeItem` (bulldoze), `findItem`, `insidePlot`. Also defines `$`, `esc` and `nodeKey`, which sim files use too. Its `moveProblem(kind, it)` is for the Move tool; moving animals between exhibits uses keepers.js's `keeperMoveProblem(e)`.
- Animal sprites: `sprites/<species id>.png` standing (side-on, facing right, transparent), listed in `SPRITES` in data.js with `ratio` (width over height) and `walk`, the frame count of `sprites/<id>-walk.png` (frames side by side, each the standing picture's size, feet on the same row). `spriteSvg` in map.js draws one in place of the dot (open exhibits, once it's at least 6px across); in `animateAnimals` it flips to face the way it walks and `setPose` steps the walk frame by distance walked (`h.stride`), back to standing (or, if the entry also has `idle`, cycling `sprites/<id>-idle.png` via `restPose`; `setPose` frames: -1 stands, 0+ walk, -2 and down idle) while it waits. Vivarium animals don't walk: they stay on their spot (spaced by `cramped`) and, if the entry has `idle` (frame count of `sprites/<id>-idle.png`, same strip format) and optionally `ms` (per frame), cycle it; with no idle strip they show the standing picture, and with no `SPRITES` entry the dot. Publish new PNGs through `files` too. Every species is drawn from its PNGs (there used to be coded copies of the Coelophysis, Dimetrodon and Tiktaalik in `js/pixelart.js` that the game painted instead; they're gone, so a change to a PNG is what shows). Each has a shading pass over its PNGs: `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py`, `pixelprio_shade.py` (also sways the walk strip's tail), `pixeltikt_shade.py`, `pixelcoel_shade.py`, `pixeldime_shade.py`; all but the Dimetrodon's give the same result when run twice. The Lystrosaurus (`sprites/lyst*.png`, drawn at `size:.7`) is shaded by `tools/pixellyst_shade.py` (smooth leathery hide, no scales: creased thigh and shoulder, wrinkles, sparse grain; it also cuts `lyst.png` from the walk strip). Its 8-frame idle strip (`idle:8`: lowers its head and sniffs the dirt, then plays back to standing) is built by `tools/pixellyst_idle.py` from hand-posed pictures in `tools/lyst-idle/` (cells sampled off their 13.55 px grid, snapped to the palette, shaded with the same rules, the whole animal moved 2 px left so the lowered snout fits); run the shade script first. Cotylorhynchus keeps its own palette and body shape; `tools/pixelcoty_shade.py` adds the flat-style shading and texture to `sprites/coty*.png` in place and redraws its head (`reshape_head`: a small blunt-snouted caseid skull, per frame offset, eye and mouth). Its idle strip is 8 frames (`idle:8` in `SPRITES`): it dips and blows a bubble, the bubble pops, it is surprised, settles and stands (`IDLE_EXPR`, `SPECKS`); the first run grew it from 6 frames. It reads shaded colors back first, so re-running is safe, and it asserts nothing left of the head moved.
- Footprints and tap shapes: `SPRITE_HIT` in data.js (made by `tools/spritehit.py` from the PNGs' opaque pixels; re-run it after changing a plant or rock picture) gives each sprite's base width and a silhouette in 4 px rows. landscape.js turns the base into `LAND[x].fr`, the ground a plant or rock takes up (`footR(t)`, never more than `r`): crowding, fences, paths and animals use it, while `r` still sizes the drawing and habitat math. Bulldozing taps the silhouette (`spriteHitPath`, `hitShape`), not the picture's box, and the placement ghost shows the sprite with its footprint ringed.
- Landscape sprites: plants are `sprites/plants/<LAND key>.png` (upright, base at the bottom middle), listed in `PLANT_SPRITES` in data.js (`ratio`, `size`); `plantSpriteSvg` in landscape.js draws one in `featSvg`, flipped by its seed, and plants with no entry (and vivarium plants) keep their SVG. Rocks are `sprites/rocks/<type>-<biome>-<n>.png`, listed in `ROCK_SPRITES` (`[width, height]` per picture, `ROCK_MPP` meters per pixel); `rockSpriteSvg` picks one by the rock's id. Water: `WATER_TEX` in landscape.js lists the biomes whose water is a pixel tile (`sprites/water/<biome>.png`, the `#w-<biome>` pattern in index.html, made by `tools/pixelwater_flat.py`, one palette per biome; wetland, scrubland and temperate so far, each with its four bank colors in `WATER_TEX[biome]`, with a stair-stepped shore cut to the pixel grid by `waterMask`: a light shallows rim inside and a wet-mud (scrubland: salt-crust) bank outside, cached by shape); `waterSvg` takes the biome (the exhibit's, or the park's for water outside) and other biomes keep the flat blue. ## Art direction (locked)
The Dimetrodon (`sprites/dime*.png`, shaded by `tools/pixeldime_shade.py`) is the reference: flat, simple, cartoony pixel art.
- **Shapes and color:** flat fills, no outlines, stair-stepped silhouettes, readable at game size. About 8 base colors a sprite, each with a lighter and a darker shade of the same hue. Palette family: olive greens (97,111,34), rust orange (178,78,0), yellow-olive and warm browns; buildings use their theme's colors.
- **Detail budget:** flat shapes plus about 30%, all structured: a lit top-left edge, shadow on undersides and the right side, rhythmic texture (rows of short dashes, grooves beside ribs, scutes, a lighter rib line). Never random scatter, noise or heavy dithering; it reads as sandpaper.
- **Light** comes from the top left everywhere.
- **Exhibits are full 2D:** flat ground and upright sprites, y-sorted. Animals are side-on facing right (mirrored by `face`). Don't render animals from 3D models; that was tried, cost a lot, and looked worse than simple hand-made shapes.
- **Buildings are 3/4 and front-facing**, extruded from the footprint by `building34`, so they rotate. Flat style: one flat tone per face (left-facing lightest, front the base tone, right-facing darkest), no strokes except for selected, doomed and unreachable states, every point snapped to the pixel grid (`flatSnap34`: `ROCK_MPP` = 0.13 m a pixel, with the vertical step divided by `TILT` so pixels are square after the squash), and pixel-built text and pictograms. Use the game's own names ("Restrooms", never "WC"). Keep the snap's tie-break (the `+ 1e-4`), or exact half-pixel positions round at random and drop columns.
- **Pixel size** is the same everywhere: 0.13 m a pixel.
- **Ground tiles** wrap and repeat, so keep them free of large or distinctive features, and of anything from the wrong era or biome (no grass or sage in Permian scrubland).
- **Check at game zoom and zoomed in.** A blurry screenshot hides bugs; reconstruct the drawn pixels (count them, or print them as ASCII) when something looks off.
- **Status:** in this style so far: the Dimetrodon, the Cotylorhynchus (shading and a new head, own palette and body), all 24 scrubland landscape plants, the scrubland rocks, boulders, ground and water, the temperate rocks and boulders (grey, moss-capped, stained and leaf-litter), the wetland ground, rocks, boulders, all 27 plants and the six wetland Park Plants, the three Quaternary temperate plants and the seven temperate Park Plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate.py`), the temperate Devonian, Carboniferous and Permian plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_old.py`) and Triassic, Jurassic and Cretaceous plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_meso.py`) and Paleogene and Neogene plants (`tools/pixelplants_temperate_ceno.py`, so all 27 temperate plants are done), the Genesis restroom. Still in the older shaded or outlined style: every other plant (the wetland ones and the three Quaternary temperate ones are done, and the temperate Park Plants have sprites), and every other building, fence and theme. New work follows this direction; redoing the old work is by request. To finish the remaining biomes (ground, rocks, plants, Park Plants, water), follow `tools/BIOME_ART.md`.

## Sprite rigger (tools/rigger)
A standalone page for animating sprites from parts, with no build step: `tools/rigger/index.html`. It is NOT part of the game (not in `index.html`, not in the smoke test). Also published as an Artifact, https://claude.ai/artifact/Es5nLZcG73gpnDT71tbLgi (capability `downloads`); the artifact copy is the repo file with the `<!doctype>`/`<html>`/`<head>`/`<body>` wrapper stripped (the repo copy keeps it, so it opens from disk), and it can't use `alert`/`confirm`/`prompt` or `<a download>` (it has `ask()` and the `downloads` capability instead). Republishing it is separate from publishing the game.
- **Model.** Each part is one PNG, a full cell in size with the part drawn where it belongs. A part ("node") has a joint `px,py` (pixel in the image it turns about), a `parent`, and `lx,ly`, its joint's place in the parent's frame (the world for a root). Pose per frame is `{nodeId:{r,dx,dy}}`: `r` degrees (positive turns clockwise on screen), `dx,dy` shift in the parent's frame. World matrix = parent world · translate(lx+dx, ly+dy) · rotate(r); the image is drawn at (-px,-py) (`flip` mirrors it about the joint). The empty pose is the rest pose, the standing picture. List order is draw order (last is in front) and is separate from parenting. `mirror` pairs two parts (near and far leg).
- **Project JSON** (Save/Load project): `{name, cell:{w,h}, ground, seq, assets:{id:dataURL}, nodes:[{id,name,asset,px,py,parent,lx,ly,flip,hidden,mirror}], ref, clips:[{name,fps,frames:[pose]}]}`. One rig, several clips (walk, idle...). Export strip gives `<name>-<clip>.png`, the frames side by side at `cell` size, the game's walk format. Export standing gives the rest pose. "Whole pixels" rounds each part's position to the pixel grid, rotation is nearest-neighbor (no smoothing).
- **Tools.** Rig mode: Move, Joint (click the pivot; the picture and the children stay put), parent, Mirror partner, Duplicate (pairs the copy). Pose mode: drag the orange knob (or the part) to rotate about its joint, Shift snaps 5°, Move shifts, arrows nudge. Part picking: a click within a few screen pixels of a thin part counts, the selected part stays selected if it's under the pointer, Alt+click cycles to the part beneath. Timeline: copy frame, rest frame, reorder, Fill to next / first (tween), Mirror pose, Mirror clip, Half-cycle partner (copies the selected part's motion to its partner half a cycle later), onion skin, play with ping-pong.
- **Driving it headless** (how the Plateosaurus walk was made): load it in Playwright and call page globals: `openProject(json)`, `P`, `frames()`, `worlds(pose)`, `setParent(node, id)`, `setMirror`, `halfCycle`, `drawPose(ctx, pose, 1, snap)`, `stripCanvas(clip())`, `pruned()` (the project JSON), `save(true)`. `tools/rigger/plateosaurus/gen-walk.js` is the model: it sets the frames of a clip from code, with 2-bone IK on each hind leg (stance foot planted and sliding back at body speed, swing foot lifted, far leg half a cycle behind), body bob, and tail, neck and arm counter-motion, then writes the strip, a contact sheet and the project. Its node ids, ground height and gait numbers (`N`, `ds`, `S`, `af`, `lift`, `B`) are for the Plateosaurus, and its paths are for the session it was written in, so adapt them. Check a walk by looking at zoomed frames, not the numbers alone.
- **Workflow for an animal.** Cut it into flat base-color parts (no shading or texture), rig and animate, export the strip and the standing picture, then run the shading and detail pass on the strip (like the `tools/pixel*_shade.py` scripts). Shading baked into parts would rotate with them, turning the top-left light and breaking rows of dashes. Then add the `SPRITES` entry in data.js (`ratio:100/75, walk:N, step:`about `1/N`, and a standing `sprites/<id>.png` whose feet are on the same row as the walk frames) and copy the PNGs to `sprites/`. 4 to 8 frames suits a walk; the game only moves the viewport per frame, so the count costs nothing in speed.
- **Known limits.** Rotated pixel art gets uneven edges, and big angles break texture and leave wedge gaps at joints (hide them with a small joint-cap part). Not built yet: IK in the tool (only in scripts), RotSprite-style pixel-clean rotation, replacing a part's image while keeping the rig, a darker tint for far-side limbs.

## Where the code lives (read this before every change)
- **`main` is the source of truth.** Start from it, and get changes merged into it before or right after publishing.
- Publish only from a branch that has `origin/main` merged in. If you publish from a stale branch, you overwrite other people's fixes in the live game.
- Never copy the live artifact's files back into git wholesale. If the live game seems to have something git doesn't, diff each file against `main` and port only the missing hunks. A wholesale copy once undid a fix (the animal Move dialog).
- A file's helpers aren't always where you'd guess (see the notes above). Search for `function name` or `const name =` across `js/` before adding one.
